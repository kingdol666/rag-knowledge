\section{Conclusion}
\sys{} is a deployable knowledge-base management platform that organizes documents
by content and verifies every retrieval by reading it. Each query runs one protocol
--- vector-first recall, an interpretable $0$--$8$ content gate, a librarian
fallback, and an honest not-found contract --- and leaves an auditable trace from
candidates to verdict. At the booth: bring a PDF, watch it classified, ask a
question, inspect the score and the citation, and see a genuine not-found.

The three tracks in Figure~\ref{fig:threeway} agree from different directions: the
dense baseline was fastest and the only one that failed to answer, the bare agent
answered best without being able to say where its answer came from, and the protocol
answered with a score and a citation --- or said so in a report when it could not.
What the faster systems lacked was a component whose job is to check the evidence; as
corpora outgrow hand inspection, that component becomes the only thing between a
knowledge base and fluent fabrication. The extensions follow the same contract:
broaden the gate from one judge to an ensemble with per-dimension agreement,
calibrate the pass threshold from the recorded traces, and let not-found reports
accumulate into a map of what the corpus does not cover.

Code and run artifacts: \url{https://github.com/kingdol666/rag-knowledge}; demo
video: \url{https://github.com/kingdol666/rag-knowledge/blob/master/paper_demo/video/qdcvr-demo.mp4}.

\section*{Data Availability}
Every number here traces to a JSON artifact in the repository's
\texttt{benchmark-suite} results directory: the parse survey and ingestion report,
the classification decisions with their rationales, the 10-question regression,
per-question gate scores and five-section answers, the three answering tracks, the
three out-of-corpus probes, and the timing logs. The corpus is fetched by a
reproducible script from arXiv identifiers in a manifest.
