\section{Demonstration}
\label{sec:demo}

\paragraph{Demo setup.}
The live instance holds 5 category bases populated with 50 real papers --- 3.8\,M
characters that index to 165 documents (9 whole papers and 156 parts) --- plus three
reproduction indices used for comparison. Attendees interact through the web console
or through any MCP-capable agent harness driving the same 41
\texttt{kb\_*} tools; both surfaces execute the same protocol. Attendees may bring
their own PDFs and watch them parsed, classified, and tagged on the spot; the
published numbers come from the fixed 50-paper corpus. All three scenarios run
against this one instance with no state reset between them. A demonstration video is
available at \url{https://github.com/kingdol666/rag-knowledge/blob/master/paper_demo/video/qdcvr-demo.mp4}.

\paragraph{Scenario 1: from upload to answerable.}
The attendee drops a PDF into the console upload panel. \sys{} parses it into
structured Markdown, splits the long document into parts that each carry their
section-path header, reads the text, and files it into the recommended category base
with tags propagated to every part. No dialog asks the attendee to pick a folder,
define a schema, or author a routing rule. The console lists five content-classified bases of 72, 43, 32, 12 and 6 indexed
documents beside three reproduction indices built by chunking rather than by
reading; a second panel shows the part and tag model. Point proven: the administrator writes no
rules, and re-filing is a logged operation rather than a silent move.

\paragraph{Scenario 2: gate-adjudicated answering.}
The attendee asks, ``What defines NISQ technology and what is its central
limitation?'' The live console shows Phase~1 fusing BM25 with
vector similarity across all five bases, the NISQ paper ranks first at $0.62$ fused
similarity, and the hit card names the owning base, the document and the part
(\texttt{part 1 of 3}) so the attendee can open the exact fragment. The gate reads
that part, finds the defining sentence plus the no-error-correction limitation, and
scores it 7/8 --- a fast exit that ends the search. The five-section answer's
citation names base, document, part and section path. Point proven: the answer
carries a readable score and a resolvable citation, not a bare similarity value.

\paragraph{Scenario 3: the gate discriminates, and failure is honest.}
(a)~\emph{Rescue.} For the tumor-therapy question the initial top chunk is the
paper's references page; the gate scores it 5/8, the librarian fallback re-searches
and locates the discussion section at 6/8, and the rescue appears in the trace as
named steps rather than a silent retry. Two of the ten questions take this path.
(b)~\emph{Honest not-found.} The attendee asks about a paper that does not exist:
``What did `Spectral Tuning for Low-Resource Odor Recognition' report about training
epochs?'' The trap is real --- the top candidate is the BERT paper, which genuinely
contains training-epoch strings, and a similarity-ranked pipeline would quote them.
The gate scores it 1/8 and the shelf scan confirms the paper is absent, so the system
returns a not-found report naming its search scope, the near miss, why that near miss
does not answer the question, and what to do next. Three such probes ran; all scored
1/8, 1/8 and 0/8 and all returned not-found. A logs audit found no previously
captured not-found instance, so the probes are author-designed and attendees are
invited to pose their own. Point proven: ``I don't know'' is a protocol-level,
per-query recorded output.

\paragraph{Performance highlights.}
\label{sec:perf}
Figure~\ref{fig:threeway} reports the three tracks on the same corpus and
questions, with the 10-question aggregates beneath each column. Track~A places the gold document first in all 10 questions
at 1.57\,s mean vector recall plus 0.81\,s of candidate reads; its final gate scores
are 6--8/8, with two questions rescued by the librarian after initial scores of 5/8,
so the fallback is exercised rather than decorative. A keyword check of its
five-section answers finds at least one gold keyword in 10/10 and every gold keyword
in 9/10; the single miss is a chunk-window artifact in the scripted regression
harness, and the continuation clause resolves the same question on the same corpus.

The other tracks are strong on different axes, and we report that plainly. Track~C is
the fastest at 12.2\,s end-to-end (0.59\,s of it retrieval): it answered 8 of 10 and
abstained honestly on 2, where 800-character fixed chunking had split the answer
sentence away from the retrieved window --- but its chunks carry no document path, so
its answers cannot be traced to a source at all. Track~B reads whole files and
produces the most detailed answers at 78.8\,s mean (35.5--165.3\,s), the widest
run-to-run spread, and a provenance that stops at the file name. The contract column,
not the keyword column, separates them: Track~A's residual misses surface as
not-found reports, Track~C's as abstentions or fluent prose, and Track~B's as
unlogged runs.

\paragraph{Generalizability.}
Adapting \sys{} to a new domain means ingesting a new document set and naming the
category bases; lifecycle, protocol and audit artifacts are unchanged, because
nothing in the pipeline is keyed to a domain vocabulary. All quality numbers are
spot-check scale ($n{=}10$) with per-question traces published as repository
artifacts, and we claim nothing beyond that scale.
