\section{Demonstration}
\label{sec:demo}

\paragraph{Demo setup.}
The live instance holds 5 category bases populated with 50 real papers --- 3.8\,M
characters that index to 165 documents (9 whole papers and 156 parts) --- plus three
reproduction indices used for comparison. Attendees interact through the web console
(Figure~\ref{fig:ui}) or through any MCP-capable agent harness driving the same 41
\texttt{kb\_*} tools; both surfaces execute the same protocol. Attendees may bring
their own PDFs and watch them parsed, classified, and tagged on the spot; the
published numbers come from the fixed 50-paper corpus. All three scenarios run
against this one instance with no state reset between them. A demonstration video is
available at \url{https://github.com/kingdol666/rag-knowledge/blob/master/paper_demo/video/qdcvr-demo.mp4}.

\paragraph{Scenario 1: from upload to answerable.}
The attendee drops a PDF into the console upload panel. \sys{} parses it into
structured Markdown, splits the long document into parts that each carry their
section-path header, reads the text, and files it into the recommended category base
with tags propagated to every part. No dialog asks the attendee to pick a folder,
define a schema, or author a routing rule. Figure~\ref{fig:ui}(1) shows the resulting
shelves --- five content-classified bases sized 72, 43, 32, 12 and 6 indexed
documents --- alongside three reproduction indices built by chunking rather than by
reading; panel~(3) shows what the split produces, part cards named \texttt{(part k of
N)} with their inherited tags attached. Point proven: the administrator builds no
folder tree and writes no rules, and re-filing is a logged operation rather than a
silent move.

\paragraph{Scenario 2: gate-adjudicated answering.}
The attendee asks, ``What defines NISQ technology and what is its central
limitation?'' Figure~\ref{fig:ui}(2) is the live result. Phase~1 recalls candidates
from all five bases and fuses BM25 with vector similarity; the NISQ paper ranks first
at $0.62$ fused similarity, and the hit card names the owning base (\emph{natural and
Earth sciences}), the document, and the part --- \texttt{part 1 of 3} --- so the
attendee can open the exact fragment. The content gate then reads that part, finds the
defining sentence (``NISQ stands for Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum'') plus the
no-error-correction limitation, and scores it 7/8, a fast exit that ends the search.
The five-section answer's citation names base, document, part, and section path. Point
proven: the answer carries a readable score and a resolvable citation, not a bare
similarity value.

\paragraph{Scenario 3: the gate discriminates, and failure is honest.}
Two crafted queries stress the protocol in opposite directions. (a)~\emph{Rescue.} For
the tumor-therapy question the initial top chunk is the paper's references page; the
gate reads it and scores 5/8. The librarian fallback rescans the shelves, re-searches,
and locates the discussion section, which scores 6/8 --- the rescue visible in the
trace as named steps rather than a silent retry. Two of the ten benchmark questions
take this path. (b)~\emph{Honest not-found.} The attendee asks about work that does
not exist in the corpus: ``What did the paper `Spectral Tuning for Low-Resource Odor
Recognition' report about training epochs?'' The trap is real --- the top candidate is
the BERT paper, which genuinely contains training-epoch strings, and a
similarity-ranked pipeline would quote them. The gate reads the text, scores it 1/8,
and the librarian shelf scan confirms no such paper is present. The system returns a
not-found report naming the search scope it covered, the near-miss it found, why the
near-miss does not answer the question, and what to do next --- quoting the near-miss
rather than citing it. Three such probes ran (a fabricated benchmark, a fabricated
paper title, and an out-of-domain veterinary question); all three scored 1/8, 1/8 and
0/8 and all three returned not-found reports. A logs audit found no previously
captured not-found instance, so the probes are author-designed; at the booth,
attendees are invited to pose their own. Point proven: ``I don't know'' is a
protocol-level, per-query recorded output.

\paragraph{Performance highlights.}
\label{sec:perf}
Table~\ref{tab:tracks} compares three answering tracks on the same corpus and
questions. Track~A places the gold document first in all 10 questions at 1.57\,s mean
vector recall plus 0.81\,s of candidate reads, and every question ends either in a
gate-passed answer or in an explicit not-found report. Its final gate scores are
6--8/8, with two questions rescued by the librarian after initial scores of 5/8 ---
the fallback is exercised, not decorative. A keyword check of its five-section
answers finds at least one gold keyword in 10/10 and every gold keyword in 9/10; the
single miss is a chunk-window artifact in the scripted regression harness, and the
continuation clause resolves the same question on the same corpus.

The other tracks are strong on different axes, and we report that plainly. Track~C,
the dense baseline, is by far the fastest at 12.2\,s end-to-end, of which retrieval is
0.59\,s; it answered 8 of 10 questions and abstained honestly on 2, where
800-character fixed chunking had split the answer sentence away from the retrieved
window. But its retrieved chunks carry no document path, so its answers cannot be
traced to a source at all --- a real recording defect of the reproduction harness.
Track~B, the bare agent, reads whole files and produces the most detailed answers, at
78.8\,s mean (35.5--165.3\,s) and with the largest run-to-run spread; its provenance
is a file list with no paragraph-level citation. The contract column, not the keyword
column, is what separates the tracks: Track~A's residual misses surface as not-found
reports, Track~C's as abstentions or fluent prose, and Track~B's as unlogged runs.

\paragraph{Generalizability.}
Adapting \sys{} to a new domain means ingesting a new document set and naming the
category bases; lifecycle, protocol, and audit artifacts are unchanged, because
nothing in the pipeline is keyed to a domain vocabulary. All quality numbers here are
spot-check scale ($n{=}10$ questions) with per-question traces published as
repository artifacts, and we claim nothing beyond that scale.
