\begin{abstract}
Research administrators must organize shared paper collections and inspect
the evidence behind the answers drawn from them. We present \sys{}, an
agent-operated platform coupling content-guided knowledge organization
with librarian-style retrieval through one shared address space. Packaged
agent skills parse documents, route them by content into category bases,
and maintain tags. Retrieval rewrites each query, recalls and reads
candidates, gates them with a prompted 0--8 rubric (topic, scenario,
evidence), and --- when nothing passes --- escalates to browsing base
summaries and tag-matched shelves, returning a scoped not-found report
instead of answering from the nearest neighbor. The platform's own ingest
loop built a 100-paper, five-base instance; ingest, storage, and retrieval
probes then audited it. In a monitored three-mode run under one harness and
model, the platform grounds 9 of 10 designated-paper answers and
keyword-verifies 9 of 10 (gold key facts matched verbatim); the bare agent
even locates the designated paper in all 10 cases yet passes conservative
word-boundary verification in only 6, and dense retrieval grounds 9 and
verifies 6. Part/section-level citations reach 7 of 10 versus 4 (bare
agent) and 1 (dense). A measured dense-mode abstention and three scripted
out-of-corpus probes complete the demonstration. The platform trades
roughly three times the bare agent's cost per question for this
inspectable, addressable evidence. Attendees organize a document, inspect an answer's evidence, and
examine a failure report. Demo video:
\url{https://github.com/kingdol666/rag-knowledge/blob/master/paper_demo/video/qdcvr-demo.mp4}.
\end{abstract}
