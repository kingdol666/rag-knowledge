\begin{abstract}
Research administrators who maintain shared paper collections must organize
documents and inspect the evidence behind the answers drawn from them. We
present \sys{}, an agent-operated platform that couples content-guided
knowledge organization with librarian-style retrieval through one shared
address space. Packaged agent skills call tool APIs to parse documents,
route them by content into category bases, and maintain tags; retrieval
rewrites each query, recalls and reads candidates, gates them with a
prompted 0--8 rubric (topic, scenario, evidence), and --- when nothing
passes --- escalates from ranking to browsing base summaries and tag-matched
shelves, returning a scoped not-found report instead of answering from the
nearest neighbor. A
100-paper, five-base demonstration instance was built by the platform's own
ingest loop and audited across ingest, storage, and retrieval probes. In a
monitored three-mode run under one harness and model (platform, bare agent,
dense retrieval), the platform grounds 9 of 10 designated-paper answers and
keyword-verifies 9 of 10; the bare agent locates the designated paper in
all 10 cases yet verifies only 6, and dense retrieval grounds 9 and
verifies 6. Part/section-level citations reach 7 of 10 for the platform
versus 4 and 1, whose citations name files and chunks rather than the full
base/document/part/section address. One measured dense-mode abstention is
shown alongside the platform's verified answer, and three scripted
out-of-corpus probes receive scoped not-found reports. The platform trades
roughly three times the baseline cost for this verified, addressable
evidence. Attendees organize a document, inspect an answer's evidence, and
examine a failure report. Demo video:
\url{https://github.com/kingdol666/rag-knowledge/blob/master/paper_demo/video/qdcvr-demo.mp4}.
\end{abstract}
