QDCVR reads section-addressed evidence and applies a zero-to-eight gate. A score of at least six leads to a cited answer; otherwise a librarian search and re-reading lead to a strong-evidence answer, a qualified score-five backstop answer, or a scoped not-found report when no usable evidence remains.}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../figures/submission-20260919/fig2-architecture.pdf}
\caption{Platform operations and the agent-executed QDCVR workflow. (a) Parsing, content-guided placement, and part-level checks prepare indexed evidence. (b) The packaged skill prescribes query rewriting, recall, reading, and a conditional content assessment. Scores of at least six permit a direct answer; lower initial scores invoke the librarian. After reassessment, usable score-five evidence can support an attributed backstop answer; no usable evidence yields not-found. These instructions are not mandatory behavior of ordinary HTTP search endpoints.}
\Description{Two areas distinguish offline preparation from the online agent skill. The first parses PDFs into Markdown, organizes and checks parts, and connects to vector, graph, and file-based storage. The second recalls and reads candidates, then branches on a content gate. Passing evidence yields a cited answer. Initial failure triggers targeted librarian search and re-reading; after that search, a usable score-five backstop supports an attributed answer, while no usable evidence yields a report with scope, near miss, and reason.}
\label{fig:arch}
\end{figure*}
