\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../figures/submission-20260922/fig1-core.pdf}
\caption{QDCVR at a glance. Left: a conventional dense-RAG configuration ---
fixed chunks, retrieval of two excerpts, a packed context, single
generation; sources are not addressable. Right: the two QDCVR agent loops ---
autonomous ingest (parse, route by content, tag, index parts with
base/document/part/section addresses) and librarian-style retrieval
(rewrite, cross-base recall, read, 0--8 content gate, then cited answer,
qualified answer, or scoped not-found). The left lane is shown for contrast;
the evaluated dense variant packs retrieved chunks into one prompt.}
\Description{Two lanes. The left lane shows a conventional dense-RAG
pipeline: fixed 800-character chunks, dense retrieval of the top two
excerpts, a packed context of at most 4,000 characters, and a single
generation step; the reader gets an answer without citations, sources it
cannot address, and retrieval errors that pass through silently; provenance
is not logged. The right lane shows the two QDCVR agent loops. Loop one
manages the knowledge base autonomously: PDFs are parsed, routed to a
category base by content, tagged, and indexed as parts that carry base,
document, part, and section addresses. Loop two retrieves librarian-style:
the query is rewritten, candidates are recalled across bases and read, a 0-8
content rubric gates them; a score of at least six yields a cited answer
with section-addressed sources, lower scores send the librarian to browse
base summaries and tag-matched shelves for new candidates, ending in a
qualified answer with attribution or a scoped not-found report. A takeaway
strip states that the same base, document, part, and section identifiers
power both loops, so every answer can be checked at its source.}
\label{fig:teaser}
\end{figure*}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../figures/submission-20260922/fig2-architecture.pdf}
\caption{Platform operations and the agent-executed QDCVR workflow. (a) Parsing, content-guided placement, and part-level checks prepare indexed evidence. (b) The packaged skill prescribes query rewriting, recall, reading, and a conditional content assessment. Scores of at least six permit a direct answer; lower initial scores invoke the librarian. After reassessment, usable score-five evidence can support an attributed backstop answer; no usable evidence yields not-found.}
\Description{Two areas distinguish offline preparation from the online agent skill. The first parses PDFs into Markdown, organizes and checks parts, and connects to vector, graph, and file-based storage. The second recalls and reads candidates, then branches on a content gate. Passing evidence yields a cited answer. Initial failure triggers targeted librarian search and re-reading; after that search, a usable score-five backstop supports an attributed answer, while no usable evidence yields a report with scope, near miss, reason, and remedy.}
\label{fig:arch}
\end{figure*}
