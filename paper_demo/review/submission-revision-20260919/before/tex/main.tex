\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.70\textwidth]{../figures/fig1_protocols.png}\caption{Two retrieval stacks answering the same question. A conventional dense
RAG stack (left) splits documents into fixed chunks, ranks fragments, and stuffs
the top hits into one LLM call, so its answer carries no section provenance and
a missed passage is never retried. The \sys{} protocol (right) routes the query
by content, recalls vector-first across the category bases, has a 0--8 gate read
the candidate text, and falls back to a librarian before answering --- emitting
either a five-section answer citing base/doc/part/\S with declared blind spots,
or an honest not-found report.}
\Description{Two pastel panels fed by the same question card ("What defines
NISQ technology and what is its central limitation?"). Left, a grey panel
"Conventional dense RAG": three steps (fixed 800-character chunking, single
dense retrieval pass, 4,000-character evidence pack to the LLM) ending in an
answer card with empty provenance fields (source, section, evidence check) and
three failure chips (answer split across chunks, no source paths, no retry).
Right, a teal panel "QDCVR protocol - this system": three steps (content-routed
category KBs, vector-first recall, a 0-8 content gate reading the text) ending
in an answer card quoting the NISQ definition with a citation chip
(quantum-physics__1801.00862, part 2/3, section 6.4), a gate 7/8 fast-exit
chip, a declared blind-spot line, and a dashed not-found report card. A
verdict strip contrasts "an answer you cannot trace" with "an answer you can
verify - or an honest not-found".}
\label{fig:teaser}
\end{figure*}

\caption{One benchmark question answered by three retrieval stacks. The \sys{}
protocol (left) answers with section-level citations and declares its unread
blind spots; a bare agent (middle) produces the most complete prose, but its
provenance stops at a self-reported file name; a dense RAG baseline (right)
abstains because fixed 800-character chunking left the answer out of its
evidence window. Below, an out-of-corpus trap question ends in a not-found
report that names its near miss instead of fabricating.}
\Description{A question card at the top asks how the Transformer computes
attention and why it replaces recurrence and convolution. Three answer panels
follow. Left, the QDCVR protocol answers with section citations (parts 1/2 and
2/2, sections 3.2.3 and 7), a gate score of 7/8, and a declared blind spot for
the unread attention formula. Middle, a bare agent prints the full scaled
dot-product equation with only a self-reported file name as provenance. Right,
a dense RAG baseline abstains, saying the provided evidence only discusses
constituency parsing, because the method section was chunked away. A bottom row
poses a trap question about a non-existent odor-recognition paper; the QDCVR
not-found report names the BERT near miss (gate 1/8), the shelf scan over 5
bases and 165 documents, and a quote refusing to fabricate.}
\label{fig:threeway}
\end{figure*}
