## Q1 — Early scene (Ch III; part 2)
**Question:** At the Meryton assembly where the Netherfield party first appears, what does Mr. Darcy say about Elizabeth when Bingley suggests he dance with her, and how does Elizabeth respond to it?

**Required facts (all must be present for "correct"):**
1. The scene is the Meryton assembly/ball (first appearance of Bingley's party; Darcy reported to have £10,000 a year and a large estate in Derbyshire).
2. Darcy danced only once with Mrs. Hurst and once with Miss Bingley and **refused to be introduced to any other lady**.
3. Bingley urges him to dance and points out Elizabeth (sitting down for two dances just behind him).
4. Darcy's actual words: **"She is tolerable: but not handsome enough to tempt me"** (+ "in no humour ... to give consequence to young ladies who are slighted by other men").
5. **Elizabeth overhears it** (she was within earshot).
6. Her reaction: **no very cordial feelings** toward him, but she **tells the story with great spirit among her friends** (lively/playful disposition that delighted in anything ridiculous) — i.e. she laughs it off rather than being crushed.

**Missing/partial markers:** saying she was "insulted/cried" without the "told it with spirit" nuance = partial; attributing the line to Bingley = wrong.

## Q2 — Mid-book turning point (Ch XXXIV–XXXV; parts 13–14)
**Question:** What happens at Hunsford when Mr. Darcy proposes to Elizabeth, why does she refuse him, and what does his letter the next morning reveal about Wickham?

**Required facts:**
1. Darcy proposes at **Hunsford Parsonage** (where Elizabeth is staying with the Collinses), unexpectedly, with the opening **"In vain have I struggled. It will not do. My feelings will not be repressed."**
2. His proposal dwells on her **inferiority / the degradation / family obstacles** — offending her.
3. Elizabeth **refuses**, giving **two grounds**: (a) he ruined her sister Jane's happiness by separating Bingley from Jane; (b) his treatment of **Wickham**.
4. Darcy admits (in the argument) that he **did everything to separate Bingley from Jane and rejoices in his success**.
5. Next morning he hands her a **letter** (Ch XXXV) which reveals:
   - Re Jane: he believed Jane was indifferent; he also objected to the **imprudence/impropriety of the Bennet family**.
   - Re Wickham: Wickham was his father's godson; given a **£1,000 legacy** and intended for the church; Wickham **resigned the church living for £3,000**; later, at **Ramsgate**, Wickham (aided by **Mrs. Younge**) tried to elope with Darcy's sister **Georgiana, then only 15**, to get her **£30,000 fortune** — the elopement was prevented when Darcy arrived unexpectedly.

**Missing/partial markers:** omitting the £3,000 or the Ramsgate/Georgiana reveal = partial; getting the two grounds for refusal right but not the letter contents = partial.

## Q3 — Late-book reveal (Ch LII; part 21)
**Question:** How does Elizabeth learn the truth about Mr. Darcy's role in Lydia's marriage, and what exactly did Darcy do and pay?

**Required facts:**
1. Elizabeth learns it from **Mrs. Gardiner's letter** (from Gracechurch Street, dated Sept 6) answering Elizabeth's inquiry.
2. **Mr. Darcy (not Mr. Gardiner)** secretly did it: he **left Derbyshire the day after the Gardiners**, came to London to hunt for Lydia and Wickham.
3. He used **Mrs. Younge** (former governess of Georgiana) to find them; saw Wickham repeatedly and Lydia once.
4. He persuaded/bribed Wickham to marry Lydia; found Lydia **resolved to stay with Wickham**.
5. The financial settlement: **Wickham's debts paid (>£1,000), a further £1,000 settled on Lydia, and Wickham's commission purchased**.
6. Darcy insisted the credit go to **Mr. Gardiner** (Mr. Gardiner was "forced to put up with only having the probable credit of it").
7. Mrs. Gardiner's letter also conveys that Darcy attended the wedding and implies **Darcy's continued partiality for Elizabeth** ("another interest in the affair").

**Missing/partial markers:** saying Mr. Gardiner paid = wrong; omitting the money amounts = partial.

## Q4 — Character-relationship arc (Ch XXII; part 9)
**Question:** How did Charlotte Lucas come to marry Mr. Collins, and how did Elizabeth react to the engagement?

**Required facts:**
1. Mr. Collins had just proposed to **Elizabeth and been refused** (Ch XIX); within about **three days** he transfers his addresses.
2. **Charlotte Lucas deliberately encouraged him** — her "kindness extended farther than Elizabeth had any conception of: its object was ... to secure her from any return of Mr. Collins's addresses, by engaging them towards herself."
3. Charlotte accepts **"solely from the pure and disinterested desire of an establishment"**; she is **27 years old** and "without having ever been handsome."
4. Her stated reasoning: **"I am not romantic, you know; I never was. I ask only a comfortable home"** — marriage as "the only honourable provision for well-educated young women of small fortune."
5. Elizabeth's reaction: **astonishment / disbelief** — **"Engaged to Mr. Collins! my dear Charlotte, impossible!"** — then a pang of disillusionment: she thinks Charlotte **"disgracing herself, and sunk in her esteem,"** that she **"sacrificed every better feeling to worldly advantage,"** and that it was **impossible for Charlotte to be tolerably happy**; Elizabeth took a long time to become reconciled.

**Missing/partial markers:** getting the engagement but not Charlotte's "establishment"/27/not-romantic reasoning = partial.

## Q5 — Confusable detail: two Wickham elopements (Ch XXXV & XLIX; parts 14 & 20)
