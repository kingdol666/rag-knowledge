# History of the Internet

The Internet grew out of ARPANET, a research project of the US Advanced Research Projects Agency that sent its first message between two computers on 29 October 1969. The TCP/IP protocol suite, designed by Vinton Cerf and Robert Kahn, was adopted by ARPANET on 1 January 1983, an event often called "flag day".

The Domain Name System was created in 1983 by Paul Mockapetris, replacing a single hosts file. The World Wide Web, invented by Tim Berners-Lee at CERN in 1989 and released to the public in 1991, made the Internet usable by non-specialists; the first web browser was called WorldWideWeb.

Commercial restrictions were lifted in 1995, and the browser wars between Netscape Navigator and Internet Explorer followed. Google Search launched in 1998, Wikipedia in 2001, and the iPhone, which brought broadband internet to the pocket, in 2007.

By 2023 about 5.4 billion people, roughly two thirds of humanity, used the Internet. The underlying backbone moves traffic over optical fibres at speeds beyond 100 terabits per second per fibre pair on the busiest submarine cables.
