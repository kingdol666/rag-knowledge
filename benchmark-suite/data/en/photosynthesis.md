# Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. The overall reaction consumes six molecules of carbon dioxide and six of water to produce one glucose molecule and six oxygen molecules.

The process has two stages. In the light-dependent reactions, chlorophyll in the thylakoid membranes absorbs photons and splits water, releasing oxygen and generating ATP and NADPH. In the Calvin cycle, the enzyme RuBisCO fixes carbon dioxide; RuBisCO is probably the most abundant protein on Earth.

About 3 percent of the light reaching a typical plant is converted into biomass under field conditions, and global photosynthesis fixes roughly 120 gigatonnes of carbon per year. C4 plants such as maize and sugarcane pre-concentrate carbon dioxide and achieve higher efficiency in hot climates.

Artificial photosynthesis seeks to replicate this chemistry: in 2022 researchers reported a solar-to-fuel efficiency of about 10 percent for a device converting water and carbon dioxide into ethylene, far above the roughly 1 percent of natural photosynthesis.
