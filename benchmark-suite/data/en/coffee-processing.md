# Coffee Processing

Coffee cherries must be processed within hours of picking to prevent spoilage. Three traditional methods dominate: the washed (wet) process, the natural (dry) process, and the honey process.

In the washed process, the cherry skin and pulp are removed mechanically in a depulper, and the beans, still covered by mucilage, ferment in water for 12 to 72 hours before washing and drying. This method highlights acidity and floral notes and is standard in Colombia and most of Central America.

The natural process dries the whole cherry in the sun for two to four weeks, letting sugars migrate into the bean; it produces fruity, wine-like flavours typical of Ethiopian and Brazilian coffees but risks off-flavours if drying is uneven. The honey process removes the skin but leaves some mucilage, sitting between the two; yellow, red and black honey grades refer to how much mucilage remains.

After drying to about 11 percent moisture, beans are hulled, graded by size and density, and rested. Green coffee is then shipped and roasted; a medium roast is reached at roughly 205 to 215 degrees Celsius, shortly after the first crack.
