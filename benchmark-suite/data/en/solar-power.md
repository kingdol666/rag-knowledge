# Solar Power

Photovoltaic (PV) solar panels convert sunlight directly into electricity using semiconductor materials, most commonly crystalline silicon. The first practical silicon solar cell was demonstrated at Bell Labs in 1954, with an efficiency of about 6 percent. Modern commercial panels reach 20 to 23 percent efficiency, while laboratory perovskite-silicon tandem cells have exceeded 33 percent.

The cost of solar electricity has fallen dramatically. Utility-scale solar photovoltaic projects cost around 0.048 US dollars per kilowatt-hour on average in 2021, down more than 80 percent from 2010, making solar the cheapest source of new electricity in most countries.

China leads global manufacturing, producing roughly 80 percent of the world's solar panels as of 2023. Global installed solar capacity passed 1 terawatt in 2022 and is expected to exceed 5 terawatts by 2030 according to industry forecasts.

Concentrated solar power (CSP) is a complementary technology: mirrors focus sunlight to heat a working fluid, driving a turbine. The Ivanpah plant in California, completed in 2014, uses 347,000 mirrors and can power about 140,000 homes. Unlike PV, CSP with molten-salt thermal storage can deliver electricity after sunset.
