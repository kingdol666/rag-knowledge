# Electric Vehicles

An electric vehicle (EV) is propelled by one or more electric motors using energy stored in rechargeable battery packs. Modern mass-market EVs use lithium-ion batteries with energy densities around 250 watt-hours per kilogram.

The modern EV era is often dated to the launch of the Tesla Roadster in 2008, the first highway-legal production EV to use lithium-ion battery cells and to travel more than 320 kilometres per charge. By 2023, global electric car sales exceeded 14 million units, roughly 18 percent of all new car sales, with China accounting for about 60 percent of the market.

Charging standards differ by region: CCS is common in Europe and North America, while GB/T dominates in China and CHAdeMO persists in Japan. DC fast chargers can add 200 kilometres of range in about 15 minutes on vehicles with 800-volt architectures, such as the Hyundai Ioniq 5 and the Porsche Taycan.

Battery costs fell from about 1,100 US dollars per kilowatt-hour in 2010 to under 140 dollars in 2023. Solid-state batteries, which replace the liquid electrolyte with a solid one, promise higher energy density and lower fire risk; Toyota plans limited production around 2027.
