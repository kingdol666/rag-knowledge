**The Gene Ontology consists of three non-overlapping sub-ontologies: Molecular Function (MF), Biological Process (BP), and Cellular Component (CC).**

Original text (P0 direct evidence):

> "The terms are subdivided in three distinct ontologies that represent different biological aspects: **Molecular Function (MF), Biological Process (BP), and Cellular Component (CC)** (1). These ontologies are **non-redundant** and share a common space of identifiers and a well-specified syntax."
> -- *Primer on the Gene Ontology*, S2 "What is the Gene Ontology?"

Additional corroboration (same reference, S5.2, describing column 9 of the GAF format):

> "field 9 denotes the **sub-ontology** of GO, either Molecular Function, Biological Process, or Cellular Component."

That is: GO annotation files dedicate a column that explicitly marks which sub-ontology each annotation belongs to, making it easy to parse annotations by GO aspect.

One comparable piece of structural data (same section, Oct 2015 release): the whole ontology holds 43,835 terms, 73,776 `is_a` relations, 7,436 `part_of` relations and 8,263 `regulates`-type relations -- showing that the three sub-ontologies share one identifier space and syntax, divided by "biological aspect" rather than by identifier scheme.
