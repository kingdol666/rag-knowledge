\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\noindent\textbf{Data and scope.} The collections represent local
demonstration and benchmark workloads. Developers authored or expanded the
questions and gold annotations. The routing audit uses a local in-domain
question set, not an independent test of transfer to new domains.
Same-question reruns show whether the paired direction persists as the
corpus and service state change. Transfer requires an independent question
set. Storage reconciliation was sampled, and the out-of-domain probes
characterise individual responses rather than a population failure rate.

\noindent\textbf{Measurement and comparisons.} The content metric counts a
literal target string anywhere in the pack, including in a sibling
document. A post-hoc split by case-insensitive question--target overlap
gives frozen A0 and A0h containment of
16/29 and 21/29 on overlapping questions, and 21/43 and 31/43 on the
remainder. The subsets describe exact overlap and leave other lexical
cues unmeasured. E7 checks identifiers,
keywords and citation markers with one model and one run per arm.
Evaluating claim support calls for resolving the cited passages and judging
their relation to the claims.

\noindent\textbf{Configuration contrasts.} The read-all comparator scans the
corpus mechanically, and the content arms vary candidate construction,
reading scope and pack assembly. A0, A1b and A3 can use the gold domain for
reference resolution; A3 can also fall back to descriptions or retrieve
outside the evaluated collections. The archives do not record how often
these paths occur, so the E4 contrasts quantify configuration differences
rather than isolated component effects.

\noindent\textbf{Answer-quality protocol.} E2 reuses the ten questions from
the E7 development grids and post-repair validation. The agent made no
catalogue or shelf calls, so E2 did not test catalogue-first selection. The HMRS agent
was configured for the five collections, but the search tools take one
collection identifier. In nine of ten questions, at least one search ran
without that restriction, so the HMRS row searched a wider space than the
one-shot baselines. The archived tool-name records
contain no returned passages or source coordinates. A flat agent with
equivalent tools and reading budget would
help separate the effect of the interface from the effect of agent access.
In each call, the unified judge saw the question, a prefix of the gold
reference and eight anonymised answers. It could not resolve baseline
\texttt{[S\#]} markers to passages; arm names were masked, but language,
length and citation style remained visible. Earlier passes produced lower
baseline scores and individual reversals (for example, dense scored 5.00
against HMRS's 3.75 on BQ07 in the one-answer-per-call pass). Judge--human
and judge--judge agreement remain unmeasured.

\noindent\textbf{External-system comparison.} The external arms of E2 used
one unified blind judge over 80 answers, but answer-time corpus scope was
not uniform. RAPTOR and GraphRAG used 30 gold parts, LightRAG used a
different 699-chunk representation of the full export. DeepRead's native
answer-time index included the gold papers but not the full set of
distractors; the full parse completed after the answer round. This smaller
search space may favour DeepRead. Its answer record also carries conflicting
generator metadata between its top-level field and its native provenance
note. The E2 scores therefore describe the observed
configurations and should not be interpreted as a matched-corpus
leaderboard.

\noindent\textbf{Statistical and operational validity.} The paired
comparisons are exploratory. Wilson intervals are nominal binomial intervals for observed hit
proportions; they do not capture variation across runs. Section~\ref{sec:e10} summarises the E2
paired-test outcomes; Supplementary Tables~S18--S22 report the tests and
their sensitivity to the judge pass and the post-hoc comparison family. Candidate- and span-level tests can
understate uncertainty when they treat observations from the same query as
independent. The ordering analysis replays records from a historical
wrapper. Some earlier reference-mode and judge inputs and code hashes are
missing. Lifecycle and concurrency checks cover one installation.
Competing lifecycle writes, experience approval, multi-host reliability
and crash recovery remain open.
