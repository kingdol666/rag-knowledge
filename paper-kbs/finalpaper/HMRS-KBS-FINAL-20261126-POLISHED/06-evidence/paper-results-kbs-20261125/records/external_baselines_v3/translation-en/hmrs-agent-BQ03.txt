**Conclusion: MultiMedQA is the medical question-answering benchmark introduced by Google in "Large Language Models Encode Clinical Knowledge" (six existing open datasets plus the newly built HealthSearchQA, seven in total); Flan-PaLM 540B achieved 67.6% accuracy on its MedQA subset (USMLE four-option questions), surpassing the previous state of the art by more than 17%.**

### 1. What MultiMedQA is

- Definition (abstract verbatim): "we present MultiMedQA, a benchmark combining six existing open question answering datasets spanning professional medical exams, research, and consumer queries; and HealthSearchQA, a new free-response dataset of medical questions searched online."
- Composition (listed explicitly in Section 1): the six existing datasets **MedQA, MedMCQA, PubMedQA, LiveQA, MedicationQA, MMLU clinical topics**, plus the newly built seventh, **HealthSearchQA** (3,375 common health questions collected from web search).
- Design purpose: to cover the three scenarios "medical exam / medical research / consumer medical questions", together with a **human evaluation framework** (clinicians + lay users) that assesses axes such as factuality, agreement with scientific consensus, potential harm, bias, readability, and retrieval-and-reasoning evidence (Table 2, 12 axes in total; Table 3 adds 2 axes for lay users) -- because multiple-choice accuracy cannot reflect real clinical usability.

### 2. Flan-PaLM's performance on MedQA

- **Core number**: "On the MedQA dataset consisting of USMLE style questions with 4 options, our Flan-PaLM 540B model achieved a multiple-choice question (MCQ) accuracy of **67.6%**, surpassing the DRAGON model by 20.1%." (S4.1)
- Relative-improvement wording: the text elsewhere states the previous best model stood at **50.3%** and "Flan-PaLM 540B exceeded this by **17.3%**"; the section heading itself reads "Flan-PaLM exceeds previous state-of-the-art on MedQA (USMLE) by over 17%". The two percentages correspond to different comparison baselines (DRAGON and the 50.3% SOTA record) and must be distinguished when cited.
- How it was achieved: the **few-shot + chain-of-thought + self-consistency** combination of prompting (no fine-tuning), hence "prompting strategies alone".
- Multiple-choice results in the same batch: MedMCQA **57.6%**, PubMedQA **79.0%**, and SOTA on the MMLU clinical topics as well ("state-of-the-art accuracy on every MultiMedQA multiple-choice dataset").

### 3. The limitations that must be stated alongside (the paper's key point)

Strong multiple-choice performance does not mean clinically usable. In the human evaluation of Flan-PaLM's long-form answers:

- only **61.9%** agreed with scientific consensus (Med-PaLM 92.6%, clinicians 92.9%);
- **29.7%** were judged as potentially leading to harmful outcomes (Med-PaLM 5.8%, physicians 6.5%);
- only 60.6% were considered helpful by lay users (Med-PaLM 80.1%, physicians 90.8%).

Exactly for this reason the authors propose **instruction prompt tuning** (40 clinician-authored exemplars), which yields Med-PaLM.
