**Conclusion: NISQ (Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum) technology refers to "quantum computers with roughly 50 to a few hundred qubits, gate error rates of about 0.1% or above, and no protection from quantum error correction"; its central limitation is that noise caps the circuit size that can be executed reliably at roughly 1000 two-qubit gates -- and this ceiling on circuit size directly caps the computational power of NISQ technology.**

## 1. What defines NISQ (Preskill's original definition)

In *Quantum Computing in the NISQ Era and Beyond* (2018-07-30), John Preskill recounts coining the term:

> "I needed a name to describe this impending new era, so I made up a word: NISQ. This stands for Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum."

The three constituent elements, unpacked verbatim (same section, 4.1 "The 50-qubit barrier"):

| Element | Original wording |
|---|---|
| **Intermediate-scale** | "the size of quantum computers which will be available in the next few years, with **a number of qubits ranging from 50 to a few hundred**"; the point of 50 qubits is "beyond what can be simulated by brute force using the most powerful existing digital supercomputers" |
| **Noisy** | "'Noisy' emphasizes that we'll have **imperfect control over those qubits**; the noise will place serious limitations on what quantum devices can achieve in the near term" |
| **No error-correction protection** (implicit but explicit) | "When I speak of the NISQ era, I'm imagining quantum computers with **noisy gates unprotected by quantum error correction**" |

The formal statement in the abstract:

> "Quantum computers with 50-100 qubits may be able to perform tasks which surpass the capabilities of today's classical digital computers, but **noise in quantum gates will limit the size of quantum circuits that can be executed reliably**."

## 2. The central limitation: noise caps circuit size, and thereby caps computing power

The original text (S4.2 Qubit "quality") gives the quantitative chain:

> "with these noisy devices we don't expect to be able to execute a circuit that contains **many more than about 1000 gates -- that is, 1000 fundamental two-qubit operations** -- because the noise will overwhelm the signal in a circuit much larger than that. **That limitation on circuit size imposes a ceiling on the computational power of NISQ technology.**"

That is: **the essence of the limitation is not too few qubits but the fact that accumulated noise scales with the gate count until it swamps the signal**. Supporting data: two-qubit gate error rates "above the .1% level (and often much worse)"; measurement error rates around 1% (superconducting qubits). The way out is quantum error correction, but "quantum error correction imposes a heavy overhead cost in number of qubits and number of gates", so that belongs to a more distant goal.

## 3. Corroboration: an equivalent characterization in a second paper (P1, partially consistent)

*Benchmarking Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum...* (arXiv 2303.00445), S I:

> "We find ourselves in the era of noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) computation, which is characterized by various obstacles including **restrictive qubit topologies, short coherence times and imperfect quantum gates**."

This **complements the causes** of the "noise" limitation (restricted topology, short coherence times, imperfect gates) and is the same limitation as Preskill's "gate error rate -> circuit-size ceiling" stated at a different level; it does not constitute a conflict.
