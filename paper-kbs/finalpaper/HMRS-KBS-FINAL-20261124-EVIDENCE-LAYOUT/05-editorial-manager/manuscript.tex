\begin{abstract}
When document collections change, retrieval depends on where documents are
stored, how they are described and whether the search index reflects those
changes. The Hierarchical Management Retrieval System (HMRS) links
collection, document and segment management to agent retrieval. Its
catalogue, shelf and document views let an agent narrow a search and
inspect source passages through the Model Context Protocol. Lifecycle
probes distinguished stale index entries from stale labels served by a
retrieval cache. On 72 questions, a scripted selector identified the labelled
collection in
72 cases with description-bearing cards and 37 with nominally name-only
cards. Correct routing did not guarantee that the evidence pack contained
the target. Combining scored document text with prioritised chunk windows
raised target-string containment from 37 to 52 of 72 questions. The
arm-specific text counters recorded 309~kB per question for this
configuration and 31.4~kB for the reference, at different logging boundaries.
Both same-question reruns retained the gain. In 16 fixed candidate pools,
reordering by verifier score reduced top-five gold-document hits from 15
to seven. In the native eight-configuration deployment case study, a blind judge
rated all ten HMRS answers correct, but no paired contrast with the three
baselines indexed on the intended corpus was significant. The deployed
agent read source documents and searched outside its configured
collections; the indexed corpora were not all matched. These results support
separate evaluation of collection selection, target-string containment and
candidate ordering. They do not establish better backend answer quality.
\end{abstract}
