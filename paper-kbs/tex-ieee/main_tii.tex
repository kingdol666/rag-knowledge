\begin{abstract}
Industrial enterprises accumulate process regulations, failure analyses,
and equipment-telemetry reports faster than domain experts can search
them. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) promises natural-language
access to these stores, yet the evidence behind an answer is rarely
verified: a document ranked top by vector similarity can enter an answer
without its content ever being checked against the question. This article
presents QDCVR (Query-Driven Content-Verified Retrieval), a self-hosted
knowledge-base platform for industrial settings in which every candidate
citation must pass a verification gate---a locally hosted judge that reads
the fetched document and scores whether it contains direct evidence for
the query---and three interchangeable retrieval lanes (vector,
hierarchical, server-side hybrid) that all terminate at this gate. On a
production corpus of five industrial photovoltaic knowledge bases
(42 documents, 16 gold-annotated questions), the gate's verdicts expose a
same-domain inflation failure: non-gold documents receive scores (median
0.86, $n{=}174$) with no statistically detectable separation from gold
documents (median 0.88, $n{=}17$; Mann--Whitney $p{=}0.26$), and the
judged pipeline's Hit@5 falls from its fusion stage's 0.94 to 0.44, while
an offline cut-margin replay retains every judged gold at margin 0.20.
Out-of-corpus probes show the threshold alone never abstains; honest
not-found behavior lives in the reading-and-answer layer. In end-to-end
agent runs (full grid run twice), all knowledge-base-grounded lanes named
the gold document in all twelve runs and reproduced its fact markers,
whereas a bare agent never cited one; wall-clock time is dominated by
agent turns ($\approx$12--15\,s) rather than retrieval (sub-second). We
distill deployment lessons for verifiable industrial retrieval: verifier
calibration is the open problem, honesty needs an enforcement layer, and
silent index loss must be treated as failure.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{I}{ndustrial} manufacturers accumulate process regulations,
failure-analysis reports, and operational lessons as unstructured
documents spread across shared drives. In a photovoltaic (PV) module
plant, answering a concrete engineering question---\emph{why did edge
delamination appear in the EL inspection of a laminated module?}---means
locating the one report among thousands that records the decision,
reading it, and citing it. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) addresses
the location step by embedding documents and queries into a shared vector
space \cite{rag-lewis,dpr}, and modern agentic stacks let a large
language model (LLM) orchestrate retrieval tools iteratively
\cite{mcp,singh-agentic-rag}. Yet the dominant ranking signal---dense
cosine similarity---measures \emph{topic relatedness}, not \emph{evidence
presence}. A document that merely discusses similar materials can outrank
the one that contains the answer, and a fluent generator can readily
synthesize an answer from the wrong source: the well-documented
hallucination and misgrounding behavior of LLM pipelines \cite{siren}.

This article argues that a primitive most deployed RAG stacks leave
implicit is an \emph{explicit content-verification gate}: before a
candidate document may support an answer, its actual text must be fetched
and judged against the query by a model whose only task is to answer
``does this text contain direct evidence for this question?'' Vectors are
fast; content is accurate. We built QDCVR (Query-Driven Content-Verified
Retrieval), a self-hosted platform that makes this primitive the backbone
of a complete industrial knowledge-base system---content-routed
organization at ingestion, a 97-tool Model Context Protocol (MCP) server
\cite{mcp} as the machine interface, and three retrieval lanes that all
terminate at the same gate---and, because the primitive is itself a
model, subjects it to the same scrutiny as any model
(Section~\ref{sec:inflation}).

Three lanes embody a deliberate latency/recall trade-off for industrial
operations. The \emph{Search} lane is a vector fast lane for pinned
libraries. The \emph{Librarian} lane walks library$\to$document catalogs,
routes enumeration-style queries to a machine scanner, and judges
survivors in small batches---all within a hard budget of twelve tool
calls. The \emph{Hybrid} lane collapses vector and catalog channels into
a single server-side orchestrated call that merges candidates, judges
them, applies a relative cut with a top-5 floor, and returns full text
for the top documents. All lanes fail closed: zero verified evidence
yields an explicit not-found report, never a speculative answer.

We assess the platform with two experiment tiers on a production corpus
of five industrial PV knowledge bases (42 documents, 16 gold-annotated
questions), two full repetitions of the agentic grid, and out-of-corpus
abstention probes. The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A verifiable-retrieval architecture}
(Sections~\ref{sec:system}--\ref{sec:lanes}): content-verified retrieval
as a platform primitive whose measured value lies in auditability,
per-citation provenance, and fail-closed semantics---with three
interchangeable lanes under enforced budgets and an engine-agnostic
harness layer exposed entirely as MCP tools.
\item \textbf{A two-tier empirical analysis}
(Sections~\ref{sec:setup}--\ref{sec:results}): at the tool layer, BM25
and reciprocal-rank fusion are strong baselines on a curated same-domain
corpus, and dense retrieval misses exactly one question through a
near-duplicate confuser family; the gate's re-ranking \emph{reduces}
Hit@5 from 0.94 to 0.44 when confusers dominate the pool. At the agent
layer, all knowledge-base-grounded lanes ground their answers in gold
documents while latency is almost entirely LLM turns.
\item \textbf{A quantified verifier-failure analysis}
(Section~\ref{sec:inflation}): same-domain evidence inflation---non-gold
documents scoring median 0.86 versus gold 0.88 with no statistical
separation---plus an offline cut-margin replay that converts the failure
into a calibration result (margin 0.20 retains all judged golds).
\item \textbf{Operational lessons from production use}
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}): three documented incidents with
detection symptoms and fixes, including resident judge workers
(10--12\,s$\to$1--2\,s per batch) and honest-failure observability for
silent index loss.
\end{enumerate}

A preliminary demonstration of the platform's organization layer appeared
as a demo paper \cite{qdcvr-demo}; this article adds the multi-lane
agentic retrieval methodology, the end-to-end evaluation, the
verifier-failure analysis, and the deployment lessons.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Generation and Ranking}
RAG couples a retriever with a generator to ground open-domain QA
\cite{rag-lewis}; dense retrievers encode queries and passages into a
shared space \cite{dpr}, and multi-lingual embedders such as BGE-M3
support hybrid dense--sparse signals \cite{bge-m3}. Lexical probabilistic
ranking remains a strong baseline in many regimes \cite{bm25}, and
reciprocal-rank fusion (RRF) merges ranked lists with one parameter
\cite{rrf-cormack}. Our tool-layer evaluation confirms this picture on a
curated industrial corpus: BM25 and RRF match or beat dense retrieval at
Hit@5 with millisecond latency. QDCVR uses dense, BM25, and graph
signals, but treats all of them as \emph{candidate generators} whose
output must still pass content verification.

\subsection{Industrial Knowledge Systems}
Industrial-informatics platforms organize production knowledge explicitly:
graph models enable AI-based production reconfiguration \cite{rrpdg}, and
LLM-driven multiagent frameworks automate industrial knowledge-graph
construction \cite{comaikg}. QDCVR is complementary: it operates on the
unstructured-document side of the same problem, maintaining a lean
Document/KnowledgeBase/Tag graph with weighted \textsc{RelatedTo} edges
for navigation and candidate expansion rather than global summarization
\cite{graphrag,lightrag,raptor}. Our measurements show keyword-limited
graph search is the weakest standalone signal on this corpus (Hit@5
0.56), which is why the platform never lets it answer alone.

\subsection{Verification, Correction, and Abstention}
Self-RAG trains a generator to critique retrieved evidence
\cite{selfrag}; CRAG re-queries when retrieval quality is low \cite{crag};
FLARE interleaves generation with retrieval triggers \cite{flare}; Kamath
et al.\ formalize selective prediction under domain shift \cite{kamath};
and CyberBOT applies ontology-grounded retrieval for
reliability-critical domains \cite{cyberbot}. These approaches verify
\emph{during generation} with the generator itself. QDCVR externalizes
verification into a dedicated judge that sees only (query, document-text)
pairs and is consumed as a tool by any lane or agent; its verdicts fail
closed, making verification an inspectable, reusable platform service
rather than an implicit property of one generator's prompting.

\subsection{Agentic Retrieval and Tool Protocols}
ReAct established interleaved reasoning and acting over tools
\cite{react-yao}; tool-learning extended it to self-supervised tool use
\cite{toolformer}; MCP standardizes tool servers \cite{mcp}; and recent
surveys systematize agentic RAG \cite{singh-agentic-rag}, listing
efficiency among its open challenges. DeepRead shows structure-aware
navigation over long documents improves agentic search \cite{deepread},
matching our librarian lane's document-coordinate reading. QDCVR
contributes an agentic-retrieval \emph{policy} layer: per-lane tool
whitelists enforced by the server, hard call budgets, and single-call
server-side orchestration targeting the turn count that our measurements
(Section~\ref{sec:composition}) show dominates latency.

\section{The QDCVR Platform}
\label{sec:system}

\subsection{Design Principles}
QDCVR (v2.3) is a self-hosted platform that turns a folder of PDFs,
Office documents, and scans into knowledge bases that agents can be
trusted to answer from. Five principles shape the architecture
(Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{P1 (content-routed organization):} documents are organized
by what they \emph{say}, not by which folder they sat in; ingestion
derives tags and descriptions from extracted content, and retrieval
starts from a library catalog rather than a directory path.
\item \textbf{P2 (MCP-first machine interface):} every capability is a
tool of an MCP server \cite{mcp}---97 tools in eight groups (search,
graph, judging, experience, persona management, ingestion/parsing,
library lifecycle, filesystem/tags)---so web, HTTP, CLI, and MCP clients
share one layer.
\item \textbf{P3 (verification before promotion):} no similarity score
promotes a document into an answer; a judge must read the candidate's
fetched content and vote. Verdicts fail closed: missing text, engine
errors, or timeouts reject the candidate.
\item \textbf{P4 (honest abstention):} when verification eliminates
every candidate, the system returns an explicit not-found report listing
what was searched. Refusing to answer is a terminal success state.
\item \textbf{P5 (engine-agnostic agency):} answer agents are pluggable
harnesses (14 adapters); the platform constrains them through tool
whitelists and prompts, never by coupling to a vendor.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ingestion and Storage}
Ingestion is a nine-step, quality-gated flow executed by an archival
sub-agent through MCP tools: (1)~tool probing; (2)~duplicate detection
(SHA-256 plus near-duplicate vector search at $\geq 0.90$ cosine);
(3)~library census; (4)~parsing with MinerU \cite{mineru} (local OCR for
scanned PDFs); (5)~a semantic split gate for documents beyond 30\,000
characters (agent-proposed boundaries, 0.85 target utilization, no
oversized atomic units); (6)~content-derived tagging and description;
(7)~multi-channel indexing (vector + graph + BM25); (8)~a post-condition
retrievability check; (9)~an audited report. Descriptions are treated as
\emph{claims to be verified} rather than truth---in an audit of 26
descriptions on a predecessor corpus, 24 mismatched their documents. And
indexing success is judged by observable effects
(\texttt{total\_chunks}$>0$), because we observed GPU out-of-memory
failures that returned success envelopes with empty index payloads.

Four stores back the platform: a tree filesystem (Markdown plus metadata
registry); per-library ChromaDB \cite{chroma} collections of BGE-M3
embeddings \cite{bge-m3} (1024-d, cosine, 500-char chunks, 50-char
overlap, HNSW \cite{hnsw}); Neo4j \cite{neo4j} with
Document/KnowledgeBase/Tag nodes and weighted \textsc{RelatedTo} edges;
and a jieba-tokenized BM25 inverted index \cite{bm25}. Domain services
wrap these stores; the two-stage service fuses BM25 and graph expansion
before a dense refine; the experience service versions operational
lessons as first-class retrievable documents; and the verification gate
is served by a resident judge worker (Section~\ref{sec:impl}).

\section{Multi-Lane Verified Retrieval}
\label{sec:lanes}

All answering agents reach the platform through one chat endpoint. The
server-side \emph{lane policy} attaches per-lane tool whitelists and
instructions; a librarian-lane agent is denied the vector tools at the
protocol level, so the lane is a property of the enforcement layer, not
of prompt discipline. Fig.~\ref{fig:lanes} summarizes the three lanes.

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig2_lanes}
\caption{The three retrieval lanes. Every lane ends at the same
verification gate and the same honesty contract: zero verified evidence
escalates once (Lane A$\to$B) or terminates in an explicit not-found
report.}
\label{fig:lanes}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Lane A: Search --- Vector Fast Lane With a Gate}
Lane A optimizes latency on pinned libraries. Phase A0 rewrites the query
into (subject, attribute, constraints) form and routes incident-style
queries to the experience store first. A1 issues one wide dense query
(top-30, threshold 0.35, cross-library balancing) and deduplicates by
document. A2 submits citation-only references to the verification gate in
batches of at most six; the server fetches full text itself. A document
with vector score 0.95 that the judge rejects is dropped; a document at
0.40 with one \emph{yes} verdict survives. A3 returns the judge's
evidence pack and the agent composes an answer whose citations carry
confidence levels (P0 direct, P1 partial, P2 weak).

\subsection{Lane B: Librarian --- Hierarchical Navigation Under a Budget}
Lane B targets exhaustive recall across all libraries. It walks a fixed
spine within twelve tool calls: L0 reads the library catalog and prunes
shelves (libraries); L1 lists each surviving shelf's documents;
L2 routes by query shape---literal/enumeration questions to a machine
scanner ($\approx$0.5\,s per document, no LLM turns), semantic questions
to judging; L3 judges in batches of at most six references; L4 reads only
survivors (2\,000 characters from the judge-reported evidence line); L5
emits a five-part report (search paths, answer, sources, confidence,
blind spots). Catalog descriptions never prune documents by themselves,
and an exhausted budget terminates in blind-spot disclosure, not a
guessed answer.

\subsection{Lane C: Hybrid --- Server-Side Orchestration in One Call}
Because agentic latency is dominated by LLM turns (Section
\ref{sec:composition}), Lane C spends exactly one call. A single
\texttt{kb\_hybrid\_search} executes P1 (a parallel block: vector channel
top-30 at threshold 0.35, and catalog keyword channel); P3 merges
candidates by (library, document) with agreed-by-both documents first,
capped at twelve; P4 judges every candidate (criterion \emph{evidence},
threshold 0.5); P4b applies a relative cut---keep scores
$\geq \mathit{best}-0.15$ with a top-5 floor and a cross-domain anchor
guard; P5 reads the top five documents in full. The response carries the
complete audit trail, so a one-call lane is still an inspectable lane.

\subsection{The Verification Gate}
\label{sec:gate}
The gate is a locally hosted evidence-classification model (the Laya
judging service \cite{laya}) invoked through a \texttt{kb\_laya\_judge}
tool: given the query and a batch of references, fetch each document
server-side, segment it, and score \emph{does this text contain direct
evidence that answers the query} under criteria \emph{evidence}
(specific substantiation; topic match insufficient), \emph{instance}
(contains a member of the requested class), or \emph{auto}. Scores live
in $[0,1]$ with acceptance threshold 0.5 (0.3 for enumeration); the
platform documents them as a 0--8 rubric (topic 0--3, scenario 0--3,
evidence 0--2), of which the $[0,1]$ score is the machine-readable
projection. All operating constants---thresholds, six-reference batches,
the twelve-candidate cap, and the 0.15 cut margin with top-5 floor---are
shipped production defaults fixed on an earlier corpus, not tuned on the
evaluation questions. Fail-closed semantics apply to every failure mode.

\subsection{Honest Abstention}
If a lane ends with zero verified documents, Lane A escalates once to
Lane B; if Lane B also exhausts its budget, the answer is the not-found
report: what was searched, how the query was routed, and which topics sit
outside the corpus. Because abstention is a terminal state in the lane
contract---enforced by the server-side policy rather than advised in a
prompt---it can be evaluated like any other outcome
(Section~\ref{sec:abstention}).

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:impl}
The backend is Python 3.12/FastAPI; stores are ChromaDB and Neo4j 5.20;
embeddings are BGE-M3 \cite{bge-m3} (lazy singleton, LRU encode cache);
parsing uses MinerU \cite{mineru}; lexical indexing uses jieba
\cite{jieba}. The MCP server exposes 97 tools over SSE and stdio
\cite{mcp}. The judge runs as a resident worker with a machine-level file
lock, an LRU verdict cache, and explicit CUDA-cache release: a cold judge
load costs 10--12\,s per batch, the resident worker 1--2\,s; when judge
and bulk-embedding workloads ran interleaved without cache cleanup, one
end-to-end run degraded from 68\,s to 27 minutes under the Windows WDDM
display-page manager (internal profiling; released log). The web tier is
Nuxt 3 with a chat endpoint adapting 14 harnesses; the lane policy
whitelists 21 read-only MCP tools per retrieval turn and denies 40+
harness-internal tools which, when left enabled in our profiling, added
14--170\,s of detour behavior per run. An experience subsystem versions
operational lessons with a higher retrieval threshold (0.55) and a
rules-based rolling summary every fifth ingestion.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Industrial Corpus and Gold Questions}
The live catalog contains five production knowledge bases from an
industrial PV setting: thin-film process regulations (3 documents), a
PV-module failure-analysis library (2), a PV-plant O\&M library (5), an
industrial workshop library with 31 documents including 20 near-duplicate
equipment-telemetry reports, and a one-document production watch-clause
library---42 documents. The telemetry cluster is a deliberate stressor:
its documents share prefixes, domains, and vocabulary, mimicking the
confuser density of real deployments. We authored sixteen domain
questions (released with the evaluation artifacts), each with one or two
gold documents identified by unique filename fragments and 1--3 fact
markers (numbers or terms any grounded answer must contain, e.g.\ the
61--68\% vs.\ $\geq$85\% cure-degree contrast and the recorded
$-8^\circ$C thermocouple drift for the EL question, and the ``TC0''
dead-loop verdict for the telemetry question). Two corpus-integrity
incidents surfaced during setup and were repaired through the platform's
own remediation path before any measurement (one document missing from
its collection; one library whose collection had been silently lost)---
instances of the failure class discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection{Methods, Arms, and Probes}
\emph{Tool layer} (Section~\ref{sec:retrieval}): seven methods produce
top-5 document lists over the full 42-document catalog with no pinning:
BM25 (jieba+Okapi, $k_1{=}1.5$, $b{=}0.75$, full texts), Dense
(\texttt{kb\_search\_vector}, per-document best chunk), RRF ($k{=}60$),
Graph (\texttt{kb\_graph\_search} over jieba keywords), Two-stage
(\texttt{kb\_search\_two-stage}), Fusion-only (the hybrid merge order
without judging), and QDCVR-Hybrid (the full lane with judging). All
output document-level rankings; all platform methods are invoked exactly
as an external agent would invoke them, through the MCP interface.
\emph{Agent layer} (Section~\ref{sec:agentic}): four arms answer four
questions through the public chat API, non-streaming, 20-turn budget:
\emph{Search} (library pinned), \emph{Librarian} (all libraries),
\emph{Hybrid} (library pinned + Lane C suffix), \emph{Bare} (no
knowledge-base tools). Pinned arms search a single library---as small as
two documents---so their confuser pools are a fraction of the full
catalog; Section~\ref{sec:agentic} returns to this. Every arm runs on the
\texttt{omp} CLI agent engine\footnote{\url{https://github.com/can1357/oh-my-pi}}
(v18.4.10) wrapping the same LLM (GLM-5.3-Flash\footnote{Model card:
\url{https://open.bigmodel.cn}}, default thinking level). The full grid
is run twice. Zero-turn empty responses and transient timeouts are
retried once (fired on two runs, both successful on re-run).
\emph{Abstention and calibration probes}: four out-of-corpus queries
probe the honesty contract at the tool layer, two of them also at the
agent layer (across all three grounded arms); and because every judged
score is logged, the hybrid cut's margin is replayed offline across a
sweep of values (Section~\ref{sec:inflation}).

\subsection{Metrics}
Tool layer: Hit@5, Recall@5, MRR, per-call latency (warmed). Agent layer:
end-to-end wall time, agent turns, gold-document citation hit (automated
substring check against unique filename fragments), and fact-marker hits
(automated substring checks; we additionally report decontaminated checks
computed after removing answer lines that name a gold document). Refusal
is a hedging marker in the answer's opening 200 characters. No human
rater was involved. Hardware: one RTX 5060 (8\,GB) serves embeddings and
the judge worker; all services run locally.

Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} and Fig.~\ref{fig:tool} give the tool-layer
results. Three observations matter for practitioners. \textbf{Curated
same-domain corpora favor lexical precision}: with 42 well-described
documents and questions in the corpus's vocabulary, BM25 achieves Hit@5
1.00 (MRR 0.958) at millisecond latency, RRF matches it, and two-stage
fusion joins them once the corpus is healthy. Dense retrieval misses
exactly one question (Q16): the query asks about the
\emph{heatdecay092 post-compensation verification} while three
near-duplicate sibling reports from the same incident family crowd the
top-5---a confuser-density failure, not a vocabulary one. Graph search,
limited to \textsc{contains} matching on names and tags, is the weakest
standalone signal (0.56). \textbf{Verification is cheap; re-ranking is
the risk}: judging twelve candidates adds well under a second, but the
QDCVR-Hybrid arm loses top-5 hits relative to its own fusion stage
(0.44 vs.\ 0.94). The failure decomposes cleanly: on eight questions the
gold document \emph{was} judged and the gate's verdicts reordered
same-domain confusers above it; on Q16 the gold never reached the judge
at all---all twelve candidate slots were taken by confusers upstream in
the fusion stage. Section~\ref{sec:inflation} quantifies why.

\subsection{Agent Layer: Three Lanes, One Honesty Contract}
\label{sec:agentic}

Table~\ref{tab:agentic} and Fig.~\ref{fig:e2e} summarize the round-1
runs; we present them as an end-to-end case study of the deployed system
rather than a statistical comparison. \textbf{Grounding is a property of
the lanes, not of the model.} All three grounded arms named the gold
document in all twelve valid runs and reproduced all twelve available
fact-marker sets---including the exact watch-clause numbers
(31.35\,g, $\pm$0.05\,g, 5\,min) and the ``TC0'' thermocouple verdict.
The marker hits survive decontamination (after deleting every answer line
that names a gold document, all twelve grounded runs still reproduce
their full marker sets). The bare arm never cited a source: on the EL
question it produced a plausible \emph{textbook} diagnosis that misses
the corpus's recorded root cause; on the telemetry question it hit none
of the markers. \textbf{Reconciling the two layers.} At the tool layer
the judged hybrid missed nine of sixteen questions; at the agent layer
the hybrid arm hit the gold on all four. Two contract-level differences
explain this: pinned scope (Q2's failure-analysis library holds two
documents, so same-domain confusers never enter its pool) and agency (a
lane that can read what it retrieved and issue follow-up calls is not
bound by one ranking). The tool layer measures ranking in isolation; the
agent layer measures the lane as deployed. \textbf{Partial honesty
counts}: hybrid-Q4 cites the POE gold document correctly and states
explicitly that the library holds no EVA-side comparison data---a scoped,
honest disclosure; no grounded run fabricated a citation. \textbf{Turn
structure buys latency profiles}: hybrid completed every round-1 question
in 3--5 turns (36--70\,s); librarian spent 5--6 turns (54--91\,s) buying
exhaustive coverage with blind-spot disclosure; search needed 8 turns on
the hardest question. \textbf{Repetition: outcomes reproduce, latency
does not.} In round 2, citation outcomes agree on all 16 cells; the
decontaminated fact-marker check is 12/12 in round 1 and 11/12 in round 2
(in the one exception the key values appear only inside evidence lines
that also cite the gold document---an artifact of the line-deletion rule,
not a grounding failure). Wall time and turn counts fluctuate
substantially (e.g.\ search-Q2: 70\,s/6 turns vs.\ 165\,s/11 turns),
which is exactly what the turn-economics model predicts and the
strongest argument for fixed turn budgets. \textbf{The fast lane has a
real tail}: two first-pass runs hit the server's 600\,s turn budget and
returned gateway timeouts; both re-runs completed normally.

\subsection{The Verifier's Own Failure: Same-Domain Inflation}
\label{sec:inflation}

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig4_inflation}
\caption{Evidence-criterion judge scores from the sixteen QDCVR-Hybrid
runs: all 191 judged candidates. Gold documents ($n{=}17$; Q16's gold
never entered its candidate pool) and same-domain non-gold survivors
($n{=}174$) overlap almost completely: medians 0.876 vs.\ 0.863,
Mann--Whitney $p{=}0.26$.}
\label{fig:inflation}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig5_margin}
\caption{Offline replay of the relative cut (top-5 floor unchanged):
golds kept and mean kept-set size versus cut margin. The shipped 0.15
margin loses one gold; 0.20 retains all 17.}
\label{fig:margin}
\end{figure}

Fig.~\ref{fig:inflation} plots every judge score from the sixteen hybrid
runs: 191 candidates (twelve per run except Q14, whose merge produced
eleven). Not one candidate was rejected---every logged score is
$\geq 0.589$---so at the threshold layer the gate's filtering power on
this corpus is zero; the failures concentrate in ranking. Aggregating the
17 in-pool gold instances: only 5 rank in the top-3 of the judged
ordering, and 9 rank eighth or lower. Gold and non-gold score
distributions are statistically indistinguishable at this sample size
(medians 0.876 vs.\ 0.863; Mann--Whitney $p{=}0.26$, a gold-favoring
trend with Cliff's $\delta\approx0.17$; scores are nested within 16 runs,
so we treat the test as indicative only). On Q6, the fusion ranking
placed the gold clause document first, but judged re-ranking promoted
five unrelated telemetry reports (0.92--0.93) while the gold scored 0.82
---pushing it out of the top-5 the answer agent reads; on Q2,
extruder-telemetry reports and an unrelated PET document (0.89--0.93)
outrank the gold EL report (0.85). Even the relative cut participates:
it bound in most rounds, and on Q11 its best-minus-0.15 line fell
\emph{above} the gold's score (0.741 vs.\ 0.711), deleting the gold
outright.

\textbf{An offline replay turns this failure into a calibration result}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:margin}). Sweeping the margin from 0.05 to 0.35: at 0.05
the kept set shrinks to a mean 6.4 candidates but retains only 8/17
golds; the shipped 0.15 margin keeps 10.4 candidates and 16/17 golds; at
0.20 all 17 golds survive with 11.1 retained. The margin is repairable by
a small configuration change---an analysis the platform should run per
library at deployment time---but no margin separates golds from
confusers, because the scores themselves overlap.

\textbf{Cross-domain claims do not survive measurement either.} On four
out-of-corpus probe queries, dense retrieval still returns same-corpus
documents at 0.40--0.60 cosine, and the judged hybrid keeps 9--11 of its
12 candidates---the same inflation, now cross-domain (one early
production incident in which an out-of-domain document scored 0.92 on a
PV question motivated the anchor guard; the probes show such inflation is
the norm). What the probes establish is where honesty actually lives:
not in the threshold, but in the reading-and-answer layer
(Section~\ref{sec:abstention}). The quantified repair is cut-margin
calibration per library; the open problem is the score overlap itself:
domain-aware negative training, score normalization against a
per-library null distribution, or pairing the judge with a contradiction
check. We report this as a measured open problem rather than a solved
one.

\subsection{Honest Abstention Under Out-of-Corpus Probes}
\label{sec:abstention}

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig6_ood}
\caption{Out-of-corpus probes at the tool layer: the judged hybrid keeps
9--11 of 12 candidates and dense retrieval passes chunks above the 0.35
threshold for every probe---the threshold alone never abstains. At the
agent layer, all six probe runs (three arms $\times$ two probes) returned
explicit not-found reports with zero fabricated citations.}
\label{fig:ood}
\end{figure}

The lanes' final contract was never exercised by the in-corpus gold
questions. The four probes (lithium-battery thermal runaway, child
vaccination schedules, Python concurrency, injection-mold vent design)
close that gap at both layers. At the tool layer, abstention does
\emph{not} happen (Fig.~\ref{fig:ood}): the threshold keeps 9--11 of 12
candidates even for vaccination or battery queries. At the agent layer,
the contract holds without exception: all six runs answered with an
explicit not-found report---each enumerates the libraries searched,
states that the corpus does not cover the topic, and declines to answer
from memory; none cites a document as evidence (zero \texttt{.md}
citations across all six transcripts). One librarian run voluntarily
reports that its vector and experience channels were unavailable that
round and it ``cannot claim exhaustive coverage.'' The honesty contract
is real, but its enforcement point is the reading-and-answer layer---
precisely why the platform wraps verification inside lanes that must read
before they answer, rather than exposing a bare similarity threshold.

\subsection{Where the Time Goes: LLM Turns, Not Retrieval}
\label{sec:composition}
Machine-side retrieval is uniformly sub-second
(Table~\ref{tab:retrieval}) while the twelve grounded end-to-end runs
take 36--91\,s: wall time is dominated by LLM turns at 12--15\,s on
average (per-arm means: search 11.7, librarian 12.8, hybrid 15.3). The
lanes differ almost entirely in turn structure; the practical
consequence is an engineering rule the platform now follows everywhere:
\emph{move orchestration server-side and cap turns}---the single-call
hybrid lane is both the fastest and the most auditable, because its
audit trail ships with the one response.

\section{Discussion and Lessons Learned}
\label{sec:discussion}
\textbf{L1. Vectors are fast; content is accurate---and verification has
two costs.} The tool-layer table is the cleanest statement of the
retrieval subproblem the platform must address: neither a millisecond
BM25 nor an 18\,ms dense retriever knows whether a document
\emph{answers} the question. The gate converts ranked candidates into
accountable, per-citation-provenance evidence; its costs are temporal
($\approx$0.5\,s per dozen candidates; 1--2\,s per six-document batch in
production load) and, on this corpus, an accuracy one
(0.94$\to$0.44 Hit@5). Auditability is what the gate buys today; ranking
quality is what calibration must earn back.
\textbf{L2. Latency engineering is turn engineering.} Tool whitelists
that remove detour tools, one-call server-side pipelines, and small judge
batches took hybrid runs from $\approx$29 turns in the earliest serial
configuration to 3--5 turns.
\textbf{L3. The verifier is a model too---calibrate it.} The margin
replay is the constructive form of this lesson; the threshold itself
calibrated on cross-domain negatives does not separate same-domain
near-misses.
\textbf{L4. Honesty needs an enforcement layer, not a prompt.} The
probes show a threshold cannot refuse---it accepted every out-of-corpus
candidate it was shown---while the reading-and-answer layer refused all
six probe prompts with disclosed searches and zero fabricated citations.
\textbf{L5. Production incidents taught honest observability.} A
corrupted HNSW segment made one library silently un-retrievable across
lanes (detected because ``success with zero results'' is suspect);
GPU-OOM indexing returned success envelopes with empty payloads (success
is now judged by chunk counts); and both failure classes recurred during
evaluation setup and were repaired through the platform's own
remediation path. Every layer reports what it actually verified, and
every consumer treats unverifiable as failed.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\textbf{Scale.} The corpus is 42 documents; absolute Hit@5 values will
not transfer to corpora orders of magnitude larger, where the candidate
cap and shelf pruning become load-bearing. \textbf{Question set.} Sixteen
tool-layer and four agent-layer questions were authored by the system's
developers; all golds and markers are released, but the sample favors the
corpus's vocabulary. \textbf{Judge and engine dependence.} Scores and
turn counts come from one judge and one engine; lanes, not models, are
the object of comparison. \textbf{Nondeterminism.} We report two full
repetitions (citation agreement 16/16; latency fluctuates substantially)
and release all per-run logs; the OOD probe answers were audited
manually. \textbf{BM25 granularity.} The BM25 baseline is an
evaluation-side reimplementation over full texts; its granularity and
the questions' corpus-vocabulary phrasing both favor lexical matching.

\section{Conclusion}
QDCVR turns ``the retriever found it'' into ``the system verified it'':
organization, retrieval, and agency all terminate at an explicit
content-verification gate; three interchangeable lanes trade latency for
recall under enforced budgets; and the system's refusal to speculate is a
first-class outcome. On a production industrial corpus, lexical and fused
retrieval remain strong tool-layer baselines; the verification gate costs
well under a second but inherits a same-domain blind spot that we
quantify and partially repair by cut-margin replay; the honesty contract
refuses every out-of-corpus probe at the answer layer even though the
threshold refuses nothing; and end-to-end latency belongs to LLM turns,
so the fastest lane is the one that moved its orchestration
server-side. The immediate roadmap is verifier calibration against
per-library null distributions, larger-corpus evaluation with the
platform's shipped benchmark suite, and lane auto-selection from
query-shape features---steps toward industrial retrieval systems whose
trustworthiness is an inspectable property of the pipeline rather than a
hope about the model.
