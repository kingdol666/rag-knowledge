\section{Discussion and lessons learned}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{L1. Vectors are fast; content is accurate --- and verification has
two costs.} The tool-layer table is the cleanest statement of the retrieval
subproblem the platform must address: on a curated corpus a millisecond
BM25 beats an 18\,ms dense retriever, and neither knows whether a document
\emph{answers} the question. The verification gate converts ranked
candidates into accountable, per-citation-provenance evidence; its costs
are both temporal ($\approx$0.5\,s per dozen candidates end-to-end in
Table~\ref{tab:retrieval}; 1--2\,s per six-document batch for the
resident worker in production load) and, on this corpus, an accuracy one
(0.94$\to$0.44 Hit@5 under same-domain inflation,
Section~\ref{sec:inflation}). Auditability is what the gate buys today;
ranking quality is what calibration must earn back.

\textbf{L2. Latency engineering is turn engineering.} With retrieval
sub-second, the only lever that matters for interactive latency is the
number of agent turns. Tool whitelists that remove detour tools, one-call
server-side pipelines, and small judge batches that avoid transport
timeouts together took our hybrid runs from $\approx$29 turns in the
earliest serial-skill configuration (historical runs, logs released) to
3--5 turns in the present measurements.


\textbf{L3. The verifier is a model too --- calibrate it.}
Section~\ref{sec:inflation} is the paper's cautionary result: a verifier
inherits domain-internal blind spots, and a threshold calibrated on
cross-domain negatives (0.5) does not separate same-domain near-misses.
Fail-closed semantics and reading requirements contain the damage; they do
not replace calibration.

\textbf{L4. Honesty needs an enforcement layer, not a prompt.} Lane
separation works because the server physically withholds tools: a
librarian-lane agent cannot call the vector tool even if instructed to.
The out-of-corpus probes (Section~\ref{sec:abstention}) show the same
asymmetry for abstention: a threshold cannot refuse --- it accepted every
out-of-corpus candidate it was shown --- while the reading-and-answer
layer refused all six probe prompts with disclosed searches and zero
fabricated citations. Honesty is a property of the pipeline's shape, not
of the model's mood. The dichotomy is about reliability, not exclusivity:
the bare agent did refuse explicitly in two of its four runs --- honest
about its ignorance, but holding no corpus facts either way --- while in
the others it produced confident, unsourced, partly wrong answers whose
wrongness no audit trail exposes. Unassisted honesty is a coin flip;
enforced honesty is inspectable.

\textbf{L5. Production incidents taught honest observability.} Three
incidents shaped the implementation: a corrupted HNSW segment that made one
library silently un-retrievable across lanes (fixed by re-index; detected
because ``success with zero results'' is itself treated as suspect);
GPU-OOM indexing that returned success envelopes with empty vector payloads
(success is now judged by chunk counts); and judge/engine failures that
must reject candidates rather than pass them. The common thread: every
layer reports what it actually verified, and every consumer treats
unverifiable as failed. Table~\ref{tab:failures} consolidates the incident
log into the failure-mode catalogue we wish we had on day one.

\subsection{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}
\textbf{Scale.} The live corpus is 42 documents across five libraries;
absolute Hit@5 values (e.g.\ BM25's 1.00) will not transfer to corpora
orders of magnitude larger, where the hybrid lane's candidate cap and the
librarian's shelf pruning become load-bearing rather than optional. The
benchmark suite shipped with the platform is the intended scaling path;
as a first probe, its ten gold arXiv papers now serve as a domain-shifted
second corpus (Section~\ref{sec:robustness}), and a
hundreds-of-documents run with an author-independent question set remains
the full answer.
\textbf{Question set.} Sixteen tool-layer and four agent-layer questions
were authored by the system's developers; all gold documents and fact
markers are released for independent scrutiny, but the sample is small and
favors the corpus's vocabulary. The paraphrase grid quantifies exactly
that exposure --- BM25's ceiling drops to 0.88 under rewording while the
dense backbone and the verifier findings hold --- and a larger
author-independent question set remains the main scaling need. One
circularity is worth naming rather than silently benefiting from: Q16's
question embeds its gold document's filename token (heatdecay092), which
guarantees lexical success on that question; the paraphrase grid keeps
the token, so the robustness tier does not break this specific shortcut. The
fix is mechanical and scheduled for the next revision: reword Q16 to describe
the event without the filename token (or replace it with a held-out question)
and re-run the tool layer; because Q16 is the one question on which the fusion
stage loses a gold, the correction can only tighten, never loosen, the
reported decomposition.
\textbf{Experimental controls.} The tool layer isolates the gate's
contribution cleanly --- its nine configurations include retrieval without
verification (fusion-only, Hit@5 0.94) and the same retrieval with the gate in
either role --- so the ranking-layer claim of RQ2 rests on a matched control.
The agent layer does not yet carry the corresponding control: its four arms
are three grounded lanes (retrieval \emph{and} verification) plus a bare agent
(neither), so they cannot separate the contribution of \emph{retrieval} from
that of \emph{verification} end to end. The missing arm --- a grounded lane
with the gate disabled (verdicts ignored, fusion order returned) --- is the
single most important next experiment, and its protocol is fixed here so it
cannot be tuned post hoc: same four questions, same pinned libraries, same
engine and turn budget, the gate's \texttt{enable\_judge} flag set false, and
the same citation and fact-marker checks. The released tool-layer logs already
contain the ranking-layer half of that comparison.
\textbf{Judge and engine dependence.} Scores and turn counts come from one
judging model and one agent engine; lanes, not models, are the object of
comparison, but absolute numbers will move with engine versions. The
deployed gate remains a single checkpoint, but the two strongest
verifier findings are no longer single-signal: the NLI replay
(Section~\ref{sec:robustness}) shows that a generic entailment model
fails too --- though \emph{differently}: it anti-separates the golds and
still rejects no out-of-corpus topic --- while a symbolic rule decides
the numeric case (Q6) that the shipped judge mishandles (the generic NLI
is actually the stronger model signal there, ranking the gold first), so
the paper's conclusions survive the choice of verification
signal better than the original submission could claim. What remains
open is trained evidence-verification models and learned-signal
ensembles: the naive span-max NLI probe and the rank-fusion ensemble
(Section~\ref{sec:robustness}) already answer the two easiest upgrades
negatively.
\textbf{Design surface.} Three lanes triple the policy surface an adopter
must test, and the reported evidence supports two distinct operating
points (Hybrid for latency, Librarian for exhaustive coverage), with the
Search lane as the latency-tuned interactive default between them; a
two-lane deployment is defensible today, and query-shape auto-selection is
future work.
\textbf{Consumer-side evidence.} The honesty contract is evaluated from the
system's side --- that the not-found reports are well formed and
non-fabricated --- not from the reader's side. Whether an operator acts on a
not-found report, escalates to a human, or ingests the recommended source is
not measured here, and this is the paper's main missing external-validity leg.
The natural study is a small operator trial over a fixed probe set with two
conditions (QDCVR not-found reports versus the same model's unaided answer),
scored on the downstream action the operator takes; we release the probe set
and transcripts so it can be run independently.
\textbf{Nondeterminism.} Agent runs are stochastic; we report two full
repetitions of the 16-cell grid (citation agreement 16/16; wall time and
turn counts fluctuate substantially), retry only zero-turn transport
flakes, and release all per-run JSON logs, including the timeout re-runs.
Fact-marker checks are automated substring matches; we additionally report
decontaminated checks computed after removing answer lines that name a
gold document, and the OOD probe answers were audited manually.
