\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Retrieval-augmented generation and ranking}
RAG couples a retriever with a generator to ground open-domain QA
\cite{rag-lewis}; dense retrievers encode queries and passages into a shared
space \cite{dpr} and multi-lingual, multi-granularity embedders such as
BGE-M3 support hybrid dense--sparse signals \cite{bge-m3}. Lexical
probabilistic ranking remains a strong baseline in many regimes \cite{bm25},
and reciprocal-rank fusion is a standard parameter-light way to merge ranked
lists \cite{rrf-cormack}. Surveys systematize the design space as
retrieve-then-read pipelines \cite{gao-ragsurvey} and as modular,
reconfigurable stacks \cite{modular-rag}; QDCVR is naturally read as a
modular-RAG instantiation whose added module is the verification gate.
Our tool-layer evaluation confirms this picture on
a curated industrial corpus: BM25 and RRF match or beat dense retrieval at
Hit@5, at millisecond latency. QDCVR uses dense, BM25, and graph signals, but
treats all of them as \emph{candidate generators} whose output must still
pass content verification.

\subsection{Structured and graph-based organization}
GraphRAG builds entity--relation graphs over corpora for query-focused
summarization \cite{graphrag}, LightRAG distills the idea into a simpler
dual-level retrieval \cite{lightrag}, and RAPTOR recursively abstracts
document trees \cite{raptor}. QDCVR's graph layer is intentionally leaner ---
Document, KnowledgeBase, and Tag nodes with \textsc{BelongsTo},
\textsc{HasTag}, and weighted \textsc{RelatedTo} edges --- because its graph
serves two engineering purposes (two-stage candidate expansion and
cross-library navigation) rather than global summarization. Our measurements
show the keyword-limited graph search is the weakest standalone signal on
this corpus (Hit@5 0.56), which is why the platform never lets it answer
alone. Industrial deployments in adjacent domains --- reliability-preserving
diagnosis for distributed drive systems \cite{rrpdg} and LLM-multi-agent
knowledge-graph QA for industrial models \cite{comaikg} --- share QDCVR's
concern that domain answers be inspectable; QDCVR differs by making the
inspection a standalone, fail-closed gate rather than a property of the
answering agent.

\subsection{Verification, correction, and abstention}
Self-RAG trains a generator to critique retrieved evidence and its own output
\cite{selfrag}; CRAG adds a corrective retriever that re-evaluates and
re-queries when retrieval quality is low \cite{crag}; FLARE interleaves
generation with forward-looking retrieval triggers \cite{flare}; Kamath et
al.\ formalize selective prediction under domain shift \cite{kamath}; and
CyberBOT applies ontology-grounded retrieval for reliability-critical
domains \cite{cyberbot}. Verifiability measurement in generative search
\cite{liu-verifiability} and citation-capable generation \cite{gao-citations}
frame the problem QDCVR attacks: citations that are present but not
supportive.

\emph{LLM-as-a-judge.} The verification gate is an LLM judge, and the
judging literature documents exactly the failure modes our measurements
expose. MT-Bench/Chatbot-Arena established the paradigm and its bias
taxonomy \cite{zheng-llmjudge}; LLM relevance judges agree imperfectly with
human relevance \cite{sun-urara}; listwise LLM re-rankers trade quality for
instability at scale \cite{rankgpt}; evaluator scores exhibit position and
verbosity biases \cite{zheng-llmjudge}, and evaluators favor generations
that resemble their own distribution \cite{panickssery-selfpref};
and verifiers can be gamed by optimization pressure \cite{lightman-verify}.
Our contribution is not the discovery that judges miscalibrate --- it is the
operational quantification of \emph{same-domain inflation} inside a deployed
verified-retrieval pipeline and the decomposition of which judge \emph{role}
(filter, ordering, or threshold) is safe under which pool conditions.

These approaches verify \emph{during generation} with the generator itself.
QDCVR instead externalizes verification into a dedicated judging model that
sees only (query, document-text) pairs and is consumed as a tool; any lane,
agent, or external system can call it, and its verdicts fail closed. This
makes verification an inspectable, reusable platform service rather than an
implicit property of one generator's prompting.

\emph{Abstention.} Beyond selective prediction under domain shift
\cite{kamath}, recent work probes whether LLMs know what they do not know
\cite{yin-knowwhat} and trains or orchestrates models to abstain on
knowledge gaps \cite{feng-abstain}. Section~\ref{sec:abstention} locates
QDCVR's abstention not in any of these calibrated-uncertainty mechanisms
but in the pipeline's reading-and-answer structure --- a complementary,
architectural answer to the same problem.

\subsection{Agentic retrieval and tool protocols}
ReAct established interleaved reasoning and acting over tools
\cite{react-yao}; tool-learning extended it to self-supervised tool use
\cite{toolformer}; the Model Context Protocol standardizes tool servers as a
machine interface \cite{mcp}; and recent surveys systematize agentic RAG
\cite{singh-agentic-rag}, listing efficiency among its open challenges.
DeepRead shows that structure-aware navigation over
long documents improves agentic search \cite{deepread}, which matches our
librarian lane's document-coordinate reading. QDCVR contributes an
agentic-retrieval \emph{policy} layer: per-lane tool whitelists enforced by
the server (a librarian-lane agent physically cannot call the vector tool),
hard call budgets, and single-call server-side orchestration for the hybrid
lane, targeting the turn count that our measurements (Section
\ref{sec:composition}) show dominates agentic latency.

\subsection{Positioning}
Table~\ref{tab:positioning} contrasts QDCVR with representative systems.
No prior system combines (i) content-verified citations as a platform-wide
gate, (ii) multiple interchangeable lanes with enforced tool budgets, and
(iii) fail-closed abstention, inside one self-hosted knowledge-base product
with a standard tool protocol. We also report where this design fails
(Section~\ref{sec:inflation}), which closed systems rarely do.
