\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) grounds large language models in
document stores, yet the evidence itself is rarely verified: a top-ranked
document can enter an answer without its content being checked against the
question. We present QDCVR, a self-hosted knowledge-base platform in which
every candidate citation must pass a verification gate --- a locally hosted
judge that reads the fetched document and scores whether it contains direct
evidence --- and three retrieval lanes terminating at this gate. On a
deployed industrial corpus (five knowledge bases, 42 documents, 16
gold-annotated questions) the judge exposes a same-domain score-inflation
failure consistent with known LLM-judge miscalibration: non-gold scores
(median 0.86, $n = 174$) are indistinguishable from gold (median 0.88,
$n = 17$; Mann--Whitney $p = 0.26$), and judged re-ranking lowers Hit@5
from its fusion stage's 0.94 to 0.44. Reusing the same verdicts as a binary
filter restores 0.94 (sign test $p = 0.008$); a leave-one-question-out
replay localizes the margin's boundary to a single question and shows the
smallest-margin rule transferring to 15 of 16 holdouts, with a small
engineering safety pad retaining all 16, and the failure and repair are
robust to question paraphrase. A generic document-head entailment signal
also fails to separate golds from confusers --- by anti-separation, its
gold scores sitting \emph{below} the non-golds --- and rejects no
out-of-corpus topic; a trivial symbolic rule decides the numeric case the
shipped judge misses. Out-of-corpus probes show
the threshold alone never abstains: honest not-found behavior lives in the
reading-and-answer layer. In a $4\times4$ agentic grid run twice, grounded
lanes named the gold document in all twelve first-round runs --- six
against pinned libraries of only one to two documents --- while a bare
agent never cited one. Verifier calibration is the open problem; honesty
needs an enforcement layer.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Enterprises accumulate process regulations, failure analyses, and operational
lessons as unstructured documents spread across shared drives. Answering a
concrete engineering question --- \emph{why did edge delamination appear in an
EL inspection of a laminated photovoltaic module?} --- requires locating the
one report among thousands that records the decision, reading it, and citing
it. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) addresses the location step by
embedding documents and the query into a shared vector space
\cite{rag-lewis,dpr}, and modern agentic stacks let a large language model
(LLM) orchestrate retrieval tools iteratively \cite{mcp,singh-agentic-rag}.
Yet the dominant ranking signal --- dense cosine similarity --- is a measure
of \emph{topic relatedness}, not of \emph{evidence presence}. A document that
merely discusses similar materials can outrank the one document that contains
the answer, and a fluent generator can readily synthesize an answer from the
wrong source. The result is the well-documented hallucination and
misgrounding behavior of LLM pipelines \cite{siren}.

This paper argues that a primitive most deployed RAG stacks leave implicit
is an \emph{explicit content-verification gate}: before a candidate document
is allowed to support an answer, its actual text must be fetched and judged
against the query by a model whose only job is to answer ``does this text
contain direct evidence for this question?'' Vectors are fast; only the
content can confirm. We built QDCVR (Query-Driven Content-Verified Retrieval), a
self-hosted platform that makes this primitive the backbone of a complete
knowledge-base system --- content-routed organization at ingestion, a
97-tool MCP server as the machine interface \cite{mcp}, and three retrieval
lanes that all terminate at the same verification gate --- and, because the
primitive is itself a model, subjects it to the same scrutiny as any model
(Section~\ref{sec:inflation}).

The three lanes embody a deliberate latency/recall trade-off. The
\emph{Search} lane is a vector fast lane: rewrite the query, retrieve a wide
candidate net, and submit citation-only references to the verification gate. The
\emph{Librarian} lane is a hierarchical navigator that walks
library$\to$document catalogs, routes enumeration-style queries to a machine
scanner, judges survivors in small batches, and reads only what survived ---
all within a hard budget of twelve tool calls. The \emph{Hybrid} lane
collapses vector and catalog channels into a single server-side orchestrated
call that merges candidates, judges them, applies a relative cut with a
top-5 floor, and returns full text for the top documents. All lanes fail
closed: zero verified evidence yields an explicit not-found report, never a
speculative answer.

We assess the platform with two experiment tiers on a deployed industrial
corpus of five knowledge bases spanning photovoltaic materials, module
failure analysis, plant O\&M, and discrete-manufacturing telemetry
(42 documents): a tool-layer
comparison of seven retrieval pipelines plus two derived configurations
on sixteen gold-annotated questions (plus a sixteen-question paraphrase
grid), and
16 end-to-end agentic runs that answer four questions through the platform's
public chat API in four arms (three lanes plus a bare agent without
knowledge-base tools), followed by a robustness tier: leave-one-out margin
generalization, a second verification signal replayed over the logged
candidate pools, and graded abstention probes. The contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A verifiable-retrieval architecture} (Sections
\ref{sec:system}--\ref{sec:lanes}): content-verified retrieval as a
platform primitive whose measured value lies in auditability, per-citation
provenance, and fail-closed semantics --- with three interchangeable lanes
under enforced budgets and an engine-agnostic harness layer that exposes
everything as MCP tools.
\item \textbf{A two-tier empirical analysis} (Sections
\ref{sec:setup}--\ref{sec:results}): at the tool layer, BM25 and reciprocal-rank
fusion are strong baselines on a curated same-domain corpus, and dense
retrieval misses exactly one question (Q16) through a near-duplicate
confuser family; the
verification gate's re-ranking \emph{reduces} top-5 hit rate from 0.94 to
0.44 when confusers
--- near-miss same-domain distractors --- dominate the candidate pool. At
the agent layer, all knowledge-base-grounded lanes ground their answers in
gold documents while end-to-end latency is almost entirely LLM turns
(machine retrieval is sub-second).
\item \textbf{A quantified verifier-failure analysis} (Section
\ref{sec:inflation}): an operational quantification, inside a deployed
verified-retrieval pipeline, of LLM-judge miscalibration --- whose
general causes (score compression, self-preference) are documented in the
judging literature \cite{zheng-llmjudge,panickssery-selfpref} but whose
pipeline-level consequences we decompose and measure: same-domain evidence
inflation (non-gold median 0.86 versus gold 0.88; Mann--Whitney $p = 0.26$,
i.e.\ no separation), a verdict role asymmetry (verdicts are safe as a
binary filter --- Hit@5 restored to 0.94, sign test $p = 0.008$ --- and
unsafe as an ordering), a threshold that never abstains out of corpus,
and robustness of both the failure and the repair to question paraphrase
and to the choice of verification signal.
Mitigations (hierarchical reading, relative cuts with floors, calibration)
are scoped to what this decomposition supports.
\item \textbf{Operational lessons from production use} (Section
\ref{sec:discussion}): three documented incidents, each with its detection
symptom and fix --- resident judge workers (batch judging 10--12\,s
$\to$ 1--2\,s), vector-index segment corruption surfacing as honest
``success with zero results'' failures, and GPU memory hygiene between
embedding and judging workloads.
\end{enumerate}

A preliminary demo describing the platform's organization and retrieval
layers was accepted as a CIKM~2026 demo \cite{qdcvr-demo}. This paper extends
it with three components that do not appear in the demo: (i)~the multi-lane
agentic retrieval methodology with enforced per-lane tool budgets; (ii)~the
verifier-failure analysis --- quantified same-domain inflation, the
filter/re-ranker role decomposition, and its configuration-level repair;
and (iii)~the two-tier empirical evaluation on the deployed corpus with all
per-run logs released.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Retrieval-augmented generation and ranking}
RAG couples a retriever with a generator to ground open-domain QA
\cite{rag-lewis}; dense retrievers encode queries and passages into a shared
space \cite{dpr} and multi-lingual, multi-granularity embedders such as
BGE-M3 support hybrid dense--sparse signals \cite{bge-m3}. Lexical
probabilistic ranking remains a strong baseline in many regimes \cite{bm25},
and reciprocal-rank fusion is a standard parameter-light way to merge ranked
lists \cite{rrf-cormack}. Surveys systematize the design space as
retrieve-then-read pipelines \cite{gao-ragsurvey} and as modular,
reconfigurable stacks \cite{modular-rag}; QDCVR is naturally read as a
modular-RAG instantiation whose added module is the verification gate.
Our tool-layer evaluation confirms this picture on
a curated industrial corpus: BM25 and RRF match or beat dense retrieval at
Hit@5, at millisecond latency. QDCVR uses dense, BM25, and graph signals, but
treats all of them as \emph{candidate generators} whose output must still
pass content verification.

\subsection{Structured and graph-based organization}
GraphRAG builds entity--relation graphs over corpora for query-focused
summarization \cite{graphrag}, LightRAG distills the idea into a simpler
dual-level retrieval \cite{lightrag}, and RAPTOR recursively abstracts
document trees \cite{raptor}. QDCVR's graph layer is intentionally leaner ---
Document, KnowledgeBase, and Tag nodes with \textsc{BelongsTo},
\textsc{HasTag}, and weighted \textsc{RelatedTo} edges --- because its graph
serves two engineering purposes (two-stage candidate expansion and
cross-library navigation) rather than global summarization. Our measurements
show the keyword-limited graph search is the weakest standalone signal on
this corpus (Hit@5 0.56), which is why the platform never lets it answer
alone. Industrial deployments in adjacent domains --- reliability-preserving
diagnosis for distributed drive systems \cite{rrpdg} and LLM-multi-agent
knowledge-graph QA for industrial models \cite{comaikg} --- share QDCVR's
concern that domain answers be inspectable; QDCVR differs by making the
inspection a standalone, fail-closed gate rather than a property of the
answering agent.

\subsection{Verification, correction, and abstention}
Self-RAG trains a generator to critique retrieved evidence and its own output
\cite{selfrag}; CRAG adds a corrective retriever that re-evaluates and
re-queries when retrieval quality is low \cite{crag}; FLARE interleaves
generation with forward-looking retrieval triggers \cite{flare}; Kamath et
al.\ formalize selective prediction under domain shift \cite{kamath}; and
CyberBOT applies ontology-grounded retrieval for reliability-critical
domains \cite{cyberbot}. Verifiability measurement in generative search
\cite{liu-verifiability} and citation-capable generation \cite{gao-citations}
frame the problem QDCVR attacks: citations that are present but not
supportive.

\emph{LLM-as-a-judge.} The verification gate is an LLM judge, and the
judging literature documents exactly the failure modes our measurements
expose. MT-Bench/Chatbot-Arena established the paradigm and its bias
taxonomy \cite{zheng-llmjudge}; LLM relevance judges agree imperfectly with
human relevance \cite{sun-urara}; listwise LLM re-rankers trade quality for
instability at scale \cite{rankgpt}; evaluator scores exhibit position and
verbosity biases \cite{zheng-llmjudge}, and evaluators favor generations
that resemble their own distribution \cite{panickssery-selfpref};
and verifiers can be gamed by optimization pressure \cite{lightman-verify}.
Our contribution is not the discovery that judges miscalibrate --- it is the
operational quantification of \emph{same-domain inflation} inside a deployed
verified-retrieval pipeline and the decomposition of which judge \emph{role}
(filter, ordering, or threshold) is safe under which pool conditions.

These approaches verify \emph{during generation} with the generator itself.
QDCVR instead externalizes verification into a dedicated judging model that
sees only (query, document-text) pairs and is consumed as a tool; any lane,
agent, or external system can call it, and its verdicts fail closed. This
makes verification an inspectable, reusable platform service rather than an
implicit property of one generator's prompting.

\emph{Abstention.} Beyond selective prediction under domain shift
\cite{kamath}, recent work probes whether LLMs know what they do not know
\cite{yin-knowwhat} and trains or orchestrates models to abstain on
knowledge gaps \cite{feng-abstain}. Section~\ref{sec:abstention} locates
QDCVR's abstention not in any of these calibrated-uncertainty mechanisms
but in the pipeline's reading-and-answer structure --- a complementary,
architectural answer to the same problem.

\subsection{Agentic retrieval and tool protocols}
ReAct established interleaved reasoning and acting over tools
\cite{react-yao}; tool-learning extended it to self-supervised tool use
\cite{toolformer}; the Model Context Protocol standardizes tool servers as a
machine interface \cite{mcp}; and recent surveys systematize agentic RAG
\cite{singh-agentic-rag}, listing efficiency among its open challenges.
DeepRead shows that structure-aware navigation over
long documents improves agentic search \cite{deepread}, which matches our
librarian lane's document-coordinate reading. QDCVR contributes an
agentic-retrieval \emph{policy} layer: per-lane tool whitelists enforced by
the server (a librarian-lane agent physically cannot call the vector tool),
hard call budgets, and single-call server-side orchestration for the hybrid
lane, targeting the turn count that our measurements (Section
\ref{sec:composition}) show dominates agentic latency.

\section{Platform architecture}
\label{sec:system}

\subsection{Design principles}
QDCVR (v2.3) is a self-hosted platform that turns a folder of PDFs, Office
documents, and scans into knowledge bases that agents can be trusted to
answer from. Five principles shape the architecture (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}):

\begin{description}
\item[P1 -- Content-routed organization.] Documents are organized by what
they \emph{say}, not by which folder they sat in: ingestion derives tags and
a five-field description from the extracted content, and retrieval starts
from a library catalog rather than a directory path.
\item[P2 -- MCP-first machine interface.] Every capability is exposed as a
tool of a Model Context Protocol server \cite{mcp}: 97 tools in eight groups
(search, graph, judging, experience, persona management (SOUL),
ingestion/\allowbreak parsing, library lifecycle, filesystem/\allowbreak tags). Web, HTTP, CLI, and MCP clients sit on
the same layer, so a capability added once is available to every harness.
\item[P3 -- Verification before promotion.] No similarity score, however
high, promotes a document into an answer; a judging model must read the
candidate's fetched content and vote. Verdicts fail closed: missing text,
engine errors, or timeouts reject the candidate rather than pass it.
\item[P4 -- Honest abstention.] When verification eliminates every
candidate, the system returns an explicit not-found report listing what was
searched and what was not covered. Refusing to answer is a terminal success
state, not an error path.
\item[P5 -- Engine-agnostic agency.] Answer agents are pluggable harnesses
(14 adapters, e.g.\ \texttt{omp}, Claude, ACP-compatible CLIs); the platform
constrains them through tool whitelists and prompts, never by coupling to a
particular vendor.
\end{description}

\subsection{Ingestion pipeline}
Ingestion is a nine-step, quality-gated flow executed by an archival
sub-agent through MCP tools: (1)~pre-flight tool probing; (2)~duplicate
detection (exact SHA-256 plus near-duplicate vector search at $\geq 0.90$
cosine); (3)~a library census; (4)~parsing with MinerU \cite{mineru} (local
OCR engine for scanned PDFs, direct reading for Markdown/text); (5)~a
semantic split gate for documents beyond 30\,000 characters (the agent
proposes split boundaries; a validator enforces a 0.85 target utilization
and forbids oversized atomic units); (6)~content-derived tagging and
description; (7)~multi-channel indexing (vector + graph + BM25); (8)~a
post-condition check that the new document is actually retrievable; and
(9)~an audited report. Two design choices matter in practice. First,
descriptions are generated by reading the content, and the retrieval layer
treats them as \emph{claims to be verified} rather than truth --- in an
audit of 26 document descriptions on a predecessor corpus, 24 were found to
mismatch their documents' actual content (released audit logs). Second,
indexing success is judged by observable effects (\texttt{total\_chunks}
$> 0$), because we observed GPU out-of-memory failures that returned
success envelopes with empty index payloads.

\subsection{Storage and service layers}
Four stores back the platform. A tree filesystem holds Markdown documents
with a metadata registry (library states, document paths, tags); per-library
ChromaDB collections index BGE-M3 embeddings \cite{bge-m3,chroma} (1024-d,
cosine space, 500-char chunks with 50-char overlap); Neo4j \cite{neo4j}
maintains the Document/KnowledgeBase/Tag graph with weighted
\textsc{RelatedTo} edges derived from shared tags, vector similarity, and
agent judgments; and a jieba-tokenized BM25 inverted index \cite{bm25}
supports lexical matching. Domain services wrap these stores: the vector
service serializes writes per collection (we traced one production incident
to concurrent HNSW segment handles), the two-stage service fuses BM25 and
graph expansion for candidate generation before a dense refine stage, the
experience service versions operational lessons as first-class documents
with their own retrieval threshold, and the verification gate is served by
a resident judge worker whose engineering details appear in
Section~\ref{sec:impl}.

\section{Multi-lane verified retrieval}
\label{sec:lanes}

All answering agents reach the platform through one chat endpoint. The
server-side \emph{lane policy} inspects the request (pinned libraries,
prompt suffixes, engine) and attaches the corresponding tool whitelist and
instructions; a librarian-lane agent, for example, is denied the vector and
two-stage tools at the protocol level, so the lane is a property of the
enforcement layer, not of prompt discipline. Fig.~\ref{fig:lanes} summarizes
the three lanes.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig2_lanes}
\caption{The three retrieval lanes. Every lane ends at the same
content-verification gate and the same honesty contract: zero verified
evidence escalates (Lane A$\to$B) or terminates in an explicit not-found
report.}
\label{fig:lanes}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Lane A: Search --- vector fast lane with a verification gate}
Lane A optimizes for latency on pinned libraries. Phase A0 rewrites the
query into (subject, attribute, constraints) form, splits multi-entity and
comparison questions into parallel sub-queries, and routes incident-style
queries to the experience store (Section~\ref{sec:system}) first. Phase A1
issues one wide dense query
(top-30, threshold 0.35, cross-library balancing), deduplicates by document,
and \emph{keeps all candidate documents rather than truncating to the top
chunks}. Phase A2 submits citation-only references (library id + document
path) to the verification gate in batches of at most six; the server fetches the
full text itself, so the agent never fabricates content when arguing for a
document. A document with vector score 0.95 that the judge rejects is
dropped; a document at 0.40 with one \emph{yes} verdict survives. Phase A3
returns the judge's evidence pack (the only full-content channel in this
lane) and the agent composes an answer whose citations carry confidence
levels (P0 direct evidence, P1 partial, P2 weak).

\subsection{Lane B: Librarian --- hierarchical navigation under a budget}
Lane B is for exhaustive recall across all libraries and for queries whose
vocabulary may not match the corpus. It walks a fixed spine with a hard
budget of twelve tool calls: L0 reads the library catalog (ids, names,
descriptions, counts) and prunes shelves (libraries) by the rewritten
query; L1 lists
each surviving shelf's documents with descriptions; L2 routes by query
shape --- literal/enumeration questions (``which documents mention X'') go
to a machine-side scanner ($\approx$0.5\,s per document, no LLM turns),
semantic questions go to judging; L3 judges in batches of at most six
references (larger batches historically triggered transport timeouts and
fail-closed batch loss); L4 reads only survivors (bounded to 2\,000
characters, starting at the judge-reported evidence line); L5 emits a
five-part report (search paths, answer, sources, confidence, blind spots).
Two rules are absolute: catalog descriptions never prune documents by
themselves, and an exhausted budget terminates in blind-spot disclosure,
not in a guessed answer.

\subsection{Lane C: Hybrid --- server-side orchestration in one call}
Agentic latency is dominated by LLM turns, not by tools (Section
\ref{sec:composition}); Lane C spends exactly one call by moving the entire
pipeline into the server. A single \texttt{kb\_hybrid\_search} call executes
P1 (a parallel block: vector channel, top-30 at threshold 0.35, and catalog
keyword channel); P3 merges candidates by (library, document) with
agreed-by-both
documents first, capped at twelve; P4 judges every candidate with the
verification gate in batches of six (criterion \emph{evidence}, threshold
0.5); P4b
applies a document-level relative cut --- keep scores $\geq \mathit{best} -
0.15$ with a top-5 floor, plus a cross-domain anchor guard that prevents an
inflated out-of-domain score from setting the cut line; and P5 reads the
top five documents in full ($\leq$2\,000 characters each). The response
carries the complete audit trail (per-lane candidates, merge counts, judge
statistics, cut decisions, read contents), so a one-call lane is still an
inspectable lane.

\subsection{The verification gate}
\label{sec:gate}
The gate is a locally hosted evidence-classification model (the Laya
judging service \cite{laya}) invoked through a
\texttt{kb\_laya\_judge} tool. Its contract: given the
query and a batch of document references, fetch each document server-side,
segment it, and score \emph{does this text contain direct evidence that
answers the query} under one of three criteria --- \emph{evidence}
(specific substantiation; mere topic match is insufficient),
\emph{instance} (contains at least one member of the requested class;
routed automatically for enumeration questions), or \emph{auto}. Scores
live in $[0,1]$ with an acceptance threshold of 0.5 (0.3 for enumeration).
All operating constants in this section --- thresholds, the six-reference
judge batch, the hybrid candidate cap of twelve, and the 0.15 relative-cut
margin with its top-5 floor --- are the platform's shipped production
defaults, fixed during earlier deployments on a different corpus, and were
not tuned on the evaluation questions of Section~\ref{sec:setup}. At the
protocol layer the platform documents scores as a 0--8 rubric (topic 0--3,
scenario 0--3, evidence 0--2; $\geq$6 answers directly, 5 escalates to the
librarian lane, $\leq$4 drops); the $[0,1]$ score is the machine-readable
projection of that rubric, and the rubric is the human-auditable contract
published to platform clients. The projection can saturate --- the logged
maximum score is 1.0000 --- one more reason thresholds carry no
discriminating power between golds and same-domain confusers. The judge
model itself is a locally
fine-tuned evidence classifier (checkpoint reference released with the
platform artifacts); it is deterministic in our replays --- repeat calls
with identical inputs reproduced identical orderings
(Section~\ref{sec:retrieval}) --- and it is a \emph{single} checkpoint:
every verifier finding in this paper inherits that choice, which
Section~\ref{sec:threats} treats as an explicit scope limit.
Fail-closed semantics apply to every
failure mode: fetch errors, engine errors, and timeouts count as
rejection. Survivors are aggregated into an evidence pack with
per-passage provenance.

\subsection{Honest abstention}
If a lane ends with zero verified documents, Lane A escalates once to Lane
B; if Lane B also exhausts its budget without a survivor, the answer is the
not-found report: what was searched (libraries, document counts), what the
query was routed as, and which topics sit outside the corpus. Because
abstention is a terminal state in the lane contract --- enforced by the
server-side policy rather than advised in a prompt --- it can be evaluated
like any other outcome, which Section~\ref{sec:agentic} does.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:impl}

The backend is Python 3.12/FastAPI; storage engines are ChromaDB (per-KB
HNSW \cite{hnsw} collections, cosine space) and Neo4j 5.20; embeddings are BGE-M3
\cite{bge-m3} (1024-d, normalized, batch 32, lazy-loaded singleton with an
LRU encode cache); parsing uses MinerU \cite{mineru} with a local OCR
engine; lexical indexing uses jieba tokenization \cite{jieba}. The MCP
server exposes 97 tools over SSE and stdio transports \cite{mcp}; the
judging model runs as a resident worker with a machine-level file lock, an
LRU verdict cache, and explicit CUDA-cache release between workloads. The
worker matters in production: a cold judge load costs 10--12\,s per batch,
the resident worker 1--2\,s; and when judge and bulk-embedding workloads
ran interleaved without cache cleanup, one end-to-end run degraded from 68\,s
to 27 minutes under the Windows WDDM display-page manager (internal
profiling; released operations log). The
web tier is Nuxt 3 with a chat endpoint that adapts 14 agent harnesses to a
uniform message stream; the lane policy whitelists 21 read-only MCP tools
per retrieval turn and denies 40+ harness-internal tools (file editors,
web search, sub-agents) which, when left enabled in our profiling, added
14--170\,s of detour behavior per run (per-run logs in the released
artifact). An experience subsystem versions operational
lessons as retrievable documents (higher threshold, 0.55) and supports a
rules-based rolling summary triggered every fifth ingestion. The full
system, including the experiment harness of Section~\ref{sec:setup}, is
open source.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/kingdol666/rag-knowledge}}

\subsection{Methods and arms}
\sloppypar
\emph{Tool layer} (Section~\ref{sec:retrieval}): seven methods answer ``which
documents back this question'' with top-5 document lists: BM25 (jieba+Okapi,
$k_1{=}1.5$, $b{=}0.75$, built over all 42 full texts), Dense
(\texttt{kb\_search\_vector}, BGE-M3, per-document best chunk), RRF (k=60
fusion of the two), Graph (\texttt{kb\_graph\_search} over top jieba
keywords, best-rank merge), Two-stage (\texttt{kb\_search\_two-stage}:
BM25+graph candidates then dense refine), Fusion-only (the hybrid lane's
merge order without judging), QDCVR-Hybrid (the full single-call lane
with judging and relative cut), and two \emph{derived} configurations
computed from the hybrid lane's logged candidate pools and per-candidate
judge scores --- \emph{judge-as-filter} (verdicts accept or reject; fusion
order preserved) and \emph{RRF-fuse} (reciprocal-rank fusion of the fusion
and judge orderings) --- which isolate whether the retrieval loss comes
from the verdicts or from the ordering they induce. (The replay re-issues
one QDCVR-Hybrid call per question with an identical configuration and
reads the pools and scores from its logged response; the judged orderings
reproduce the original runs exactly, indicating deterministic judging.
One merge-stage exception: Q14's replay merge produced twelve candidates
against the original run's eleven judged; all statistics use the original
logged pools.)
The seven pipeline methods
run over the full
42-document catalog with no library pinning; all output document-level
rankings (BM25 scores full texts; dense aggregates chunks by max-pooling),
so lists are directly comparable. Two construction asymmetries should be
kept in mind when reading the lexical results: BM25 is an evaluation-side
reimplementation over full texts (the platform's internal lexical channel
uses the same tokenizer with chunked documents), and its full-text
granularity plus the questions' corpus-vocabulary phrasing both favor
lexical matching. All platform methods are invoked exactly as
an external agent would invoke them: through the MCP tool interface against
the live services.

\emph{Agent layer} (Section~\ref{sec:agentic}): four arms answer four
questions (Q2, Q4, Q6, Q7) through the public chat API, non-streaming,
20-turn budget, 15-minute client timeout: \emph{Search} (library pinned,
Lane A policy), \emph{Librarian} (all libraries, Lane B policy),
\emph{Hybrid} (library pinned + Lane C suffix), and \emph{Bare} (no
knowledge-base tools, answer from parametric knowledge only). Note the
deliberate scope difference from the tool layer: pinned arms search a
single library (as small as two documents), so their confuser pools are a
fraction of the full catalog --- Section~\ref{sec:agentic} returns to this.
Every arm is
driven by the \texttt{omp} CLI agent engine\footnote{\url{https://github.com/can1357/oh-my-pi}}
(v18.4.10) wrapping the same
LLM (GLM-5.3-Flash,\footnote{Model card:
\url{https://open.bigmodel.cn}} default thinking level), so arms differ
only in retrieval affordance --- plus one honesty instruction appended to
the bare arm's prompt, disclosed verbatim in
Section~\ref{sec:agentic}. Zero-turn empty responses and transient
gateway timeouts are retried once (a known transport flake): across the
campaign the retry fired on two runs, both of which succeeded on re-run;
orphaned engine processes are cleaned between runs. The entire grid is run
twice (rounds 1 and 2); Section~\ref{sec:agentic} reports round 1 in its
tables and both rounds in the text.

\subsection{Abstention and calibration probes}
Beyond the gold questions, four out-of-corpus queries probe the honesty
contract at the tool layer, two of them also at the agent layer (across
all three grounded arms); and because every judged score is logged, the hybrid cut's relative margin is replayed offline across a
sweep of values (Section~\ref{sec:inflation}).

\subsection{Metrics}
Tool layer: Hit@5 (any gold document in top-5), Recall@5 (fraction of gold
documents in top-5), MRR@5, and per-call latency (mean and median; each
method warmed up once, first call discarded; BM25 is timed in-process
while platform methods additionally pay MCP transport, a sub-millisecond
cost at the reported scale). Agent layer: end-to-end wall
time, agent turns returned by the engine, gold-document citation hit ---
the answer text contains the gold document's unique filename fragment
(automated substring check; gold fragments were chosen to be unambiguous) ---
and fact-marker hits (automated substring check against 2--3
ground-truth values or terms per question). Fact markers are treated as
\emph{corroborating}, not primary, evidence: several markers overlap the
question's own wording, so marker hits alone cannot establish grounding
(the citation check is primary; Section~\ref{sec:agentic} additionally
decontaminates marker matching by deleting gold-citing lines). Refusals
are detected when a hedging marker appears in the answer's opening 200
characters; on the six out-of-corpus agent probes this rule agrees with
the manual transcript audit on five and misses one explicit not-found
report, so refusal counts reported here are the manually audited ones. All checks
are automated string matches against the released transcripts; no human
rater was involved. For the verifier analysis we log per-candidate judge
scores from the hybrid response. Hardware: one RTX 5060 (8\,GB) serves
embeddings and the judge worker; all services run locally on Windows.
Per-question best ranks for all nine retrieval configurations are
tabulated in \ref{app:perq}.

Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} and Fig.~\ref{fig:retrieval} give the tool-layer
results. With sixteen questions the Wilson 95\% intervals on the headline
Hit@5 proportions are wide (judged hybrid $[0.23, 0.67]$; judge-as-filter
$[0.72, 0.99]$; BM25/RRF $[0.81, 1.00]$) --- the qualitative decomposition,
not the point estimates, is the claim. Three observations matter for
practitioners.

\textbf{Curated same-domain corpora favor lexical precision.} With 42
well-described documents and questions authored in the corpus's vocabulary,
BM25 achieves Hit@5 1.00 (MRR 0.958) at millisecond latency, RRF matches it
(MRR 0.927), and two-stage fusion joins them at Hit@5 1.00 once the
corpus is healthy. Dense retrieval misses exactly one question (Q16): the
query asks about the \emph{heatdecay092 post-compensation verification}
while four near-duplicate sibling reports from the same incident family
crowd the top-5 --- a confuser-density failure, not a vocabulary one (the
earlier 8-question round additionally exposed a synonym miss, Q5's
\emph{drop} vs.\ \emph{plunge}, which fusion repairs). Graph search,
limited to \textsc{contains} matching on node names and tags, is the
weakest standalone signal (0.56).

\textbf{Verification is cheap; re-ranking is the risk.} Judging twelve
candidates adds well under a second with the resident worker. But the
QDCVR-Hybrid arm loses top-5 hits relative to its own fusion stage
(0.44 vs.\ 0.94 Hit@5). The failure decomposes cleanly: on eight questions
(Q2, Q4, Q5, Q6, Q8, Q9, Q11, Q12) the gold document \emph{was} judged and
the gate's verdicts reordered same-domain confusers above it; on Q16 the
gold never reached the judge at all --- all twelve candidate slots were
taken by confusers upstream in the fusion stage. Keeping the verdicts but
discarding the induced ordering --- the \emph{judge-as-filter} rows of
Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} --- repairs every one of the eight losses and
regresses none (8 wins, 0 losses, 8 ties on Hit@5; exact binomial
$p = 0.008$, two-sided), restoring Hit@5 to 0.94 and MRR to 0.856 with
every returned citation still judge-verified; the only ranking regression
anywhere is Q7, where the gold slips from third to fifth. Said plainly:
judge-as-filter returns \emph{exactly the fusion-only ranking} --- the
per-question orderings are identical on all sixteen questions --- so at
the tool layer the gate's measured contribution is the audit metadata
attached to each citation, bought at the hybrid call's $\approx$480\,ms
surcharge over fusion-only. We therefore recommend deployments consume
verdicts as a filter rather than an ordering; the change is a single
configuration value, and migrating the shipped default is tracked in the
roadmap (Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}). Both halves
trace to the same root cause, quantified next.

\textbf{Two-stage fusion recovers its inputs here.} On the healthy corpus
the BM25+graph-then-dense pipeline reaches Hit@5 1.00 (Recall@5 0.97, one gold of a two-gold question ranked
outside the top-5), confirming that its added stages
 neither add nor subtract on this corpus once the stores are intact;
per-question results are in the released logs.

Table~\ref{tab:agentic} and Fig.~\ref{fig:e2e} summarize the round-1 runs.
We present them as an end-to-end case study of the deployed system rather
than a statistical comparison; full per-run JSON transcripts are released.

\textbf{Grounding is a property of the lanes, not of the model.} All three
knowledge-base-grounded arms named the gold document in all twelve valid
runs and reproduced all twelve available fact-marker sets --- including the
exact watch-clause numbers (31.35\,g, $\pm$0.05\,g, 5\,min) and the
telemetry root cause (a dead thermocouple loop, ``TC0''). The marker hits
survive decontamination: after deleting every answer line that names a
gold document (so markers must come from the answer's own prose or
tables), all twelve grounded runs still reproduce their full marker sets.
Six of the twelve runs answer from pinned libraries of only one or two
documents (Q2's failure-analysis library holds two; Q6's watch-clause
library one), where hitting the gold is a low bar; the substantive
evidence is the six runs over Q4 and Q7's 31-document process library.
The bare arm's prompt adds one instruction --- ``answer using only your
own knowledge; if you are uncertain, say so explicitly'' --- mirroring a
deployment without retrieval; grounded arms receive no honesty
instruction beyond their lane prompts. Even so, the bare arm
never cited a source: on the EL question it produced a plausible
\emph{textbook} diagnosis (interfacial adhesion failure, moisture ingress)
that misses the corpus's recorded root cause --- lamination-machine
thermocouple drift leaving EVA under-cured, the mechanism Q2's fact
markers encode --- and on the telemetry question it hit none of the fact
markers (it did refuse explicitly in two of four runs, honest about its
ignorance but holding no corpus facts either way).
This is the honesty contract of Section~\ref{sec:lanes} observed end to
end: without verification-grounded retrieval, the same engine produces
fluent, unsourced, and in these cases partly wrong answers.

\textbf{Reconciling the tool layer and the agent layer.} At the tool layer,
the judged hybrid missed nine of sixteen questions; here the hybrid arm hit
the gold on all four. Two design differences explain this, and both are
part of the lane contract rather than luck. First, pinned scope: the agent
arm searches one library (Q2's failure-analysis library holds two
documents), so the same-domain confusers that flood the 42-document
tool-layer catalog never enter its candidate pool. Second, agency: a lane
that can read what it retrieved and issue follow-up calls is not bound by a
single ranking --- the librarian lane in particular is built to keep
looking. The tool layer measures the ranking stage in isolation; the agent
layer measures the lane as deployed. The reconciliation yields an
explicit, testable model of what the gate buys. (i)~Verdicts as a binary
\emph{filter} are safe exactly when judge recall on gold documents is high
(here 17/17 in-pool), and they add per-citation audit; discrimination is
irrelevant. (ii)~Verdicts as an \emph{ordering} are safe only when
pairwise gold-versus-confuser score separation exceeds the base ranker's,
which same-domain inflation destroys. (iii)~\emph{Threshold abstention}
requires out-of-corpus calibration the acceptance threshold does not have.
Verification, in this deployment, buys auditability and honest termination
--- not ranked-list quality (the filter is order-preserving; the re-ranker
actively harms it) and not pool composition.

\textbf{Partial honesty counts.} The one refusal-flagged grounded run,
hybrid-Q4, is a positive case rather than a failure: its answer cites the
POE gold document correctly and states explicitly that the library holds no
EVA-side comparison data --- a scoped, honest disclosure instead of
speculation. No grounded run fabricated a citation.

\textbf{The lanes buy their latency profiles with turn structure.} Hybrid
completed every round-1 question in 3--5 turns (mean 3.8) and 36--70\,s;
librarian
spent 5--6 turns (mean 5.5) and 54--91\,s; search sat between on wall time
but needed 8 turns on the hardest question (Q7, the telemetry cluster).
The librarian's extra turns buy exhaustive coverage with blind-spot
disclosure --- the arm that must not miss a document, not the arm that
must be fast.

\textbf{Repetition: outcomes reproduce, latency does not.} The full grid
was run a second time. Citation outcomes agree on all 16 of 16 cells ---
trivially for the four bare cells (no citations in either round), so the
informative agreement is 12/12 grounded cells; the decontaminated
fact-marker check is a clean sweep in round 1 (12/12) and near-clean in
round 2 (11/12; the exception is hybrid-Q2, whose 61/85 values appear only
inside evidence lines that also cite the gold document --- an artifact of
the line-deletion rule rather than a grounding failure; its citation hit
stands). Decontamination is applied to the answer's first 600 characters,
where every matched marker occurs, so the check is conservative. Round-2
turn means track the load (hybrid 5.0, search 7.25, librarian 7.75) while
every citation outcome repeats.
Wall time and turn counts fluctuate substantially
(e.g.\ search-Q2: 70\,s/6 turns in round 1 versus 165\,s/11 turns in
round 2; librarian-Q4: 84\,s/6 turns versus 92\,s/11 turns; hybrid-Q2:
62\,s/4 turns versus 60\,s/3 turns). The dichotomy --- stable grounding
under unstable latency --- is exactly what the turn-economics model of
Section~\ref{sec:composition} predicts, and it is the strongest argument
for the platform's fixed turn budgets.

\textbf{The fast lane has a real tail.} In the first campaign pass, the
search arm (Q2) and the bare arm (Q4) hit the server's 600\,s turn budget
and returned gateway timeouts; both re-runs completed normally (70\,s and
99\,s). We attribute the stalls to engine-side transport stalls under LLM
provider load, not to the retrieval layer (whose machine-side latency is
sub-second); the platform's retry-once policy (Section~\ref{sec:setup})
fired on exactly these two runs. Interactive deployments should treat the
server turn budget as the operative tail-latency knob.

\subsection{The verifier's own failure mode: same-domain inflation}
\label{sec:inflation}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.5\textwidth]{fig4_inflation}
\caption{Evidence-criterion judge scores from the sixteen QDCVR-Hybrid tool
runs: all 191 judged candidates. Gold documents (green, $n = 17$; Q16's
gold never entered its candidate pool) and same-domain non-gold survivors
(orange, $n = 174$) overlap almost completely: medians 0.876 versus 0.863,
Mann--Whitney $p = 0.26$. The 0.5 acceptance threshold separates both
groups from zero, not from each other.}
\label{fig:inflation}
\end{figure}

Fig.~\ref{fig:inflation} plots every judge score from the sixteen hybrid
runs: 191 candidates (twelve per run except Q14, whose merge produced
eleven). Not one candidate was rejected --- every logged score is
$\geq 0.589$, far above the 0.5 acceptance threshold, so at the threshold
layer the gate's filtering power on this corpus is zero. The failures
concentrate in ranking, not acceptance. Aggregating the 17 in-pool gold
instances (Q1 and Q5 carry two golds each; Q16's gold was never judged):
only 5 of 17 rank in the top-3 of the judged ordering, while 9 rank eighth
or lower. Gold and non-gold score distributions are statistically
indistinguishable at this sample size (medians 0.876 vs.\ 0.863;
two-sided Mann--Whitney with tie correction, $U = 1726$, $p = 0.26$, a
gold-favoring trend with Cliff's
$\delta \approx 0.17$; scores are nested within 16 runs, so we treat the
test as indicative only --- and the sign test of
Section~\ref{sec:retrieval} is likewise post-hoc and descriptive, one of
nine configurations evaluated on the same sixteen questions). On Q6 (the 31.35\,g$\pm$0.05\,g watch
clause), the fusion ranking placed the gold clause document first, but the
judged re-ranking promoted five unrelated telemetry reports (0.92--0.93)
while the gold document scored 0.82 --- pushing the gold document out of
the top-5 that the answer agent reads in full; the same pattern recurs on
Q2 (Fig.~\ref{fig:anatomy}), where five extruder-telemetry reports and an
unrelated PET process document (0.887--0.926) outrank the gold EL report
(0.846). Even the relative cut participates in the failure: it
bound in most rounds (trimming one to five tail candidates), and on Q11
its best-minus-0.15 line fell \emph{above} the gold's score (0.741 vs.\
0.711), deleting the gold from the kept set outright.

\textbf{An offline replay turns this failure into a calibration result.}
Because every judged score is logged, the cut margin can be replayed
without re-running the pipeline. Sweeping the margin from 0.05 to 0.35
(with the top-5 floor unchanged): at 0.05 the kept set shrinks to a mean
6.4 candidates but retains only 8/17 golds; the shipped 0.15 margin keeps
a mean of 10.4 candidates and 16/17 golds; at 0.20 all 17 golds survive
with 11.1 candidates retained. Because the replay selects and evaluates
the margin on the same sixteen questions that motivated it, 0.20 is an
in-sample observation, not a validated setting; deployment-time
calibration must re-derive it per library on fresh questions. The margin
is therefore repairable by a
small configuration change, but no margin separates golds from
confusers, because the scores themselves overlap.

\textbf{Cross-domain claims do not survive measurement either.} The
design intent --- that reading content separates topical neighbors from
evidence --- does deliver cross-domain filtering \emph{in the answer
layer} (Section~\ref{sec:abstention}), but the acceptance threshold
carries none of it. On four
out-of-corpus probe queries (lithium-battery thermal runaway, child
vaccination schedules, Python concurrency, injection-mold vent design),
dense retrieval still returns same-corpus documents at 0.40--0.60 cosine,
and the judged hybrid keeps 9--11 of its 12 candidates --- the same
inflation, now cross-domain. (One early production incident in which an
out-of-domain document scored 0.92 on a PV question motivated the anchor
guard; the probes show such inflation is the norm, not the exception.)
What the probes establish is where honesty actually lives: not in the
threshold, but in the reading-and-answer layer that the lanes wrap around
it.

This failure is the mirror image of the one the gate was built to stop:
dense similarity over-trusts topical neighbors, and the judge --- a model
used for evidence discrimination --- over-trusts domain-internal
discourse. The lane design contains two partial mitigations, and their
limits are exactly what this analysis exposes. Lane~B's mandatory reading
of survivors surfaces mismatched content to the answering agent; and the
hybrid cut's relative margin with a top-5 floor keeps most outranked gold
documents in the machine-readable audit trail --- though, as Q11 shows,
it can also delete one. The quantified repair is cut-margin calibration
per library (the replay above); the open problem is the score overlap
itself: domain-aware negative training, score normalization against a
per-library null distribution, or pairing the judge with a contradiction
check. We report this as a measured open problem rather than a solved
one.

\subsection{Honest abstention under out-of-corpus probes}
\label{sec:abstention}

The lanes' final contract --- an explicit not-found instead of speculation
--- was never exercised by the gold questions, all of which are in-corpus.
Four out-of-corpus probe queries (lithium-battery thermal runaway, child
vaccination schedules, Python concurrency, injection-mold vent design)
close that gap at both layers (Fig.~\ref{fig:ood}). At the tool layer,
abstention does
\emph{not} happen: as Section~\ref{sec:inflation} showed, the acceptance
threshold keeps 9--11 of 12 candidates even for vaccination or battery
queries. At the agent layer, the contract holds without exception: all six
runs (three grounded arms $\times$ two probes) answered with an explicit
not-found report --- each enumerates the libraries searched, states that
the corpus does not cover the topic, and declines to answer from memory;
none cites a document as evidence (zero \texttt{.md} citations across all
six transcripts). Three runs add a dedicated blind-spot section, two disclosing search
coverage limits: one
librarian run reports that its vector and experience channels were
unavailable that round, completed only a catalog-level scan, and therefore
``cannot claim exhaustive coverage,''
and several runs recommend ingestible sources (e.g.\ the relevant national
standards) instead of fabricating a procedure. The honesty contract is
thus real but its enforcement point is the reading-and-answer layer ---
precisely why the platform wraps verification inside lanes that must read
before they answer, rather than exposing a bare similarity threshold.

\subsection{Where the time goes: LLM turns, not retrieval}
\label{sec:composition}

Across the agentic runs, machine-side retrieval is uniformly sub-second
(Table~\ref{tab:retrieval}) while the twelve grounded end-to-end runs take 36--91\,s: wall
time is dominated by LLM turns, at 11.7--15.3\,s per turn across arms (per-arm
means: search 11.7, librarian 12.8, hybrid 15.3; computed over the twelve
grounded runs), consistent with the observation that agentic-RAG latency
is turn-dominated \cite{singh-agentic-rag}.
The lanes differ almost entirely in turn structure: Lane~C compresses its
pipeline into one tool call; Lane~B spends turns on catalog navigation but
buys exhaustive coverage and readable provenance; Lane~A sits between. The
practical consequence is an engineering rule the platform now follows
everywhere: \emph{move orchestration server-side and cap turns} --- the
single-call hybrid lane is both the fastest and the most auditable, because
its audit trail ships with the one response.

\textbf{Margin.} The leave-one-question-out replay
(\texttt{exp6\_loo\_margin.py}) addresses the in-sample concern directly:
selection and evaluation are separated, the selected margins are stable
(0.18 on fifteen of sixteen folds), and the single holdout failure is the
already-known Q11 boundary. The deployed default adds a small engineering
safety pad on top of the LOO-selected value; the pad's magnitude is a
judgment call, and the Q11 fold is the warning that justifies it --- a
calibration made without a Q11-like question in its training set would
pick 0.15 and lose that gold.

\textbf{Paraphrase.} The paraphrase grid (\texttt{exp6\_paraphrase.py})
confirms the vocabulary-dependence concern about BM25's ceiling --- its
1.00 is partly a vocabulary artifact, dropping to 0.88 with MRR 0.755
under rewording --- and sharpens the practical picture: dense retrieval is
the paraphrase-robust backbone (0.94 unchanged), and both the judged
hybrid's failure (0.50) and the filter repair (0.94, again identical to
fusion-only) survive rewording. Pooled over all 32 question variants the
filter-versus-judged record is 15 wins, 0 losses, 17 ties (exact binomial
$p < 0.0001$). Two honesty notes: the paraphrases are author-written, and
one of them (Q7) strips every discriminative token --- all nine
configurations miss it, so that cell measures a rewording no retriever
could follow rather than lexical fragility; and dense's unchanged rate
hides a composition swap (it loses reworded Q7 and gains Q16). The
verification failure is a property of
the judge and the pool, not of the question phrasing.

\textbf{Second signal.} The NLI replay (\texttt{exp7\_second\_signal.py})
runs the same 191 logged (query, candidate) pairs and four out-of-corpus
topics through a generic Chinese NLI model --- one 330\,M checkpoint, one
question-as-context template, premises truncated to roughly the first 500
post-tokenization characters (59 of 191 pairs hit the cap). The result
refutes the obvious remedy, and not by replicating the judge's failure:
generic entailment ranks these candidates \emph{worse} than the judge
(Hit@5 0.31, MRR 0.138; Spearman $\rho = -0.13$), and it
\emph{anti-separates} --- gold scores sit \emph{below} non-gold scores
(medians 0.011 vs.\ 0.053, Cliff's $\delta = 0.23$, $p = 0.12$) --- while
its out-of-corpus behavior matches the judge's: corpus documents score up
to 0.997 against unrelated topics, so an NLI threshold would abstain on
nothing either. Truncation is not the explanation: six of the ten gold
documents that fit the context window entirely still score below 0.01,
and on the one question whose verdict is verbatim in the visible head
(Q7), the NLI
score is 0.011. Document-granularity evidence verification is hard for
both a fine-tuned judge and a generic NLI model (span-level
retrieval-then-verify and judge ensembles remain open), and threshold
abstention fails for both. The symbolic check tells the complementary
story: on the numeric watch clause (Q6) the generic NLI is actually the
stronger signal --- it ranks the gold first of twelve, where the shipped
judge ranks it tenth --- and a trivial substring rule decides the same
case with perfect separation (gold 1/1, confusers 0/11). Verification
signals fail in different ways and succeed in different places;
verification-primitive choice is therefore a genuinely open engineering
problem, and the answer layer's
honesty contract --- which depends on reading, not on the signal ---
becomes more, not less, important.

\textbf{Graded probes.} Six fresh probes (three partial-coverage, three
absent) extend the abstention analysis beyond the single partial-coverage
episode of the original OOD probes. The tool layer behaves identically in
every tier --- 10--12 of 12
candidates kept at scores up to 0.983 --- so the threshold's blindness is
universal, not question-specific. At the agent layer the partial probes
produced the behavior the honesty contract promises: the
acetic-acid-and-aluminum-frame probe answered with the corpus's recorded
evidence (acetic acid corroding copper ribbons), labeled the aluminum
frame question explicitly as unevidenced, and graded its own extension as
P1/P2 inference rather than fact --- a scoped, auditable disclosure. All
three absent probes, run through the same agent arm, returned explicit
not-found reports enumerating the libraries searched (48--114\,s). One
probe we had labeled partial turned out to be covered: the corpus contains
a verified secondary-crosslink rework case, which our tier labels ---
author estimates made before running --- underestimated. We release the
tier labels and transcripts with that correction noted.

\section{Discussion and lessons learned}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{L1. Vectors are fast; content is accurate --- and verification has
two costs.} The tool-layer table is the cleanest statement of the retrieval
subproblem the platform must address: on a curated corpus a millisecond
BM25 beats an 18\,ms dense retriever, and neither knows whether a document
\emph{answers} the question. The verification gate converts ranked
candidates into accountable, per-citation-provenance evidence; its costs
are both temporal ($\approx$0.5\,s per dozen candidates end-to-end in
Table~\ref{tab:retrieval}; 1--2\,s per six-document batch for the
resident worker in production load) and, on this corpus, an accuracy one
(0.94$\to$0.44 Hit@5 under same-domain inflation,
Section~\ref{sec:inflation}). Auditability is what the gate buys today;
ranking quality is what calibration must earn back.

\textbf{L2. Latency engineering is turn engineering.} With retrieval
sub-second, the only lever that matters for interactive latency is the
number of agent turns. Tool whitelists that remove detour tools, one-call
server-side pipelines, and small judge batches that avoid transport
timeouts together took our hybrid runs from $\approx$29 turns in the
earliest serial-skill configuration (historical runs, logs released) to
3--5 turns in the present measurements.


\textbf{L3. The verifier is a model too --- calibrate it.}
Section~\ref{sec:inflation} is the paper's cautionary result: a verifier
inherits domain-internal blind spots, and a threshold calibrated on
cross-domain negatives (0.5) does not separate same-domain near-misses.
Fail-closed semantics and reading requirements contain the damage; they do
not replace calibration.

\textbf{L4. Honesty needs an enforcement layer, not a prompt.} Lane
separation works because the server physically withholds tools: a
librarian-lane agent cannot call the vector tool even if instructed to.
The out-of-corpus probes (Section~\ref{sec:abstention}) show the same
asymmetry for abstention: a threshold cannot refuse --- it accepted every
out-of-corpus candidate it was shown --- while the reading-and-answer
layer refused all six probe prompts with disclosed searches and zero
fabricated citations. Honesty is a property of the pipeline's shape, not
of the model's mood. The dichotomy is about reliability, not exclusivity:
the bare agent did refuse explicitly in two of its four runs --- honest
about its ignorance, but holding no corpus facts either way --- while in
the others it produced confident, unsourced, partly wrong answers whose
wrongness no audit trail exposes. Unassisted honesty is a coin flip;
enforced honesty is inspectable.

\textbf{L5. Production incidents taught honest observability.} Three
incidents shaped the implementation: a corrupted HNSW segment that made one
library silently un-retrievable across lanes (fixed by re-index; detected
because ``success with zero results'' is itself treated as suspect);
GPU-OOM indexing that returned success envelopes with empty vector payloads
(success is now judged by chunk counts); and judge/engine failures that
must reject candidates rather than pass them. The common thread: every
layer reports what it actually verified, and every consumer treats
unverifiable as failed. Table~\ref{tab:failures} consolidates the incident
log into the failure-mode catalogue we wish we had on day one.

\subsection{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}
\textbf{Scale.} The live corpus is 42 documents across five libraries;
absolute Hit@5 values (e.g.\ BM25's 1.00) will not transfer to corpora
orders of magnitude larger, where the hybrid lane's candidate cap and the
librarian's shelf pruning become load-bearing rather than optional. The
benchmark suite shipped with the platform scales the corpus to hundreds of
scientific documents for exactly this purpose.
\textbf{Question set.} Sixteen tool-layer and four agent-layer questions
were authored by the system's developers; all gold documents and fact
markers are released for independent scrutiny, but the sample is small and
favors the corpus's vocabulary. The paraphrase grid quantifies exactly
that exposure --- BM25's ceiling drops to 0.88 under rewording while the
dense backbone and the verifier findings hold --- and a larger
author-independent question set remains the main scaling need. One
circularity is worth naming rather than silently benefiting from: Q16's
question embeds its gold document's filename token (heatdecay092), which
guarantees lexical success on that question; the paraphrase grid keeps
the token, so the robustness tier does not break this specific shortcut.
\textbf{Judge and engine dependence.} Scores and turn counts come from one
judging model and one agent engine; lanes, not models, are the object of
comparison, but absolute numbers will move with engine versions. The
deployed gate remains a single checkpoint, but the two strongest
verifier findings are no longer single-signal: the NLI replay
(Section~\ref{sec:robustness}) shows that a generic entailment model
fails too --- though \emph{differently}: it anti-separates the golds and
still rejects no out-of-corpus topic --- while a symbolic rule decides
the numeric case (Q6) that the shipped judge mishandles (the generic NLI
is actually the stronger model signal there, ranking the gold first), so
the paper's conclusions survive the choice of verification
signal better than the original submission could claim. What remains
open is span-level evidence verification (our NLI configuration reads
document heads) and judge ensembles.
\textbf{Design surface.} Three lanes triple the policy surface an adopter
must test, and the reported evidence supports two distinct operating
points (Hybrid for latency, Librarian for exhaustive coverage), with the
Search lane as the latency-tuned interactive default between them; a
two-lane deployment is defensible today, and query-shape auto-selection is
future work.
\textbf{Nondeterminism.} Agent runs are stochastic; we report two full
repetitions of the 16-cell grid (citation agreement 16/16; wall time and
turn counts fluctuate substantially), retry only zero-turn transport
flakes, and release all per-run JSON logs, including the timeout re-runs.
Fact-marker checks are automated substring matches; we additionally report
decontaminated checks computed after removing answer lines that name a
gold document, and the OOD probe answers were audited manually.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

QDCVR turns ``the retriever found it'' into ``the system verified it'':
a platform where organization, retrieval, and agency all terminate at an
explicit content-verification gate, where three interchangeable lanes trade
latency for recall under enforced budgets, and where the system's refusal
to speculate is a first-class outcome. Our experiments on the deployed
industrial corpus ground the design in measured behavior: lexical and fused
retrieval remain strong tool-layer baselines; the verification gate costs
well under a second but inherits its own same-domain blind spot, which we
quantify and partially repair by cut-margin replay; the honesty contract
refuses every out-of-corpus probe at the answer layer even though the
threshold refuses nothing; and end-to-end latency belongs to LLM turns, so
the fastest lane is the one that moved its orchestration server-side. The immediate roadmap
is verifier calibration against per-library null distributions; span-level
evidence verification and judge ensembles (the generic-NLI replay already
shows document-head entailment is not the answer); combining two-stage
candidates with the verdict-as-filter role that the tool layer suggests;
larger-corpus evaluation with the shipped benchmark suite and an
author-independent question set; consumer-side evidence that
not-found reports serve their readers; and lane auto-selection from
query-shape features --- steps toward retrieval systems whose trustworthiness
is an inspectable property of the pipeline rather than a hope about the
model.
