Abstract

Retrieval over evolving document collections depends on where documents are stored, how they are described and whether their
indices reflect changes. HMRS manages collections, documents and segments and exposes their state through catalogue, shelf,
document and segment views. A November inventory found at least one stored part for each of 100 target papers. A separate
lifecycle check retrieved a renamed document under its new name using a cache-bypassing query.

   In the November 17 evaluation on 72 questions across ten domains, the scripted selector reached the labelled collection for 72
questions with full route cards and 49 with nominally name-only cards. This contrast does not isolate the effect of descriptions.
Combining document and vector chunk windows increased target-string containment from 36 to 48 questions. The combined
configuration recorded a mean of 298.9 kB of text per query, versus 33.4 kB for the baseline, while mean retrieval tool time was
15.0 versus 16.6 s. Containment measures text access, not semantic answer support. A separate November 18 API fixture recorded
a keyword response naming the test document and a catalogue entry for its collection; the vector assertion did not establish a
document hit. These findings distinguish three checks for managed retrieval: verify what is stored and indexed, measure what
evidence reaches the pack, and report incomplete search separately from absence of evidence.

Keywords: Knowledge document management, Document classification, Hierarchical organisation, Tiered retrieval, Evidence
verification, Model Context Protocol
