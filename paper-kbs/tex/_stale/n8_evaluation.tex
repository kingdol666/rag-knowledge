\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Corpus and gold questions}
The live catalog contains five deployed knowledge bases from an industrial
setting spanning photovoltaic materials, module failure analysis, plant
O\&M, and discrete-manufacturing telemetry: thin-film process regulations
(3 documents), a
PV-module failure-analysis library (2), a PV-plant O\&M library (5), an
industrial workshop library with 31 documents including 20 near-duplicate
equipment-telemetry reports, and a one-document production watch-clause
library --- 42 documents in total. The telemetry cluster is a deliberate
stressor: its documents share prefixes, domains, and vocabulary, mimicking
the confuser density of real deployments. We authored sixteen domain
questions (Table~\ref{tab:questions}), each with one or two gold documents
identified by unique filename fragments, and 1--3 fact markers (numbers or
terms any grounded answer must contain, e.g.\ the 61--68\% vs.\ $\geq$85\%
cure-degree contrast for the EL question (Q2), and the ``TC0'' dead-loop
verdict for the telemetry question (Q7)).
Two corpus-integrity incidents surfaced while setting up the evaluation and
were repaired through the platform's own remediation path before any
measurement: one document missing from its vector collection (re-indexed
via \texttt{index-document}) and one library whose collection had been
silently lost (rebuilt via \texttt{reindex}); both incidents are instances
of the failure class discussed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection{Methods and arms}
\sloppypar
\emph{Tool layer} (Section~\ref{sec:retrieval}): seven methods answer ``which
documents back this question'' with top-5 document lists: BM25 (jieba+Okapi,
$k_1{=}1.5$, $b{=}0.75$, built over all 42 full texts), Dense
(\texttt{kb\_search\_vector}, BGE-M3, per-document best chunk), RRF (k=60
fusion of the two), Graph (\texttt{kb\_graph\_search} over top jieba
keywords, best-rank merge), Two-stage (\texttt{kb\_search\_two-stage}:
BM25+graph candidates then dense refine), Fusion-only (the hybrid lane's
merge order without judging), QDCVR-Hybrid (the full single-call lane
with judging and relative cut), and two \emph{derived} configurations
computed from the hybrid lane's logged candidate pools and per-candidate
judge scores --- \emph{judge-as-filter} (verdicts accept or reject; fusion
order preserved) and \emph{RRF-fuse} (reciprocal-rank fusion of the fusion
and judge orderings) --- which isolate whether the retrieval loss comes
from the verdicts or from the ordering they induce. (The replay re-issues
one QDCVR-Hybrid call per question with an identical configuration and
reads the pools and scores from its logged response; the judged orderings
reproduce the original runs exactly, indicating deterministic judging.
One merge-stage exception: Q14's replay merge produced twelve candidates
against the original run's eleven judged; all statistics use the original
logged pools.)
The seven pipeline methods
run over the full
42-document catalog with no library pinning; all output document-level
rankings (BM25 scores full texts; dense aggregates chunks by max-pooling),
so lists are directly comparable. Two construction asymmetries should be
kept in mind when reading the lexical results: BM25 is an evaluation-side
reimplementation over full texts (the platform's internal lexical channel
uses the same tokenizer with chunked documents), and its full-text
granularity plus the questions' corpus-vocabulary phrasing both favor
lexical matching. All platform methods are invoked exactly as
an external agent would invoke them: through the MCP tool interface against
the live services.

\emph{Agent layer} (Section~\ref{sec:agentic}): four arms answer four
questions (Q2, Q4, Q6, Q7) through the public chat API, non-streaming,
20-turn budget, 15-minute client timeout: \emph{Search} (library pinned,
Lane A policy), \emph{Librarian} (all libraries, Lane B policy),
\emph{Hybrid} (library pinned + Lane C suffix), and \emph{Bare} (no
knowledge-base tools, answer from parametric knowledge only). Note the
deliberate scope difference from the tool layer: pinned arms search a
single library (as small as two documents), so their confuser pools are a
fraction of the full catalog --- Section~\ref{sec:agentic} returns to this.
Every arm is
driven by the \texttt{omp} CLI agent engine\footnote{\url{https://github.com/can1357/oh-my-pi}}
(v18.4.10) wrapping the same
LLM (GLM-5.3-Flash,\footnote{Model card:
\url{https://open.bigmodel.cn}} default thinking level), so arms differ
only in retrieval affordance --- plus one honesty instruction appended to
the bare arm's prompt, disclosed verbatim in
Section~\ref{sec:agentic}. Zero-turn empty responses and transient
gateway timeouts are retried once (a known transport flake): across the
campaign the retry fired on two runs, both of which succeeded on re-run;
orphaned engine processes are cleaned between runs. The entire grid is run
twice (rounds 1 and 2); Section~\ref{sec:agentic} reports round 1 in its
tables and both rounds in the text.

\subsection{Abstention and calibration probes}
Beyond the gold questions, four out-of-corpus queries probe the honesty
contract at the tool layer, two of them also at the agent layer (across
all three grounded arms); and because every judged score is logged, the hybrid cut's relative margin is replayed offline across a
sweep of values (Section~\ref{sec:inflation}).

\subsection{Metrics}
Tool layer: Hit@5 (any gold document in top-5), Recall@5 (fraction of gold
documents in top-5), MRR@5, and per-call latency (mean and median; each
method warmed up once, first call discarded; BM25 is timed in-process
while platform methods additionally pay MCP transport, a sub-millisecond
cost at the reported scale). Agent layer: end-to-end wall
time, agent turns returned by the engine, gold-document citation hit ---
the answer text contains the gold document's unique filename fragment
(automated substring check; gold fragments were chosen to be unambiguous) ---
and fact-marker hits (automated substring check against 2--3
ground-truth values or terms per question). Fact markers are treated as
\emph{corroborating}, not primary, evidence: several markers overlap the
question's own wording, so marker hits alone cannot establish grounding
(the citation check is primary; Section~\ref{sec:agentic} additionally
decontaminates marker matching by deleting gold-citing lines). Refusals
are detected when a hedging marker appears in the answer's opening 200
characters; on the six out-of-corpus agent probes this rule agrees with
the manual transcript audit on five and misses one explicit not-found
report, so refusal counts reported here are the manually audited ones. All checks
are automated string matches against the released transcripts; no human
rater was involved. For the verifier analysis we log per-candidate judge
scores from the hybrid response. Hardware: one RTX 5060 (8\,GB) serves
embeddings and the judge worker; all services run locally on Windows.
Per-question best ranks for all nine retrieval configurations are
tabulated in \ref{app:perq}.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section answers RQ2--RQ4 in order: the ranking trade-off of the gate
(\S\ref{sec:retrieval}--\S\ref{sec:inflation}), the deployed lanes and the
honesty contract (\S\ref{sec:agentic}, \S\ref{sec:abstention}), the latency
accounting (\S\ref{sec:composition}), and the robustness tier that tests
whether the findings are structural (\S\ref{sec:robustness}).

Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} and Fig.~\ref{fig:retrieval} give the tool-layer
results. With sixteen questions the Wilson 95\% intervals on the headline
Hit@5 proportions are wide (judged hybrid $[0.23, 0.67]$; judge-as-filter
$[0.72, 0.99]$; BM25/RRF $[0.81, 1.00]$) --- the qualitative decomposition,
not the point estimates, is the claim. Three observations matter for
practitioners.

\textbf{Curated same-domain corpora favor lexical precision.} With 42
well-described documents and questions authored in the corpus's vocabulary,
BM25 achieves Hit@5 1.00 (MRR 0.958) at millisecond latency, RRF matches it
(MRR 0.927), and two-stage fusion joins them at Hit@5 1.00 once the
corpus is healthy. Dense retrieval misses exactly one question (Q16): the
query asks about the \emph{heatdecay092 post-compensation verification}
while four near-duplicate sibling reports from the same incident family
crowd the top-5 --- a confuser-density failure, not a vocabulary one (the
earlier 8-question round additionally exposed a synonym miss, Q5's
\emph{drop} vs.\ \emph{plunge}, which fusion repairs). Graph search,
limited to \textsc{contains} matching on node names and tags, is the
weakest standalone signal (0.56).

\textbf{Verification is cheap; re-ranking is the risk.} Judging twelve
candidates adds well under a second with the resident worker. But the
QDCVR-Hybrid arm loses top-5 hits relative to its own fusion stage
(0.44 vs.\ 0.94 Hit@5). The failure decomposes cleanly: on eight questions
(Q2, Q4, Q5, Q6, Q8, Q9, Q11, Q12) the gold document \emph{was} judged and
the gate's verdicts reordered same-domain confusers above it; on Q16 the
gold never reached the judge at all --- all twelve candidate slots were
taken by confusers upstream in the fusion stage. Keeping the verdicts but
discarding the induced ordering --- the \emph{judge-as-filter} rows of
Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} --- repairs every one of the eight losses and
regresses none (8 wins, 0 losses, 8 ties on Hit@5; exact binomial
$p = 0.008$, two-sided), restoring Hit@5 to 0.94 and MRR to 0.856 with
every returned citation still judge-verified; the only ranking regression
anywhere is Q7, where the gold slips from third to fifth. Said plainly:
judge-as-filter returns \emph{exactly the fusion-only ranking} --- the
per-question orderings are identical on all sixteen questions --- so at
the tool layer the gate's measured contribution is the audit metadata
attached to each citation, bought at the hybrid call's $\approx$480\,ms
surcharge over fusion-only. We therefore recommend deployments consume
verdicts as a filter rather than an ordering; the change is a single
configuration value, and migrating the shipped default is tracked in the
roadmap (Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}). Both halves
trace to the same root cause, quantified next.

\textbf{Two-stage fusion recovers its inputs here.} On the healthy corpus
the BM25+graph-then-dense pipeline reaches Hit@5 1.00 (Recall@5 0.97, one gold of a two-gold question ranked
outside the top-5), confirming that its added stages
 neither add nor subtract on this corpus once the stores are intact;
per-question results are in the released logs.

Table~\ref{tab:agentic} and Fig.~\ref{fig:e2e} summarize the round-1 runs.
We present them as an end-to-end case study of the deployed system rather
than a statistical comparison; full per-run JSON transcripts are released.

\textbf{Grounding is a property of the lanes, not of the model.} All three
knowledge-base-grounded arms named the gold document in all twelve valid
runs and reproduced all twelve available fact-marker sets --- including the
exact watch-clause numbers (31.35\,g, $\pm$0.05\,g, 5\,min) and the
telemetry root cause (a dead thermocouple loop, ``TC0''). The marker hits
survive decontamination: after deleting every answer line that names a
gold document (so markers must come from the answer's own prose or
tables), all twelve grounded runs still reproduce their full marker sets.
Six of the twelve runs answer from pinned libraries of only one or two
documents (Q2's failure-analysis library holds two; Q6's watch-clause
library one), where hitting the gold is a low bar; the substantive
evidence is the six runs over Q4 and Q7's 31-document process library.
The bare arm's prompt adds one instruction --- ``answer using only your
own knowledge; if you are uncertain, say so explicitly'' --- mirroring a
deployment without retrieval; grounded arms receive no honesty
instruction beyond their lane prompts. Even so, the bare arm
never cited a source: on the EL question it produced a plausible
\emph{textbook} diagnosis (interfacial adhesion failure, moisture ingress)
that misses the corpus's recorded root cause --- lamination-machine
thermocouple drift leaving EVA under-cured, the mechanism Q2's fact
markers encode --- and on the telemetry question it hit none of the fact
markers (it did refuse explicitly in two of four runs, honest about its
ignorance but holding no corpus facts either way).
This is the honesty contract of Section~\ref{sec:lanes} observed end to
end: without verification-grounded retrieval, the same engine produces
fluent, unsourced, and in these cases partly wrong answers.

\textbf{Reconciling the tool layer and the agent layer.} At the tool layer,
the judged hybrid missed nine of sixteen questions; here the hybrid arm hit
the gold on all four. Two design differences explain this, and both are
part of the lane contract rather than luck. First, pinned scope: the agent
arm searches one library (Q2's failure-analysis library holds two
documents), so the same-domain confusers that flood the 42-document
tool-layer catalog never enter its candidate pool. Second, agency: a lane
that can read what it retrieved and issue follow-up calls is not bound by a
single ranking --- the librarian lane in particular is built to keep
looking. The tool layer measures the ranking stage in isolation; the agent
layer measures the lane as deployed. The reconciliation yields an
explicit, testable model of what the gate buys. (i)~Verdicts as a binary
\emph{filter} are safe exactly when judge recall on gold documents is high
(here 17/17 in-pool), and they add per-citation audit; discrimination is
irrelevant. (ii)~Verdicts as an \emph{ordering} are safe only when
pairwise gold-versus-confuser score separation exceeds the base ranker's,
which same-domain inflation destroys. (iii)~\emph{Threshold abstention}
requires out-of-corpus calibration the acceptance threshold does not have.
Verification, in this deployment, buys auditability and honest termination
--- not ranked-list quality (the filter is order-preserving; the re-ranker
actively harms it) and not pool composition.

\textbf{Partial honesty counts.} The one refusal-flagged grounded run,
hybrid-Q4, is a positive case rather than a failure: its answer cites the
POE gold document correctly and states explicitly that the library holds no
EVA-side comparison data --- a scoped, honest disclosure instead of
speculation. No grounded run fabricated a citation.

\textbf{The lanes buy their latency profiles with turn structure.} Hybrid
completed every round-1 question in 3--5 turns (mean 3.8) and 36--70\,s;
librarian
spent 5--6 turns (mean 5.5) and 54--91\,s; search sat between on wall time
but needed 8 turns on the hardest question (Q7, the telemetry cluster).
The librarian's extra turns buy exhaustive coverage with blind-spot
disclosure --- the arm that must not miss a document, not the arm that
must be fast.

\textbf{Repetition: outcomes reproduce, latency does not.} The full grid
was run a second time. Citation outcomes agree on all 16 of 16 cells ---
trivially for the four bare cells (no citations in either round), so the
informative agreement is 12/12 grounded cells; the decontaminated
fact-marker check is a clean sweep in round 1 (12/12) and near-clean in
round 2 (11/12; the exception is hybrid-Q2, whose 61/85 values appear only
inside evidence lines that also cite the gold document --- an artifact of
the line-deletion rule rather than a grounding failure; its citation hit
stands). Decontamination is applied to the answer's first 600 characters,
where every matched marker occurs, so the check is conservative. Round-2
turn means track the load (hybrid 5.0, search 7.25, librarian 7.75) while
every citation outcome repeats.
Wall time and turn counts fluctuate substantially
(e.g.\ search-Q2: 70\,s/6 turns in round 1 versus 165\,s/11 turns in
round 2; librarian-Q4: 84\,s/6 turns versus 92\,s/11 turns; hybrid-Q2:
62\,s/4 turns versus 60\,s/3 turns). The dichotomy --- stable grounding
under unstable latency --- is exactly what the turn-economics model of
Section~\ref{sec:composition} predicts, and it is the strongest argument
for the platform's fixed turn budgets.

\textbf{The fast lane has a real tail.} In the first campaign pass, the
search arm (Q2) and the bare arm (Q4) hit the server's 600\,s turn budget
and returned gateway timeouts; both re-runs completed normally (70\,s and
99\,s). We attribute the stalls to engine-side transport stalls under LLM
provider load, not to the retrieval layer (whose machine-side latency is
sub-second); the platform's retry-once policy (Section~\ref{sec:setup})
fired on exactly these two runs. Interactive deployments should treat the
server turn budget as the operative tail-latency knob.

\subsection{The verifier's own failure mode: same-domain inflation (RQ2, RQ3)}
\label{sec:inflation}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.5\textwidth]{fig4_inflation}
\caption{Evidence-criterion judge scores from the sixteen QDCVR-Hybrid tool
runs: all 191 judged candidates. Gold documents (green, $n = 17$; Q16's
gold never entered its candidate pool) and same-domain non-gold survivors
(orange, $n = 174$) overlap almost completely: medians 0.876 versus 0.863,
Mann--Whitney $p = 0.26$. The 0.5 acceptance threshold separates both
groups from zero, not from each other.}
\label{fig:inflation}
\end{figure}

Fig.~\ref{fig:inflation} plots every judge score from the sixteen hybrid
runs: 191 candidates (twelve per run except Q14, whose merge produced
eleven). Not one candidate was rejected --- every logged score is
$\geq 0.589$, far above the 0.5 acceptance threshold, so at the threshold
layer the gate's filtering power on this corpus is zero. The failures
concentrate in ranking, not acceptance. Aggregating the 17 in-pool gold
instances (Q1 and Q5 carry two golds each; Q16's gold was never judged):
only 5 of 17 rank in the top-3 of the judged ordering, while 9 rank eighth
or lower. Gold and non-gold score distributions are statistically
indistinguishable at this sample size (medians 0.876 vs.\ 0.863;
two-sided Mann--Whitney with tie correction, $U = 1726$, $p = 0.26$, a
gold-favoring trend with Cliff's
$\delta \approx 0.17$; scores are nested within 16 runs, so we treat the
test as indicative only --- and the sign test of
Section~\ref{sec:retrieval} is likewise post-hoc and descriptive, one of
nine configurations evaluated on the same sixteen questions). On Q6 (the 31.35\,g$\pm$0.05\,g watch
clause), the fusion ranking placed the gold clause document first, but the
judged re-ranking promoted five unrelated telemetry reports (0.92--0.93)
while the gold document scored 0.82 --- pushing the gold document out of
the top-5 that the answer agent reads in full; the same pattern recurs on
Q2 (Fig.~\ref{fig:anatomy}), where five extruder-telemetry reports and an
unrelated PET process document (0.887--0.926) outrank the gold EL report
(0.846). Even the relative cut participates in the failure: it
bound in most rounds (trimming one to five tail candidates), and on Q11
its best-minus-0.15 line fell \emph{above} the gold's score (0.741 vs.\
0.711), deleting the gold from the kept set outright.

\textbf{An offline replay turns this failure into a calibration result.}
Because every judged score is logged, the cut margin can be replayed
without re-running the pipeline. Sweeping the margin from 0.05 to 0.35
(with the top-5 floor unchanged): at 0.05 the kept set shrinks to a mean
6.4 candidates but retains only 8/17 golds; the shipped 0.15 margin keeps
a mean of 10.4 candidates and 16/17 golds; at 0.20 all 17 golds survive
with 11.1 candidates retained. Because the replay selects and evaluates
the margin on the same sixteen questions that motivated it, 0.20 is an
in-sample observation, not a validated setting; deployment-time
calibration must re-derive it per library on fresh questions. The margin
is therefore repairable by a
small configuration change, but no margin separates golds from
confusers, because the scores themselves overlap.

\textbf{Cross-domain claims do not survive measurement either.} The
design intent --- that reading content separates topical neighbors from
evidence --- does deliver cross-domain filtering \emph{in the answer
layer} (Section~\ref{sec:abstention}), but the acceptance threshold
carries none of it. On four
out-of-corpus probe queries (lithium-battery thermal runaway, child
vaccination schedules, Python concurrency, injection-mold vent design),
dense retrieval still returns same-corpus documents at 0.40--0.60 cosine,
and the judged hybrid keeps 9--11 of its 12 candidates --- the same
inflation, now cross-domain. (One early production incident in which an
out-of-domain document scored 0.92 on a PV question motivated the anchor
guard; the probes show such inflation is the norm, not the exception.)
What the probes establish is where honesty actually lives: not in the
threshold, but in the reading-and-answer layer that the lanes wrap around
it.

This failure is the mirror image of the one the gate was built to stop:
dense similarity over-trusts topical neighbors, and the judge --- a model
used for evidence discrimination --- over-trusts domain-internal
discourse. The lane design contains two partial mitigations, and their
limits are exactly what this analysis exposes. Lane~B's mandatory reading
of survivors surfaces mismatched content to the answering agent; and the
hybrid cut's relative margin with a top-5 floor keeps most outranked gold
documents in the machine-readable audit trail --- though, as Q11 shows,
it can also delete one. The quantified repair is cut-margin calibration
per library (the replay above); the open problem is the score overlap
itself: domain-aware negative training, score normalization against a
per-library null distribution, or pairing the judge with a contradiction
check. We report this as a measured open problem rather than a solved
one.

\subsection{Honest abstention under out-of-corpus probes (RQ4)}
\label{sec:abstention}

The lanes' final contract --- an explicit not-found instead of speculation
--- was never exercised by the gold questions, all of which are in-corpus.
Four out-of-corpus probe queries (lithium-battery thermal runaway, child
vaccination schedules, Python concurrency, injection-mold vent design)
close that gap at both layers (Fig.~\ref{fig:ood}). At the tool layer,
abstention does
\emph{not} happen: as Section~\ref{sec:inflation} showed, the acceptance
threshold keeps 9--11 of 12 candidates even for vaccination or battery
queries. At the agent layer, the contract holds without exception: all six
runs (three grounded arms $\times$ two probes) answered with an explicit
not-found report --- each enumerates the libraries searched, states that
the corpus does not cover the topic, and declines to answer from memory;
none cites a document as evidence (zero \texttt{.md} citations across all
six transcripts). Three runs add a dedicated blind-spot section, two disclosing search
coverage limits: one
librarian run reports that its vector and experience channels were
unavailable that round, completed only a catalog-level scan, and therefore
``cannot claim exhaustive coverage,''
and several runs recommend ingestible sources (e.g.\ the relevant national
standards) instead of fabricating a procedure. The honesty contract is
thus real but its enforcement point is the reading-and-answer layer ---
precisely why the platform wraps verification inside lanes that must read
before they answer, rather than exposing a bare similarity threshold.

\subsection{Where the time goes: LLM turns, not retrieval (RQ4)}
\label{sec:composition}

Across the agentic runs, machine-side retrieval is uniformly sub-second
(Table~\ref{tab:retrieval}) while the twelve grounded end-to-end runs take 36--91\,s: wall
time is dominated by LLM turns, at 11.7--15.3\,s per turn across arms (per-arm
means: search 11.7, librarian 12.8, hybrid 15.3; computed over the twelve
grounded runs), consistent with the observation that agentic-RAG latency
is turn-dominated \cite{singh-agentic-rag}.
The lanes differ almost entirely in turn structure: Lane~C compresses its
pipeline into one tool call; Lane~B spends turns on catalog navigation but
buys exhaustive coverage and readable provenance; Lane~A sits between. The
practical consequence is an engineering rule the platform now follows
everywhere: \emph{move orchestration server-side and cap turns} --- the
single-call hybrid lane is both the fastest and the most auditable, because
its audit trail ships with the one response.

\textbf{Margin.} The leave-one-question-out replay
(Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}a)
(\texttt{exp6\_loo\_margin.py}) addresses the in-sample concern directly:
selection and evaluation are separated, the selected margins are stable
(0.18 on fifteen of sixteen folds), and the single holdout failure is the
already-known Q11 boundary. The deployed default adds a small engineering
safety pad on top of the LOO-selected value; the pad's magnitude is a
judgment call, and the Q11 fold is the warning that justifies it --- a
calibration made without a Q11-like question in its training set would
pick 0.15 and lose that gold.

\textbf{Paraphrase.} The paraphrase grid
(Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}a; \texttt{exp6\_paraphrase.py})
confirms the vocabulary-dependence concern about BM25's ceiling --- its
1.00 is partly a vocabulary artifact, dropping to 0.88 with MRR 0.755
under rewording --- and sharpens the practical picture: dense retrieval is
the paraphrase-robust backbone (0.94 unchanged), and both the judged
hybrid's failure (0.50) and the filter repair (0.94, again identical to
fusion-only) survive rewording. Pooled over all 32 question variants the
filter-versus-judged record is 15 wins, 0 losses, 17 ties (exact binomial
$p < 0.0001$). Two honesty notes: the paraphrases are author-written, and
one of them (Q7) strips every discriminative token --- all nine
configurations miss it, so that cell measures a rewording no retriever
could follow rather than lexical fragility; and dense's unchanged rate
hides a composition swap (it loses reworded Q7 and gains Q16). The
verification failure is a property of
the judge and the pool, not of the question phrasing.

\textbf{Second signal.} The NLI replay
(Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}b; \texttt{exp7\_second\_signal.py})
runs the same 191 logged (query, candidate) pairs and four out-of-corpus
topics through a generic Chinese NLI model --- one 330\,M checkpoint, one
question-as-context template, premises truncated to roughly the first 500
post-tokenization characters (59 of 191 pairs hit the cap). The result
refutes the obvious remedy, and not by replicating the judge's failure:
generic entailment ranks these candidates \emph{worse} than the judge
(Hit@5 0.31, MRR 0.138; Spearman $\rho = -0.13$), and it
\emph{anti-separates} --- gold scores sit \emph{below} non-gold scores
(medians 0.011 vs.\ 0.053, Cliff's $\delta = 0.23$, $p = 0.12$) --- while
its out-of-corpus behavior matches the judge's: corpus documents score up
to 0.997 against unrelated topics, so an NLI threshold would abstain on
nothing either. Truncation is not the explanation: six of the ten gold
documents that fit the context window entirely still score below 0.01,
and on the one question whose verdict is verbatim in the visible head
(Q7), the NLI
score is 0.011. Document-granularity evidence verification is hard for
both a fine-tuned judge and a generic NLI model, and threshold
abstention fails for both. The symbolic check tells the complementary
story: on the numeric watch clause (Q6) the generic NLI is actually the
stronger signal --- it ranks the gold first of twelve, where the shipped
judge ranks it tenth --- and a trivial substring rule decides the same
case with perfect separation (gold 1/1, confusers 0/11). Verification
signals fail in different ways and succeed in different places;
verification-primitive choice is therefore a genuinely open engineering
problem, and the answer layer's
honesty contract --- which depends on reading, not on the signal ---
becomes more, not less, important.

\textbf{Span-level NLI and ensembles.} The obvious upgrade --- score
every $\approx$320-character span and pool by max, then rank-fuse with
the judge --- makes things worse, and informatively so. Span-max NLI
drops to Hit@5 0.19 (MRR 0.094) and turns the anti-separation
\emph{statistically significant in the wrong direction}: gold documents
score a median 0.47 while non-gold documents reach 0.92
(Mann--Whitney $p = 0.007$), because scanning a long document for
\emph{any} entailing span rewards exactly the long, topically adjacent
documents that cause the retrieval failure --- a multiple-comparisons
amplifier. The rank-fusion ensemble (judge + span-NLI) recovers nothing
(Hit@5 0.31, MRR 0.111). The lesson sharpens the architecture claim:
evidence verification is not an entailment-scanning task at any
granularity we tested --- the failure is not where the model reads but
what the question is asking it to decide --- which is why the platform's
rubric criterion, the reading requirement, and the answer layer's
disclosure duties, rather than a sharper similarity signal, are where
the design places its trust.

\textbf{Graded probes.} Six fresh probes (three partial-coverage, three
absent; Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}d) extend the abstention analysis beyond
the single partial-coverage
episode of the original OOD probes. The tool layer behaves identically in
every tier --- 10--12 of 12
candidates kept at scores up to 0.983 --- so the threshold's blindness is
universal, not question-specific. At the agent layer the partial probes
produced the behavior the honesty contract promises: the
acetic-acid-and-aluminum-frame probe answered with the corpus's recorded
evidence (acetic acid corroding copper ribbons), labeled the aluminum
frame question explicitly as unevidenced, and graded its own extension as
P1/P2 inference rather than fact --- a scoped, auditable disclosure. All
three absent probes, run through the same agent arm, returned explicit
not-found reports enumerating the libraries searched (48--114\,s). One
probe we had labeled partial turned out to be covered: the corpus contains
a verified secondary-crosslink rework case, which our tier labels ---
author estimates made before running --- underestimated. We release the
tier labels and transcripts with that correction noted.

\textbf{Second corpus.} To probe domain and language transfer
(Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}c) --- and
whether the judged lane's collapse requires the confuser density of the
production corpus --- we downloaded all 100 gold-track arXiv papers of
the platform's released benchmark suite (English; ten distinct fields)
and ingested them into a fresh knowledge base: 79 papers parsed and
indexed cleanly (8{,}031 chunks; 21 failed parsing, mostly
formula-dense scans), and nine of the suite's ten questions remain
usable --- their single shared miss is one of the parse failures. On
this corpus every main configuration reaches Hit@5 0.90, and the judged
lane \emph{loses nothing}: judged = fusion = judge-as-filter = 0.90 at
eight times the candidate-confuser scale of the first probe. The Hit@5
collapse of Section~\ref{sec:retrieval} therefore tracks confuser
density (deliberately engineered in the production corpus's telemetry
cluster), not domain, not language, not corpus size, and not the judge
checkpoint. One deployment dependency surfaces honestly: the bulk
import bypasses the ingestion-time tagging agent, so the papers graph
carries almost no relations and graph search falls to 0.20 ---
two-stage recovers to 0.90 through its BM25 candidates, but a
production deployment that wants graph expansion must run tagged
ingestion.
