\begin{abstract}
Enterprise document systems must locate source material, determine whether
its content supports an answer, and communicate when a completed search finds
no support. Retrieval-augmented generation improves discovery, but topical
similarity alone cannot establish evidentiary support. We present QDCVR, a
self-hosted knowledge-base system that links content-informed acquisition and
organization with an invoked server-side evidence-checking tool and an answer workflow
instructed to report absence; these mechanisms operate at different layers
rather than as three uniform server-enforced guarantees.
We evaluate the deployed system on three bounded corpora. In a post-hoc replay
of identical industrial candidate pools, judge-score re-ranking yields Hit@5
0.44, while a derived replay that preserves fusion order among scored survivors
yields 0.94, matching fusion-only. All 191 recorded segment scores exceed the
0.5 threshold, and one fused candidate has no recorded score; this comparison
warns against using this checkpoint as a ranker but does not demonstrate a
threshold-filter benefit. In a 30-cell end-to-end comparison, all answers
contain a gold document identifier. Strict keyword coverage is 9/10 in each
arm; the misses are word-form mismatches, and alias-aware coverage is 10/10.
A mis-specified gold key was corrected during revision and both grades are
retained. Recognized citation-marker hits are
9/10, 7/10 and 2/10 for platform, file and dense arms. These are provenance
and lexical-marker checks, not semantic faithfulness measures. An eighteen-arm
battery observed no fabricated citations; selected catalog-truncation paths
were repaired and retested. The findings support a bounded deployment account,
not universal retrieval superiority or a guarantee of answer correctness.
\end{abstract}
