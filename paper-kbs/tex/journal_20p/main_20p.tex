\begin{abstract}
Retrieval from evolving document collections depends on where documents are
placed, how they are described and whether their indexes reflect changes.
We present HMRS, a self-hosted system that manages collections, documents
and segments and exposes them through catalogue, shelf, document and
segment views. The same management state supports collection selection,
source inspection and lifecycle audits. In a 100-paper ingestion campaign,
79 papers were retrievable at the audit snapshot. A separate rename test
exposed nine stale vector entries, which re-indexing removed.

On 72 questions, the scripted selector reached the labelled collection in
72 cases with full route cards and 37 with nominally name-only cards.
The tiered configuration that combined document and vector chunk windows
returned annotated target strings on 52 of the 72 questions, compared with
37 for the baseline tiered configuration. Its trace-recorded text volume
was about ten times the baseline's and 1.3\% of a mechanical full scan's.
Two reruns on the same questions gave 51 versus 39 and 48 versus 36.
These comparisons
measure collection choice and target-string containment. In a separate
replay of sixteen candidate pools, fusion order retained 15 top-five hits
and verifier-score ordering retained seven. The findings connect retrieval
behaviour to collection metadata, reading scope and candidate order, all
of which can be inspected at the point where the system uses them.
\end{abstract}
