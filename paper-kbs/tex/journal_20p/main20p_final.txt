Abstract

Document collections support knowledge work only when acquisition, organisation, maintenance and access are connected. We
present HMRS, a self-hosted system that manages collections, documents and segments through catalogue, shelf, document and
segment views. Parsed content informs collection assignment; document files, the tree registry and per-library metadata support
lifecycle audits. An optional evidence gate scores passages but does not establish entailment; pinned search may bypass catalogue
navigation.

   In a 100-paper arXiv campaign, 79 were retrievable and 21 were not. A separate 400-page-per-language test measured assignment
accuracy of 0.875 (English) and 1.0 (Chinese/Japanese). A scripted 72-question route audit selected the gold collection in 72/72
full-card and 37/72 name-only cases; an unlogged fallback may use descriptions. On the same questions, frozen containment was
52/72 for the tiered chunk-window arm and later executions gave 51/72 (70.8%), with the arm ordering preserved across three
executions; a precise no-navigation arm reached 45/72 (difference unresolved) and a scoped flat union 39/72. Arms vary in multiple
components and the flat lookup may depend on the gold domain. Gold identifiers appeared in all 60 answer-or- trace cells; the arms
separated on document-style citation markers (9/10 versus 2/10), a lexical contrast, not semantic quality. In a same-pool replay of a
retired hybrid wrapper, verifier re-ranking lowered Hit@5 from 15/16 to 7/16; fusion order restored 15/16. All 191 recorded scores
exceeded threshold.

   The results document an auditable structure, not general retrieval superiority, semantic answer quality or reliability. This replay
warns against one historical ordering configuration; filtering was not tested on that pool.
