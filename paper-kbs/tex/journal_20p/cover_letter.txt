Dear Editors of Knowledge-Based Systems,

Please consider our research article, "HMRS: Hierarchical document
management for evidence-traced retrieval."

Document retrieval begins with how a collection is maintained. A document
must be assigned to a collection, described so that a query can find it,
and kept in sync with its index when it changes. HMRS connects these
operations to retrieval in a self-hosted system. Its catalogue, document
shelf and segment views give operators and retrieval agents access to the
metadata and source material used at each step.

The paper examines how those choices affect access. A rename audit found
nine stale vector entries and confirmed their removal after re-indexing.
On 72 questions, the tiered configuration using document and vector chunk
windows returned annotated target strings for 52 questions, compared with
37 for the baseline tiered configuration. In a separate replay on 16
fixed candidate pools, fusion order placed a hit in the top five for 15
queries, compared with seven when the candidates were ordered by verifier
score. Together, the results show how a knowledge system can inspect
which collection was searched, what evidence was read and how the final
ordering was produced.

The manuscript links the system design to its implementation and
evaluation, with per-question records and campaign inventories in the
supplementary material. The connection between knowledge organisation
and inspectable evidence access will interest readers of Knowledge-Based
Systems who design and evaluate knowledge-intensive systems.
