Abstract
Document collections need more than a retriever: documents must be parsed and admitted, organized so that they can be found,
and used to answer questions whose cited evidence can be traced. We present QDCVR, a self-hosted knowledge-base management
system that treats content as the shared object across acquisition, organization and question answering. Its management layer parses
and gates documents, splits long files, places documents by content rather than filename, and keeps files, registry and metadata
consistent through rename, move, delete and re-index. Retrieval is one module: three lanes share an invoked server-side content
verifier, and an answer workflow is instructed to report absence. We evaluate the deployed system on three corpora. Our evaluation
method combines functional audits, per-question answer checks, abstention probes and same-pool replays. On ten arXiv questions
crossed with three retrieval interfaces (30 answer cells), every answer names its gold document, strict keyword coverage is 9/10
per interface and alias-aware coverage 10/10, and recognized citation-marker hits are 9/10, 7/10 and 2/10. Acquisition left 21 of
100 submissions unretrievable without a complete failure inventory, and a reproduced catalog-transport failure was repaired. In a
same-pool replay on an industrial corpus, ordering by the verifier score reduces Hit@5 from 0.94 to 0.44 while fusion order restores
0.94; the scores are not usable as a ranker, and the 0.5 threshold admitted every candidate, so filtering is untested. Wall-clock and
cost disfavor the platform arm. The findings indicate a bounded deployment account, not universal retrieval superiority.
Keywords: Knowledge base management, Document ingestion, Knowledge organization, Retrieval-augmented generation,
