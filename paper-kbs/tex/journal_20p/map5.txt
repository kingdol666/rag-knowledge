Abstract
Retrieval-augmented generation improves discovery, but topical similarity cannot establish evidentiary support: enterprise
knowledge-base workflows rarely own the property that a cited sentence is supported by the cited document. We present QD-
CVR, a self-hosted knowledge-base system that organizes acquisition, admission and abstention around content; its three design
contracts are stated as hypotheses tested at the layer each occupies, not as uniform server-enforced guarantees. The central finding
separates the verifier’s roles. On identical logged candidate pools of a 42-document industrial corpus, ordering by the content-
verifier score lowers Hit@5 from 0.94 to 0.44, while retaining fusion order among recorded survivors matches fusion-only at 0.94;
all 191 recorded scores exceed the 0.5 threshold, and one fused candidate has none. The loss is attributable to the induced ordering,
not to filtering, and an independent NLI checkpoint fails the same way. A verifier score that survives an admission filter is not
thereby usable as a ranker. In a 30-cell end-to-end comparison, all answers contain a gold document identifier. Strict keyword
coverage is 9/10 per arm, the misses word-form mismatches; alias-aware coverage is 10/10; a mis-specified gold key was corrected
during revision and both grades retained. Citation-marker hits are 9/10, 7/10 and 2/10 for platform, file and dense arms; these are
provenance and lexical-marker checks, not semantic faithfulness measures. An eighteen-arm battery observed no fabricated cita-
tions, and a reproduced catalog-truncation failure was repaired and retested. The findings support a bounded deployment account,
not universal retrieval or answer-quality superiority.
Keywords: Knowledge base management, Document ingestion, Content verification, Knowledge organization,
Retrieval-augmented generation, Model Context Protocol
