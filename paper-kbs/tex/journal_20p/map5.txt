Abstract
Enterprise document collections need more than a retriever: documents must be parsed and admitted, organized so that they can
be found, and used to answer questions whose cited evidence can be traced. We present QDCVR, a self-hosted knowledge-base
management system that treats content as the shared object across acquisition, organization and question answering. Its management
layer parses and gates documents, splits long files at structural boundaries, places documents by content rather than filename, and
keeps files, registry and metadata consistent through rename, move, delete and re-index. Retrieval is one module of this system:
three lanes share an invoked server-side content verifier, and an answer workflow is instructed to report absence. We evaluate the
deployed system on three bounded corpora. On ten arXiv questions crossed with three retrieval interfaces (30 answer cells), every
answer names its gold document, strict keyword coverage is 9/10 per interface and alias-aware coverage 10/10, and recognized
citation-marker hits are 9/10, 7/10 and 2/10. Acquisition left 21 of 100 submissions unretrievable without a complete failure
inventory, and a reproduced catalog-transport failure was repaired and retested. In a same-pool replay on an industrial corpus,
ordering candidates by the verifier score lowers Hit@5 from 0.94 to 0.44 while fusion order restores 0.94, so the verifier serves
as an admission filter, not a ranker. Wall-clock and cost figures do not favor the platform arm. The findings support a bounded
deployment account, not universal retrieval superiority.
Keywords: Knowledge base management, Document ingestion, Knowledge organization, Retrieval-augmented generation,
