\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The central finding is a placement result for content verifiers. Replaying
the same recorded candidate pools of the industrial corpus shows that
ordering candidates by the judge score lowers Hit@5 from 0.94 under
fusion-only to 0.44, whereas retaining fusion order among the recorded
survivors matches fusion-only at 0.94; all 191 recorded scores exceed the 0.5
threshold, and one additional fusion candidate has no recorded score, so the
comparison diagnoses a harmful ordering role rather than a benefit from
threshold filtering. An independent NLI checkpoint fails the same way on the
same pools. The lesson travels beyond this checkpoint: a verifier score that
is acceptable as an admission filter should not be assumed usable as a
ranker, and the two roles must be tested separately.

QDCVR implements the surrounding workflow as three
contracts---content informs placement, content informs admission, absence is
reported---which we treat as hypotheses at different layers, not as three
equivalent server-enforced guarantees. Storage and consistency checks are
mechanically performed; semantic placement remains an ingest-agent decision;
the answer layer's abstention behavior is evaluated from observed trials
rather than certified by a server-side semantic classifier. On the industrial
corpus, lexical retrieval reaches Hit@5 1.00, so recall is not the
contribution here. In the end-to-end grid, all 30 answer texts contain a gold
document identifier; strict keyword coverage is 9/10 in each arm---the misses
are word-form mismatches---and alias-aware coverage is 10/10 in each arm; the
BQ06 gold key was corrected to the source paper's reading, and both grades are
retained. Recognized citation-marker hits are 9/10, 7/10 and 2/10 for the
platform, file and dense arms. These measures capture identifier presence and
lexical/marker coverage, not semantic support. Acquisition left 21 of 100
submissions unretrievable without a complete failure inventory, and a selected
oversized-catalog path was repaired and retested after a false refusal.

The contribution is a same-pool diagnostic of verifier placement and an
auditable deployment account, not a claim of universal retrieval or
answer-quality superiority: the results remain bounded by one workstation,
one verifier checkpoint and developer-authored questions. Next steps include independently curated
questions, repeated agent trials, claim-level evidence-support evaluation, a
confuser-density-controlled verifier evaluation, explicit execution-failure
states for incomplete searches, transport stress tests and operator studies
of not-found reports.
