\subsection{E4: Operational knowledge and automated distillation}
\label{sec:e3}
Separate from the retrieval arms, HMRS stores operational lessons as
searchable documents and uses a credibility tier to decide which lessons to present
(Section~\ref{sec:org-experience}). An extraction run produced 13 draft
lessons from one small library. In a separate end-to-end case, the system
stored one lesson (\texttt{exp-d6f4d47ae3d6}), which global experience
search later ranked first with a similarity of~0.711. The approval check
encountered an empty draft pool, and the heuristic dry run produced output
that failed the quality criteria. These checks cover extraction, storage
and retrieval. Scheduled distillation, review and approval at scale,
application feedback and credibility decay remain untested.

\subsection{E5: Content-layer configuration comparison}
\label{sec:ladder-opt}
A0 selects a collection from the route cards and gives candidate references
to a judge with a 40{,}000-character document window. A0f fetches windows of
up to 250{,}000 characters and passes them directly to the judge. A0h
combines the resulting judged text with up to eight vector-retrieved chunk
windows in a pack capped at 60{,}000 characters. A3 uses the nominally
name-only route cards.

The cross-library arms vary the candidate source: PD searches the ten
libraries separately and judges the six unique documents with the highest
scores using the A0f window. A1b merges the ten libraries' vector results
and uses the A0 reference-mode judge. A2 concatenates snippets from the
same scoped union and omits judging. Within the routed library, vector
search requests up to 30 candidates at a cosine threshold of 0.35, with
a retry at 0.30 if the first search is empty.
The configurations vary in routing, candidate construction and judging.
In the labels, ``f'' marks full-window inline judging, ``h'' the hybrid
union of judged text with chunk windows, and ``b'' the merged global
baseline; PD (per-library directed) searches each library separately.
Table~S12 lists the full configurations.
Some reference lookups may use the gold domain to resolve candidates,
as detailed in Section~\ref{sec:threats}.

\textbf{A0h put the target string into more evidence packs than any other
configuration with a bounded retrieval pack.} In
the frozen record, A0h contained the string for 52 of 72 questions (72.2\%,
Wilson 95\% interval 61.0--81.2\%), compared with 37 of 72 (51.4\%) for A0.
The gain was 20.8 percentage points, with 15 A0h-only hits and no reverse
cases (exact McNemar $p=6.1\times10^{-5}$). On the same questions in the
November~12 follow-up, A0h contained 51 targets and A0 contained 39, a
16.7-point difference with 12 A0h-only hits ($p=0.00049$).
Across the frozen and follow-up records, PD contained 46 and 45
targets, A0f 45 and 44, A1b 39 in each, and A2 25 in each
(Figure~\ref{fig:content}a and Table~S12).

The paired comparison with the scoped vector union A1b favoured A0h on
13 frozen and 12 follow-up questions, with no reverse cases
($p=0.00024$ and $0.00049$). Against A0f, the closer comparison at a
similar reading budget (both arms record roughly 300~kB of text per
question), A0h led 52 to 45 in the frozen record and 51 to 44 in the
follow-up, each with seven A0h-only hits and no reverse case ($p=0.016$).
Holding reading volume approximately constant, this contrast isolates
the added chunk windows, their priority in the pack and the
60{,}000-character cap. A0h's lead over PD was smaller. In both
records, seven questions favoured A0h and one favoured PD
($p=0.0703$). Among A0's 35 frozen misses, A0f, A0h and PD recovered
8, 15 and 9, respectively. Eight recoveries were shared by all three,
seven were unique to A0h and one to PD. Nineteen were missed by all three.
A post-hoc search placed five first matching occurrences outside the
nominal window and 30 inside. The matching file could differ from the gold
document or the one the judge read. Figure~\ref{fig:content}b and Table~S13
give the paired records, including the secondary comparisons.

The frozen A0h trace recorded 309~kB and 13.8~s of tool time per question, compared with
31.4~kB and 16.3~s for A0. The read-all scan found all 72 target strings
using 24.3~MB and 253.7~s per question, about 79 times the text recorded
for A0h. The trace counts overlap where the same text is fetched or packed
more than once. Tool time covers retrieval work and excludes answer
generation. In the follow-up, the A0 tool-time median was 9.8~s
(mean 17.4~s, maximum 540.9~s). The separately executed A3 follow-up
contained 25 target strings, down from 33, and selected the gold domain
on 49 questions. It returned no top candidate in 13 cases and an empty
pack in 23. The follow-up read-all scan completed three rows and was
excluded from the 72-question comparison.

An archived reproduction of the four content arms matched the frozen
hit status on 71/72 questions for A0f, A0h and PD and on 68/72 for A0.
Its hit/miss records match the follow-up for these four arms. A1b and A2
also had identical frozen and follow-up hit sets. A separate November~17
rerun on the same questions used the restarted live stack and omitted the
full scan. Counts fell to 48/72 for A0h, 42/72 for PD, 41/72 for A0f
and 36/72 for A0. The four arms kept their order, and A0h again contained
12 targets missed by A0 without a reverse case. Across the frozen,
follow-up and rerun records, the paired A0h-over-A0 direction held in
each, with 15, 12 and 12 A0h-only questions and no A0-only question in
any record. The corpus state was not
held fixed across these runs. The benchmark archive includes the dated
rerun traces.

In a separate 40-question diagnostic, the returned text contained the target
in 24 BM25 cases, 19 dense-search cases and 26 cases when the top five
texts from each list were concatenated. This check used different
questions, scopes and evidence construction. The trace label
\texttt{F-rrf} denotes concatenated lists.

Figure~\ref{fig:case-anatomy} shows two recorded evidence paths.
For the question on RoBERTa, the A0h pack contains a passage mentioning
RoBERTa in Lewis et~al.'s RAG paper, a different document from the
annotated source. For the \emph{Pride and Prejudice} question, A0's top
candidate is part~2, but its sole survivor is part~6; A0f's survivors are
parts~21 and~6. Neither response contains the exact target string. A0h
includes the requested opening dialogue in part~2, but a line break splits
the target phrase there. Its contiguous exact match occurs instead in a
later dialogue in part~24. The recorded HIT therefore reflects string
containment, not verification of the requested context or answer correctness.
