\subsection{E3: Operational knowledge and automated distillation}
\label{sec:e3}

The experience store holds operational lessons as first-class retrievable
documents---addressed, indexed and searched as an ordinary document, with a
credibility tier deciding whether it may be shown at all
(Section~\ref{sec:org-experience}). Three case measurements exist: an
extraction campaign over one small knowledge base produced thirteen
candidate drafts (archived \path{experience_smoke.json} in the 2026-10-13
batch tarball; \texttt{extract\_candidates}~13, \texttt{drafts\_created}~13);
one end-to-end scenario was sedimented into one experience
(\texttt{exp-d6f4d47ae3d6}) that a later global experience search returned
first, at local similarity 0.711 (archived scenario A6 of the 2026-10-12
full-test campaign); and the draft-approval path was never exercised, because
the draft pool was empty (\texttt{count}~0) and \path{experience_draft_read}
and \path{experience_draft_approve} could not run; the heuristic extraction
path had run only in dry-run mode, returning a dump the quality gate would
reject. One scenario and thirteen candidates cannot support an effectiveness
claim; scheduled distillation, review and approval at scale, application
feedback and credibility decay went unexercised. What they show is narrower:
an operational lesson can travel the document path with its tier attached.

\subsection{E4: Retrieval quality and the role of the verdict}
\label{sec:e4}
\label{sec:eval-retrieval}

Nine configurations, defined here so the table reads on its own. Five were
measured through their own tool-layer entry points: \emph{Dense} via
\texttt{kb\_search\_vector} with BGE-M3 \cite{bge-m3}; \emph{Graph} via
\texttt{kb\_graph\_search}, a keyword match over node names, paths and tags in
the document graph; \emph{Two-stage} via
\texttt{kb\_search\_two\_stage} (Section~\ref{sec:twostage}); and the hybrid
rows via \texttt{kb\_hybrid\_search}, an earlier server-side single-call
composition of the vector, catalog and judging tools, with the gate off
(\emph{Fusion-only}) and on (\emph{QDCVR-Hybrid}). \emph{BM25} runs in process
over the fetched full texts (jieba tokenizer, $k_1=1.5$, $b=0.75$), and
\emph{RRF} fuses that ranking with the dense one at $k=60$
\cite{rrf-cormack}; neither is the platform's keyword tool, so the millisecond
latencies are in-process measurements rather than a server round trip. The
evaluated hybrid wrapper is no longer registered in the current tool layer,
whose hybrid lane is the composed chat configuration of
Section~\ref{sec:twostage}, so the negative ordering result below is bounded
to that retired wrapper. Two further rows are derived offline by replaying the
judged hybrid run's logged twelve-candidate pools and verdict scores:
\emph{Judge-as-filter} keeps survivors in fusion order and \emph{RRF-fuse}
fuses the fusion ranking with the judge-score ranking at $k=60$. All three
see the same candidates and scores, so the comparison is an attribution,
not a benchmark. Because every logged candidate survived the configured 0.5
threshold, the filter replay is identical to fusion order by construction and
does not test filtering.

\textbf{On this corpus, lexical retrieval is strong.} BM25 \cite{bm25}
hits Hit@5 1.00 at about a millisecond per in-process call, reciprocal-rank
fusion \cite{rrf-cormack} matches it, and the two-stage BM25+graph-then-dense
pipeline reaches Recall@5 0.97 (Table~\ref{tab:retrieval}). Dense
retrieval with BGE-M3 \cite{bge-m3} misses one question (Q16), where four
near-duplicate sibling reports crowd the top-5 --- a confuser-density
failure, not a vocabulary one; graph search \cite{graphrag}, limited to
node-name and tag matching, is the weakest standalone signal. Nothing here
is a retrieval advance: three configurations tie at Hit@5 1.00, and the one
using only words leads on MRR. Q16 carries a filename token that also appears
in its gold path; excluding it leaves BM25 at 1.00 over the remaining fifteen
questions, so the tie does not depend on that leak.

\textbf{The measured result concerns ordering, not threshold-filter efficacy.}
Three configurations use the same logged candidate pools: fusion-only keeps
the original order (Hit@5 0.94), the judge-score reranker changes that order
(Hit@5 0.44), and the derived filter replay retains survivors in fusion order
(Hit@5 0.94). The filter replay matches fusion-only on Hit@5, Recall@5 and MRR;
it does not show a retrieval benefit from thresholding. All 191 recorded
segment scores exceed 0.5 (minimum 0.589), while Q14 has 12 fused candidates
but only 11 recorded survivors; the available logs do not identify why the
remaining candidate lacks a score. We therefore conclude only that, among the
recorded scored candidates, the threshold rejected none and the judge-induced
ordering lost gold documents. On the eight questions where the derived filter
and reranker differ, restoring fusion order recovers Hit@5 on all eight and
loses none (exact binomial sign test, two-sided $p=0.008$); Q7 moves the gold
from rank three to five, while four other questions move upward. The evidence
warns against using these verifier scores as a ranker on this confuser-heavy
corpus; it does not establish that the verdicts reliably identify evidence or
that filtering improves on fusion-only (Figure~\ref{fig:marginvariants}(b)).
The post-hoc comparison uses one checkpoint and sixteen questions; broader
calibration and replications remain necessary. Judging twelve candidates took
542\,ms per hybrid call versus 62\,ms for fusion-only, an observed difference
that also includes the judging call.

\textbf{A second corpus does not reproduce the Hit@5 collapse, but the ranking
effect is still visible.} Over ten
questions on the 79 retrievable papers of the 100-paper arXiv ingestion
(8{,}031 chunks logged; block~(b) of Table~\ref{tab:retrieval}), seven of
eight configurations reach Hit@5 0.90; graph search reaches 0.20. BQ03's
gold paper was not retrievable, so the observed denominator is 0.90 over all
ten questions and 1.00 over the nine with available gold. The judged ordering
keeps all nine golds inside the top five, but it demotes four of them from
rank one to rank two, and its MRR falls from 0.90 to 0.70 while the filter and
fusion rows stay at 0.90 (\texttt{exp10\_papers\_toollayer.json}). The
threshold-crossing harm therefore does not reproduce on this smaller,
less confuser-dense sample, while the ordering effect does. The graph result
is also configuration-specific: the bulk import bypassed the tagging path,
limiting what its node-name/path matching could retrieve.

\textbf{Thresholds and margins.} All 191 judged candidates from the
sixteen hybrid runs passed the configured absolute threshold of 0.5 (lowest
logged score 0.589), so at the threshold layer the gate filtered nothing on
this corpus (\texttt{exp1\_retrieval.json}). A sweep of the recorded scores
locates where filtering would begin to discriminate: cuts up to 0.70 keep
every in-pool gold and about 95\% of non-golds; 0.80 keeps 16 of 17 golds
while removing 21\% of non-golds; 0.85 keeps 13 of 17 and removes 40\%; at
0.88 the two retention rates converge (7 of 17 golds, 40\% of non-golds). The
signal is narrow and starts above the configured cut, and no answer-level test
of a higher cut was run (supplementary Table~S6). The margin sweep is a
separate diagnostic replay, not the configured control: it re-reads the logged pools
and keeps the top five candidates plus every candidate whose score lies
within a relative margin $m$ of the pool's best score. $m=0.15$ keeps 16 of
the 17 in-pool golds (Q11's gold sits deeper than 0.15 below its pool's
best) and $m=0.20$ keeps all 17; leave-one-out selection of the smallest
safe margin reaches 0.9375, missing only Q11, while a fixed $m=0.20$
reaches 1.00 (\texttt{exp6\_loo\_margin.json};
Figure~\ref{fig:marginvariants}(a) plots the sweep). Both constants are
replay parameters of this diagnostic; the calibration that would justify any
cut for deployment has not been run.

\textbf{Rephrasing shows where the fragility sits.} On sixteen paraphrase
questions (\texttt{exp6\_paraphrase.json}) the judged configuration falls
to Hit@5 0.5 (MRR 0.189) while judge-as-filter and fusion-only hold at
0.9375 (MRR 0.833) and dense retrieval stays at 0.9375; BM25 falls from
1.00 to 0.875 (MRR 0.958 to 0.755). Under rewording both the lexical baseline and the judged re-ranking lose
ground while the configuration that keeps fusion order does not: the weakness
is the induced ordering, not the verdicts.

\textbf{End to end, the modes differ in source-address markers.} Ten fixed
questions ran through three modes sharing one harness, model, neutral task and
output contract, but with different retrieval interfaces: platform KB tools,
file tools over the exported papers, and dense retrieval over an 800-character
replica index (4{,}528 chunks). All 30 answer texts name the gold document ID,
which is a provenance check rather than semantic grounding. Recognized
document-style citation markers appear in 9/10, 7/10 and 2/10 answers
respectively; seven dense answers also name chunks, reported separately.
The marker counts do not test whether citations resolve or support claims.
Other limitations include tool-availability aborts, a word-boundary false
negative, two platform retries and uncontrolled cache state.


E4 establishes a bounded ordering result: on the logged industrial pools,
judge-score re-ranking lowered Hit@5, while retaining fusion order among
recorded survivors matched fusion-only. This does not establish a benefit from
threshold filtering or claim-level citation support. BM25 reaches Hit@5 1.00
on the same industrial set (Figure~\ref{fig:retrieval}), so retrieval recall is
not a demonstrated contribution here. The deployment-path question is whether
source identifiers, evidence traces and answer workflows improve auditability;
the E5 marker checks only partly address that question, and the verifier
failure analysis does not establish calibration.
