\section{Discussion and lessons learned}
\label{sec:discussion}

The first implication concerns verifier placement, and it is the paper's main
design lesson: separating a content verifier's filtering role from its
ordering role is what allows the failure to be located. On identical logged
pools, re-ranking by the judge lowers Hit@5 from 0.94 under fusion-only to
0.44, while a derived replay that keeps the fusion order among scored
survivors returns the fusion-only metrics; rephrased questions separate the
roles further, with the judged configuration falling to 0.5 while both
fusion-order variants hold at 0.9375. Because all 191 recorded scores exceed
the 0.5 threshold and one Q14 fused candidate has no recorded score, the
comparison diagnoses the induced ordering, not a threshold-filter benefit. A
second checkpoint and its span-level variant fail the same way on the same
pools, and the second corpus does not reproduce the collapse, so the strongest
supported claim is that ranking by these checkpoints should not replace fusion
order under the tested confuser density; whether confuser density itself is
the cause remains an association, because the probe moved domain, language and
corpus size together.

The second implication concerns abstention. Similarity can retrieve related
material without answering a query. In this system, the answer workflow
instructs the agent to report the searched scope and say not-found when its
completed evidence review finds no support; the retrieval threshold never
played that role, since the graded probes retained 10 to 12 of their 12
candidates at every tier. The adversarial battery observed no fabricated
citations in eighteen final arms, but one partial answer fell outside the
binary grading scheme and each arm has a single final observation. Timeouts,
unavailable tools and incomplete searches must be reported as execution
failures, not conflated with semantic absence.

The third implication concerns transport. A reproduced oversized-catalog
response hid entries from the agent and led to a false refusal. Server-side
budgets, grouped views and pagination repaired the selected tested paths on
rerun. This supports the narrower point that transport limits can affect
retrieval outcomes, and that the enforcement layer honesty requires has
limits of its own; it is not a guarantee for every host or larger catalog.

Together the three implications separate document discovery from answer
construction: on the tested sets, retrieval-only recall does not by itself
determine whether an agent returns an addressable source, an explicit partial
answer or a refusal. The useful design lesson is to expose the retrieval trace
and the evidence returned to the answering step, while distinguishing a
completed search with no support from a search that did not complete.

The five research questions close at different evidence levels. RQ1 is
answered within one corpus, one checkpoint and one question family: on
identical logged pools the tested answer is to keep fusion order, and the
comparison does not validate threshold discrimination. RQ2 is partly answered:
the system combines server-side parse/index checks and an invoked content gate
with agent procedures for placement and answer reporting. RQ3 is answered as
an operational observation, bounded by 21 papers that never became retrievable
and by the use of a filename-independent membership measurement rather than a
per-document adjudication of placement. RQ4 reports tool-layer recall
separately from end-to-end source-marker checks; it does not claim similar
recall in every interface or isolate the gate's causal effect. RQ5 documents
observed abstentions, turn-dominated latency and a transport
failure/recovery, with no broad reliability or user-utility estimate.

More broadly, parsers, registries, indices and answer workflows benefit from
post-conditions that can be checked independently of success messages.
Catalogues and graphs support navigation but are not themselves evidence, in
keeping with provenance and auditability work in data systems
\cite{missier-prov-2013,cheney-provenance-2009}. The results remain bounded by
the tested corpora, developer-authored questions, one workstation and few
end-to-end observations. Larger independent question sets, repeated agent
trials, claim-level support assessments, a confuser-density-controlled
verifier evaluation that holds corpus, language and question set fixed while
varying near-duplicate density, operator studies of not-found reports and
crash-recovery tests are needed before stronger deployment claims.
