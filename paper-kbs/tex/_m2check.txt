Abstract
Enterprises accumulate regulations, failure analyses and operational lessons
as unstructured documents, and answering one engineering question requires
locating, reading and citing the right one. Retrieval-augmented generation
addresses the locating step, but its ranking signal measures topic related-
ness rather than evidence presence. QDCVR is a self-hosted knowledge-
base management system that governs content across all three actions. Six
subsystems—quality-gated parsing and ingestion, content-routed organiza-
tion, life-cycle management with three-source consistency audits, a document
graph, an operational-knowledge store with credibility tiers, and multi-lane
content-verified retrieval—expose 95 Model Context Protocol tools over one
storage layer, each terminating at an explicit contract rather than an implicit
prompt. We evaluate it on a deployed industrial corpus, a 322-document or-
ganization campaign, a 100-paper acquisition campaign and a cross-lingual
corpus. Twenty-one of 100 submitted papers never became retrievable; the
accounting gap between submission, storage and index coverage is reported,
not hidden. The gate exposes a same-domain score-inflation failure consistent
with known LLM-judge miscalibration: non-gold scores are indistinguishable
from gold (Mann–Whitney p = 0.26), judged re-ranking lowers Hit@5 from
0.94 to 0.44, while reusing the same verdicts as a filter restores 0.94 (sign
test p = 0.008). Component ablations return a largely null result, locat-
ing the binding constraint in catalog coverage and the reading layer rather
than in ranking. Functional campaigns confirm ingestion, deduplication, or-

1. Introduction
    Enterprises accumulate process regulations, failure analyses, and oper-
ational lessons as unstructured documents on shared drives. Answering a
concrete engineering question — why did edge delamination appear in an
electroluminescence inspection of a laminated photovoltaic module? — re-
quires locating the one report among thousands that records the decision
and reading it. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) addresses the loca-
tion step by embedding documents and the query into a shared vector space
[1, 2], and agentic stacks let a large language model orchestrate retrieval
tools iteratively [3, 4]. The dominant ranking signal, dense cosine similarity,
measures topic relatedness rather than evidence presence [5, 6].
    Three actions this task requires are addressed by three research commu-
nities, each solving its own and leaving the others’ problems intact. Retrieval
research optimizes finding: sparse, dense and hybrid rankers, and more re-
cently graph-based and hierarchical indexes, are judged by whether a relevant
passage appears in a top-k list. Knowledge management addresses organiz-
ing: classification schemes, controlled vocabularies and lessons-learned sys-
tems organize how documents are arranged and reused, but do not close the
loop to an answer a machine must justify. Document AI addresses reading:
layout-aware parsing recovers text, tables and formulas from scanned pages
[7], but a parsed document still has to be placed, indexed, governed and
trusted. In deployment the three concerns compound.
    The object all three actions govern is content: a knowledge-base manage-
ment system expresses that position as enforceable contracts rather than as
good practice. Three commitments follow, each implemented and measured
here. First, content decides placement: library membership, tags, descrip-
tions and graph edges are derived from what a document says, not from
the folder it arrived in, and the resulting catalog is the first retrieval instru-

                                        2
ment. Second, content decides admissibility: no similarity score, however
high, promotes a candidate into an answer, because the platform must itself
fetch the candidate’s content and judge it to contain direct evidence. Third,
absence of evidence is an answer : when verification eliminates every candi-
date, the system returns an explicit not-found report rather than the closest
text. Claims are therefore auditable, with every placement, verdict, cut and
lifecycle operation on record.
    QDCVR (Query-Driven Content-Verified Retrieval, v2.3), the self-hosted
knowledge-base management system built here, implements this position end
to end on a deployed industrial corpus and on acquisition and functional cam-
paigns. Six functional subsystems carry the platform: a quality-gated pars-
ing and ingestion pipeline, knowledge organization with content routing, tags
and a document graph, life-cycle management with three-source consistency
audits, an operational-knowledge store with credibility tiers, a multi-lane
content-verified retrieval layer, and a tool interface exposing the platform as
95 Model Context Protocol tools [3]; the system runs on a single worksta-
tion. On the acquisition side, of 100 papers submitted to a 100-document
campaign, 21 never became retrievable, and the design decision that mat-
ters is that a document which fails a gate is reported rather than stored
as an empty document (Section 8.2). On the retrieval side, the content-
verification gate is a better filter than a ranker: reusing the same verdicts
as a re-ranking signal lowers Hit@5 from 0.94 to 0.44, while using them as a
binary filter restores 0.94 (sign test p = 0.008), because same-domain score
inflation makes non-gold candidates statistically indistinguishable from gold
ones (Mann–Whitney p = 0.26) (Section 8.7).

2.2. Document parsing and acquisition pipelines
    Document AI supplies the components of an acquisition pipeline. Pub-
LayNet established large-scale layout analysis [19], unified text–image pre-
training improved layout-aware understanding of tables and figures [20], and
MinerU provides the open extraction pipeline for scientific and scanned doc-
uments that this platform drives [7]. Near-duplicate detection by locality-
sensitive hashing over shingles is the redundancy-control mechanism [21],
and provenance has mature foundations in the W3C PROV family [22] and
in database provenance [23].
    Parsing is usually presented as preprocessing, yet an extraction error
propagates into chunking, description, indexing, and every later citation and
is invisible in retrieval metrics. In our campaign 21 of 100 submitted arXiv
papers never reached a retrievable state (Section 4), so acquisition itself car-
ries a quality gate, duplicate check, index-verification step, and per-document
provenance record.

2.3. Retrieval: sparse, dense, hybrid, and RAG
     Retrieval-augmented generation grounds generation in retrieved passages
[1]; dense retrievers learn a shared query–passage space [2]; multi-granularity
embedders such as BGE-M3 combine dense and sparse signals [24]. Lexical
probabilistic ranking remains a strong baseline [25], reciprocal-rank fusion
merges ranked lists without training [26], and ranking is benchmarked on
MS MARCO [27]. Graded-relevance metrics are the evaluation instrument
[28], and surveys systematize the design space as retrieve-then-read pipelines
[6]. Vectors live in a persistent store with graph-based approximate nearest-
neighbour search [29, 30].
     The lexical baseline is not the weak part: on the gold-annotated corpus
BM25 reaches Hit@5 of 1.00. This paper does not compete on ranking qual-
ity. It treats every such signal as a candidate generator that must still be
confirmed against content (Section 6).

2.4. Structured and graph-based organization
    GraphRAG builds entity–relation graphs and community summaries for
query-focused summarization [31]; LightRAG reduces the idea to dual-level
retrieval [32]. RAPTOR recursively abstracts document trees [33]; the ac-
quisition, representation, and application taxonomy [34] joins knowledge-
graph work on embedding-based representation and link prediction [35], with


                                       7
property-graph stores as the substrate [36]. Structured organization also mit-
igates ungrounded generation [5].
    Our graph layer is deliberately thinner: Document, KnowledgeBase, and
Tag nodes joined by membership, tag, and weighted relatedness edges, built
from metadata that acquisition already verified. It navigates and expands
candidates and never answers alone, because keyword-limited graph search
is the weakest standalone signal on our corpus (Hit@5 0.56). It is therefore
exposed as one channel among several (Section 6).

2.5. Content verification, correction, and abstention
    Self-RAG trains a generator to critique retrieved evidence and its own
output [37]; CRAG adds a corrective retriever that re-evaluates and re-
queries when retrieval quality is low [38]; FLARE interleaves generation with
forward-looking retrieval triggers [39]; Kamath et al. [40] formalize selec-
tive prediction under domain shift; CyberBOT applies ontology-grounded
retrieval in a reliability-critical domain [41]. Verifiability measurement in
search [42] and citation-capable generation [43] frame the problem this pa-
per attacks: citations that are present but not supportive.
    LLM-as-a-judge. The verification gate is a locally hosted judgement
model [44], and the judging literature documents the failure modes here
precisely. MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena established the paradigm and their
bias taxonomy [45]; relevance judges agree imperfectly with human relevance
[46]; listwise re-rankers trade quality for instability at scale [47]; evaluators
favour generations resembling their own distribution [48]; and verifiers can be
gamed by optimization pressure [49]. The contribution is not the discovery
of judge miscalibration, but the operational quantification of same-domain
inflation inside a deployed pipeline and the decomposition of which judge
role is safe under which pool conditions (Sections 8.7 and 6.2).
    These approaches verify during generation with the generator itself.
This platform externalizes verification into a dedicated model that sees
only (query, document text) pairs and is consumed as a tool that any lane
or agent can call and whose verdicts fail closed, which makes verification
an inspectable, reusable service rather than a property of one generator’s
prompting.
    Abstention. Beyond selective prediction under domain shift [40], work
probes whether LLMs know what they do not know [50] and orchestrates
models to abstain on knowledge gaps [51]. Section 8.6 locates the abstention


                                       8
of this platform not in calibrated uncertainty but in the reading-and-answer
structure, a complementary architectural answer to the same problem.

2.6. Agentic retrieval and tool protocols
    ReAct established interleaved reasoning over tools [52] and tool-learning
extended this to self-supervised tool use [53]; the Model Context Protocol
standardizes tool servers as a machine interface [3]; agentic-RAG surveys
systematize the design space and list efficiency among open challenges [4];
Modular RAG frames retrieval as reconfigurable modules [54]. DeepRead
shows that structure-aware navigation over long documents improves agentic
search [55], which the librarian lane mirrors. The contribution is a policy
layer: per-lane tool whitelists, hard call budgets, and single-call server-side
orchestration for the hybrid lane (Section 8.6).

2.7. Positioning
    Table 2 compares representative systems along the dimensions a
knowledge-base management system has to satisfy.
    Each tabulated system owns one or two dimensions: GraphRAG organizes
a corpus as a graph, RAPTOR as a tree, Self-RAG and CRAG verify with the

across its lifecycle. No tabulated system combines a quality-gated acquisition
pipeline, a governed lifecycle layer, first-class operational experience, per-
citation verification, enforced abstention, budgeted multi-lane retrieval, and
a standard agent tool protocol.
    This paper contributes that combination as one design: contracts that
hold across acquisition, organization, and verification (Sections 4–8.6), not
capabilities implemented per component. It does not propose a better ranker.
On the gold-annotated corpus BM25 reaches Hit@5 of 1.00 while the judged
hybrid reaches 0.44; the value of the gate measured here is auditable, fail-
closed, per-citation provenance, not ranking quality (Sections 8.5 and 8.7).

3.1. Requirements
    The requirements below were derived from the deployment rather than
postulated, and each names the failure it prevents.

R1 — Parse heterogeneous enterprise documents. Process regula-
    tions, failure reports, scanned inspection records and journal articles
    arrive as PDFs, Office documents and images. The system must
    convert them to text it can govern, including scanned pages, tables
    and formulas, and it must make a parse failure visible rather than
    storing an empty or garbled document. The measured failure rate on
    a 100-paper acquisition was 21% and is reported as a first-class result
    (Section 8.2).

R2 — Organize by content, not by folder. A document’s home must
    be decided by what it says: library membership, tags, descriptions and
    graph edges are derived from the content.

R3 — Manage the collection over its lifetime. Libraries and docu-
    ments are created, moved, renamed, merged, split, deleted and re-
    indexed. Every such operation must preserve agreement between the
    document on disk, the tree registry and the library metadata, verifiably
    after the fact.

R4 — Retrieve with several independent channels. Lexical, dense,
    catalog and graph channels fail differently, so the system must gener-
    ate candidates with each, under explicit budgets, and expose which
    channel proposed what.

R5 — Do not promote a similarity score into a citation. A candi-
    date becomes an answer only after its fetched content has been judged
    to contain direct evidence for the question. When no candidate sur-
    vives, the system returns an explicit not-found report instead of the
    closest text.

R6 — Be operable and engine-agnostic. The platform runs on a single
    local workstation (RTX 5060, 8 GB, Windows), degrades rather than

                                    11
     crashes when a model or a subprocess fails, exposes long-running work
     as pollable tasks, and drives whichever agent harness the operator has
     configured.

3.2. Design principles
    Six principles shape the architecture, each enforced in the implementation
rather than left to prompt discipline.

P1 — Content-routed organization. Documents are organized by what
    they say, not by where they sat: placement, tagging, description and
    graph edges are derived from the parsed content, and retrieval starts
    from a library catalog rather than a directory path (§5).

P2 — Content outranks similarity. No similarity score promotes a doc-
    ument into an answer. A judging model must read the candidate’s
    fetched content and vote, and the platform fetches that content itself
    so an agent cannot argue for a document it has not read (§6.2).

P3 — Every module terminates at a contract. Ingestion ends in a
    checked post-condition, retrieval in a budgeted return value, verifica-
    tion in a rubric score, and management in an audit.

P4 — Fail closed. Missing text, an engine error, a timeout or an un-
    parseable verdict rejects the candidate rather than passing it, and a
    document that cannot be retrieved after indexing is a failed ingest
    rather than a warning.

P5 — Leave an audit trail. Every candidate, verdict, cut decision, tool
    call and lifecycle operation is recorded, so a claim about the system
    can be re-examined from evidence instead of re-argued from memory.

P6 — Engine-agnostic agency. Answer agents are pluggable harnesses
    (14 adapters, including omp, Claude and ACP-compatible CLIs); the
    platform constrains them through tool whitelists, budgets and prompts,
    never by coupling to a particular vendor.

3.3. Architecture and subsystems
    Fig. 1 shows the four layers. The client and agent layer contains the
web interface and the chat endpoint that adapts external agent harnesses;
requests are annotated with a lane policy before they leave this layer. The tool
layer is an MCP server that exposes the platform’s capabilities as 95 tools in
eight groups (knowledge-base CRUD and catalog, document read/write, file
system, parsing and task status, tags, search and vector index, knowledge
graph, and experience and persona management); the lane policy is a server-
side whitelist, so a lane physically cannot call a tool outside its set. The
domain service layer holds the tree and catalog services, the parsing scheduler
that manages the OCR engine as a supervised subprocess, the vector and two-
stage candidate services, the experience service, the graph service and the
resident judging worker. The storage layer holds the document tree with its
registries, the vector collections, the graph database and the task and session
databases.
    Three properties of this arrangement matter later. First, the retrieval
lanes of Section 6 are not separate programs: they are policies over one tool
layer, which is why a new capability is available to every lane at once and
why lane behaviour is comparable across harnesses. Second, long-running
work (parsing, indexing, graph construction, distillation) is exposed as a
pollable task rather than a blocking call, so a slow operation cannot stall an
agent’s turn. Third, the judging service is a resident worker for engineering
reasons, not architectural elegance: loading the verifier per request cost 10–
12 s against 1–2 s for a warm verdict, which is why the worker, its file lock
and its verdict cache exist (§7).

3.5. Object life cycle
    A document moves through five states. It is raw when it arrives, with a
content hash recorded. It is parsed when text has been extracted and has
passed the parse-quality checks. It is stored when the body, its description
and its tags have been written to disk and registered in both registries. It


                                                                     15
is indexed when the vector store and the retrieval indexes contain it, which
requires an explicit indexing call. It is verified when a retrieval probe returns
the document for a query derived from its own content. Only the last state
counts as a successful ingest; a document that stalls at stored is an incomplete
operation, not a warning.
    Libraries have a simpler life cycle: a library is active while it is addressable
and searchable, and merged or split when an operator consolidates or divides
it. Library metadata travels with the library when it moves, including its
distillation configuration, which is why the configuration is stored per library
rather than in the deployment file. Platform-level versioning is explicit: the
platform reports its version (2.3.0 in this deployment) and can update itself
from its repository through the same command-line interface that performs
the other operations.
    Re-indexing is where the object life cycle meets retrieval quality. It is
explicit, asynchronous and observable: it returns a task identifier, it can be
forced, and its post-condition is a segment count that matches the document
content. Nothing in the write path re-indexes as a side effect. This trades
convenience for the property that no document can silently be missing from
the vector store: the cost is that a writer who forgets the call leaves a stale
index, and the mitigation is the audit of §5.7, which detects exactly that
condition.

4. Knowledge acquisition: parsing, ingestion, and index verifica-
   tion
   Acquisition is the stage this design treats most defensively; this section
reports the audit numbers we have and the measurement gaps we do not.

4.1. Multi-format intake and parsing
    Upload starts in the browser at POST/api/parse/file-vt; the Nuxt
server forwards it to the asynchronous backend endpoint POST/api/v1/pa
rse/file/vt. The backend runs MinerU [7] as a hidden subprocess on
an ephemeral port, converting the source into structured Markdown: OCR
for scanned pages, layout-aware table recovery, and formula extraction. The
subprocess is reachable only through the manager object, which resolves
the assigned port at runtime; the service never hardcodes it. Its combined
standard output and standard error go to backend/logs/mineru-api.log
and never to a pipe—an earlier build failed on pipe closure with a bare I/O

                                        16
error, which appending to a file removed. Parse jobs are polled with a 600 s
timeout, so a stuck conversion ends in a recorded failure instead of blocking
the caller.
    The backend returns a markdown_path, a filesystem location rather than
document text; the proxy reads it back and writes the document into the
knowledge base through the tree filesystem service, updating the on-disk
Markdown, the tree index, and the per-library metadata file in one operation.
A path is transferred because parse output can exceed a single JSON response
and the proxy needs filesystem access for extracted images.

4.2. Quality gates before storage
    Every gate below runs before storage (Table 4): a document that fails
one is refused, not stored and indexed anyway. Exact-duplicate detection
hashes the source with SHA-256 and compares it to the stored records before
any parse is scheduled; near-duplicates are detected in vector space with
kb_find_duplicates(threshold=0.90); when the tool is unavailable, a
manual fallback channel matches filename and file size and probes the vector
store at 0.85. On the 322-part corpus the audit recorded 0 true duplicates
and 35 near-duplicate pairs, all adjacent parts of one paper—the expected
shape for a split document compared against itself.
    The parse-quality gate inspects the first 1,500 characters of the markdown
and refuses the parse on any of five signatures: OCR garbage (more than 30 %
garbled), binary residue such as null bytes or base64 fragments, headings
without body text, whitespace collapse, or a language mismatch between the
source and the extracted text.
    Storage has one rule: parsed documents are written with kb_doc_sav
e_parsed, never kb_doc_create. The parsed path transfers the complete
Markdown and copies all extracted images; the generic create path truncates
long content and drops images, so using it on a parsed document silently
loses both. Split parts go through the create path individually because each
is already a complete, size-bounded unit.

own structure, and a validator enforces a target utilisation of 0.85, refusing

4.4. Content-derived descriptions and tags
    Descriptions and tags come from body text the ingestion agent has actu-
ally read, not from filenames or directory positions. A description must carry

                                         18
four elements—subject, method or technology, scenario or problem, and key
data or conclusion—with at least two concrete nouns, and every claim is read
back against the body before storage; a mismatch rewrites the description,
never the body. Tags pass a blacklist filter, are normalised for case and lan-
guage, and are re-checked against a body sample: a tag absent from the text
is dropped, not kept for vocabulary reuse.
    An earlier audit is why this gate exists: of 26 descriptions checked against
their own bodies, 24 did not match—the boilerplate “Gutenberg front/back
matter” reached real chapters across a batch, and the librarian that reads part
labels selected two parts instead of seven. The wider catalog carries the same
defect, with 156 of 322 documents flagged for a broken part prefix. Descrip-
tion quality is therefore an empirical result, not an assumption: descriptions
are claims about content, not facts about it, and a claim that can be wrong
at ingest scale must be cheap to verify and cheap to repair—verification later
became a platform primitive (Section 6), and the acquisition audit ends with
descriptions upgraded 165/165 rather than a generation count.

4.5. Index construction and retrievability verification
    Indexing writes to three channels: a per-library vector collection, a lex-
ical BM25 index [25] over chunked documents, and a document graph in
Neo4j [36]. Ingestion success is judged by observable effects, not the tool’s
success envelope: a path that reported success returned an empty index pay-
load after a GPU out-of-memory failure, so a document counts as indexed
only with a non-empty collection name, the expected collection identifier, and
total_chunks ≥ 1. Graph construction is asynchronous, and task status is
not completion evidence—an earlier build left a task row stuck in running
while its artifacts were queryable. The step closes with an eight-point final
check: the stored copy read back at 500 characters, the chunk and graph
node counts, and three-layer metadata consistency across the tree index, the
library metadata file, and the files on disk.

4.6. Asynchronous execution, failure recovery, and observability
    Parsing and graph construction are non-blocking: each returns a task
identifier that a registry resolves across tool processes, so callers poll status
instead of holding a connection open. Batch ingestion is resumable and was
resumed in practice: restarting the client after an interruption in a 100-paper
run skipped documents already present and continued from the recorded
position. Per-paper parse time in the 50-paper campaign ranged from 16.3 s

                                       19
to 40.4 s over 26 subject domains, 1,115.5 s in total, an average of 22.3 s per
paper.
    That bookkeeping has a limit. Of 100 submitted papers, the state file
records 91 names, 79 were retrievable in the knowledge base at evalua-
tion time, and 21 never became retrievable. The reported failure mode is
formula-dense scanned documents, and a mid-run client crash was recovered
by restart, but no artifact counts parse attempts, parse failures, or the error
text of a failed document: the 91-versus-79 difference is an observability gap
we can report from the artifacts but not attribute from them.
    Cross-lingual ingestion shows the same gates holding without language-
specific tuning. The corpus is 400 Wikipedia pages per language in English,
Chinese, and Japanese, ingested in two rounds; the second round was bit-
identical to the first (Table B.3) and ingestion success was 1.0 for all three
languages in both rounds. Membership—where each page landed—is 0.875
for English, 1.0 for Chinese, and 1.0 for Japanese; storage completeness—
split parts summed back to the source length—is 0.975, 0.9559, and 0.9402;
and self-retrieval Hit@1, the score for the page’s own indexed text, is 0.975,
1.0, and 1.0.
    The 100-paper pipeline audit records what each gate observed—every
paper parsed and routed by content, nine per-paper ingest checks 100/100,
content and storage audits 322/322 and 33/33—but observability and re-
trievability are different ledgers: 91 papers reached the state file and 79
were retrievable at evaluation time (Table 8). What the gates do not buy
is accounting: with no counter of attempted or failed acquisitions, the dif-
ference between 91 recorded and 79 retrievable papers cannot be split into
parse losses and lost records. We treat a submission as knowledge only once
verification has found it.

5. Knowledge organization and lifecycle management
    Acquisition decides what enters the system; organization decides what
the system is. Here organization is a governed process rather than a folder
convention or an emergent property of embedding similarity, with three in-
variants: content decides where a document belongs, the disk, the tree reg-
istry and the library metadata must agree about every document, and every
operation that removes or relocates knowledge is preceded by a survey and
followed by a verification. This chapter describes their implementation and
where the three stores can still diverge.

                                      20
5.1. Content-routed placement
    Placement reads the parsed document, not its filename or its folder of
origin. The pipeline selects the library whose stated scope covers the content,
or proposes a new library when no existing scope does, writes the document,
derives a description and a tag set, and runs the same final check used by
acquisition before the document counts as placed. Two rules keep the catalog
usable as a retrieval instrument: an existing library is preferred over a new
one; and a document is placed once, because re-routing is an explicit lifecycle
operation rather than a side effect of a later ingest.
    The library tree supports one level of content grouping in which child
libraries are addressable through a parent. The implementation carries a
caveat we report rather than hide: the vector segments of child documents
are stored in the parent library’s collection, so a search that targets a child
library identifier returns no candidates. The shipped operational guidance is
to search the parent collection and to use the child libraries for navigation
and scoping rather than as search endpoints. This is a limitation of the
storage mapping, not of the routing rule.

5.2. Catalog and metadata registries
     Table B.2 lists the fields of the two registries that describe the same col-
lection: the global tree (.tree-fs.json) is the authoritative structure, and
each library carries a .knowledge-base.yml indexing its Markdown docu-
ments and their image directory.
     Agents read the catalog through a progressive-disclosure interface: the
listing returns names, descriptions and counts, document listings return
metadata without bodies, and only an explicit read returns content, at-
tributed to a specific document. A caller can therefore narrow a search to a
handful of candidates without loading a body, which makes the catalog lane
viable at 5–6 turns and 54–91 s (Table 7).
     Correctness of the catalog is three-source consistency: for every docu-
ment, the file on disk, its entry in the tree registry, and its record in the
library metadata must agree on existence, path, size and index state. The
platform verifies this agreement rather than assuming it: in the acceptance
campaign a renamed document kept its old vector segments, so the recorded
chunk count read 18 where 9 chunks existed, and the document matched
its own former path at 0.9738 similarity, high enough to be reported as a
near-duplicate pair. Only an explicit force re-index repaired it (18 → 9).
Metadata and index consistency are therefore two different obligations: the

                                       21
first is satisfied by a single write, the second is not, because the vector store
is updated by a separate call.

5.3. Tag taxonomy and duplicate detection
     Tags are the coarse, human-legible axis of the collection, and retrieval
by tag is a first-class operation. A tag registry holds the vocabulary; each
document’s tags are normalized, filtered against a blacklist of structural
strings (section headings, generic words such as “introduction”), and re-
checked against the document body so that a tag must occur in the content
it labels. Governance is a maintenance loop rather than a schema: no taxon-
omy is imposed on authors and the vocabulary grows with the corpus, so the
audit reports tags with zero referencing documents, accumulated from test
documents and chapter headings, and removes them only when instructed.
In the audited campaign 165 documents passed the tag gate with no failures,
and the live registry held 316 tags. We do not claim a controlled vocabulary;
we claim that the vocabulary is inspectable and that orphaned entries are
detectable.
     Deduplication is a collection-level question as well: the same content can
arrive through two ingest paths or into two libraries. The procedure is survey-
then-act — enumerate candidate duplicate groups at a similarity threshold
(0.90, with a manual fallback channel at 0.85), inspect each group, delete
nothing automatically — and the audited run illustrates why the survey step
matters more than the threshold. On the 322-document corpus the detector
reported 35 candidate groups with similarities between 0.90 and 0.975, every
one of them a pair of adjacent parts of the same long paper, and no deletion
was performed; a policy that deleted on detection would have destroyed 35
legitimate document parts. The same run confirmed zero true duplicates.
Cross-library duplication is detected but not merged automatically, because
merging two libraries carries provenance consequences and stays with the
operator.

5.5. The document graph
    The platform maintains a property graph with document, library, tag,
folder and entity nodes connected by membership, tagging, relatedness, iden-
tity and containment edges. Those edges derive from tag co-membership,
vector similarity above a threshold, and the verdicts produced by content
verification during retrieval. Adding one document to a library added four
edges in the audited run (4,675 → 4,679), one per assigned tag; a full build
over the 100-paper corpus produced 658 nodes covering 333 documents and
316 tags, and the 165-document intermediate state held 335 nodes and 1,671
edges.
    The graph keeps a deliberately restricted role. It is a navigation instru-
ment that expands a candidate set and surfaces documents sharing context
across libraries. It is never evidence: as a standalone retriever it reached
Hit@5 of 0.5625 on the industrial corpus and 0.2 on the second corpus, well
below the lexical and dense channels (Table 10). Graph-based organization
in this system is useful for discovery and weak as a ranker.
    Two implementation gaps belong here. Graph construction is not yet
guaranteed by the ingestion path: a document ingested through one agent
harness received no graph node, so a library reported two of three documents.
And batch graph builds have been observed to leave a task record in the
running state after the work completed, so task status cannot be used as
completion evidence; the graph itself must be queried.

5.6. Operational knowledge as a first-class collection

5.7. Integrity verification as routine
    Every property claimed in this chapter is checked by a verification pass
against the live collection, so that consistency, integrity and health are mea-
sured rather than asserted. The pass is read-only by default; repairs require


                                         25
an explicit instruction, and the report is produced first, because an audit
that repairs as it goes cannot report what it found.
    The pass is not ceremonial. It is how the two index-integrity incidents of
this deployment were found, a silently lost collection and a stale index, and
how the reorganization campaign was certified after the fact: 322/322 meta-
data checks, 165/165 description checks, 33/33 sampled consistency checks,
and 15/15 retrieval regression probes with no degradation. In the same cam-
paign the audit surfaced the failures reported above: the rename-time chunk
duplication, the missing graph node, and a task-status record that outlived
its task. A collection whose health is a command is a collection whose health
can be argued about with evidence.

5.8. What organization buys, and what it does not
    Organization buys candidate quality and auditability. Because the cata-
log is curated and consistent, an agent can select a library and a document
set without loading bodies, which is the mechanism behind the catalog lane
and behind cross-library discovery in an environment with nineteen libraries.
Because every placement, tag, description and deletion left a record, the col-
lection can be re-verified after the fact; the numbers in this chapter are audit
outputs rather than survey estimates.
    Three things organization does not do, and the paper does not claim. It
does not repair a bad parse: a document whose parse lost its tables cannot be
recovered by placing it well. It does not prevent divergence by construction:
the write path can leave the vector index behind, and only an audit and an
explicit re-index restore agreement. And it does not scale without limits: the
largest collection we report is 322 documents in five libraries, and the merge
and split operations are unevaluated. These are engineering boundaries.

6. Content-verified retrieval
     Every citation a lane proposes must pass a content-verification gate before
it may appear in an answer. The gate scores each retrieved segment on a 0–8
rubric (topic 0–3, scenario 0–3, evidence 0–2) and keeps a document only if
at least one of its segments reaches the acceptance threshold on that rubric’s
[0, 1] projection: 0.5 in general, 0.3 for enumeration questions, corresponding
to the rubric’s direct-answer band (Section 6.2). It processes at most six
references per call, takes the matched text from the server side rather than


                                      26
from the caller, and fails closed. No surviving evidence means the caller must
answer that the corpus does not contain the answer.

6.1. One query through the gate
    Q2 of Section 8.1 is “EL inspection shows edge delamination: root cause
and corrective actions”, asked through the public chat API with the failure-
analysis library pinned and the Hybrid lane selected.
  1. Policy. The chat endpoint attaches the Hybrid lane’s tool whitelist
     server-side, so the agent cannot call a tool outside it.
  2. Candidate generation (one call). The agent issues a single
     kb_hybrid_search. Two server-side channels run: a dense vector
     channel (top-30, cosine threshold 0.35) and a catalog keyword channel
     over the pinned library’s descriptions. Candidates merge on (library,
     document), agreed documents rank first, and the pool is capped at
     twelve.
  3. The gate. The server fetches each candidate’s text, segments it, and
     scores does this text contain direct evidence that answers the query in
     batches of at most six. The agent supplies no text, so it cannot argue
     for a document it has not read. On Q2 the gold report scores 0.846
     while five extruder-telemetry reports score 0.887–0.926.
  4. Cut and read budget. A document-level relative cut keeps scores
     ≥ best − 0.15 with a top-5 floor and a cross-domain anchor guard, so
     an out-of-domain score cannot set the cut line; the top five survivors
     return in full, at most 2 000 characters each. That read window is the
     agent’s only full-content channel; the rest of the pool survives as scores
     in the audit trail.
  5. The answer. Citations name the document path, the lane, and the
     engine score, graded P0 (direct evidence) through P2 (weak).
  6. The terminal paths. If the cut leaves no survivor, Lane A escalates
     once to Lane B; if Lane B exhausts its budget, the run reports not-
     found.
    Where the design fails, the same trace is explicit. Under the shipped
re-ranking role, Q2’s gold (0.846) falls to rank eight behind the five higher-
scoring telemetry reports, outside the five-document read window, so the
agent reads confusers (Fig. 8). Under the filter role the same pool, scores,
and verdicts leave the gold first: same gate, same evidence, only the role of the
induced ordering changes. This foreshadows the central result (Section 8.7).

                                       27
6.2. The gate contract
    The gate is a locally hosted evidence-classification model, the Laya judg-
ing service [44], invoked through a kb_laya_judge tool. Given a query and a
batch of references, the server fetches and segments each document and scores
every segment under one of three criteria: evidence (specific substantiation;
a topic match alone is insufficient), instance (the text contains a member
of the requested class, routed automatically for enumeration questions), or
auto. A document survives only if its best segment reaches the threshold, so
one at vector score 0.40 with a yes verdict survives while one at 0.95 that
the judge rejects is dropped.
    The score is documented as a 0–8 rubric (topic 0–3, scenario 0–3, evidence
0–2; ≥6 answers directly, 5 escalates to the librarian lane, ≤4 drops), with
the [0, 1] score as its machine-readable projection. That projection saturates
(the logged maximum score is 1.0000), one reason thresholds discriminate
poorly between golds and same-domain confusers (Section 8.7).
    Every operating constant is a shipped production default: the thresholds,
the six-reference judge batch, the hybrid candidate cap of twelve, and the
0.15 relative-cut margin with its top-5 floor. They were fixed during earlier
deployments on a different corpus and were not tuned on the evaluation
questions of Section 8.1. The paper states their status rather than selling
the operating point: on the evaluated corpus none of the three thresholds
ever fired, because every candidate the retrievers returned already scored far
above them (Section 8.7). The gate’s value there is fail-closed provenance
and the filter role, not discrimination; a deployment on other content would
have to re-tune these constants, and Section 10 records that as a threat
rather than a result. The classifier is a single, locally fine-tuned evidence
classifier whose checkpoint reference ships with the platform artifacts; it is
deterministic in our replays, and every verifier finding in this paper inherits
that choice, a scope limit noted in Section 10.
    Fail-closed semantics apply to every failure mode: fetch errors, engine
errors, and timeouts count as rejection. Survivors aggregate into an evidence
pack with per-passage provenance.

6.4. Selecting a lane is a budget decision
    The three lanes trade latency for recall under an enforced call and turn
budget, so the choice is how much budget to spend before the gate runs, not

                                     30
which retriever is better.
    What makes the lanes interchangeable is the gate. All three terminate
in the same contract: one gate, one fail-closed semantics, one evidence pack.
Replacing a lane changes the retrieval affordance and the budget, not the
meaning of a citation. Escalation follows a trigger, not a hunch: zero veri-
fied evidence escalates Lane A to Lane B exactly once. Query-shape auto-
selection is future work (Section 10).
    One caveat is honest to record. On the evaluated corpus Lane A oc-
cupies no distinct operating point, because Hybrid is faster and Librarian
more exhaustive. A two-lane deployment (Hybrid plus Librarian) is there-
fore defensible today, with Search retained as the pinned-library interactive
default; because the lanes share one gate and one abstention semantics, that
reduction costs coverage, not trust.

6.5. The two-stage entry point
    One configuration scored in Section 8.5 is not one of the three lanes:
kb_search_two_stage(query,kb_id,stage1_top_k=20,stage2_top_k=5
,enable_graph_expansion=True,score_threshold=0.0,balance_kbs=F
alse,slim=False). It serves callers that want recall without paying for a
verification lane, and the platform’s own retrieval skill names it the preferred
entry point for agent searches. Stage 1 builds a candidate document pool
from the lexical index and, when graph expansion is on, from the graph
neighbours of the matched nodes (≤20 documents); stage 2 runs the dense
retriever inside that pool and returns up to five chunks per document. A
cross-library search that returns documents from fewer than two libraries
triggers an automatic supplementary vector pass—the same lexical blind spot
the librarian lane corrects by escalating. It is fast because it never calls a
judge: Hit@5 is 1.00 at 77 ms mean (Table 10). That is also its limit, and
the reason it is not a lane: stage 2 returns chunks, so a caller that stops here
holds candidate text with no verdict attached, and any claim built on it is
unverified by construction. Only the gate turns a candidate into evidence.

6.6. The gate as a platform primitive
    The gate is a server-side tool rather than a prompt: kb_laya_judge(qu
ery,documents,criterion=auto,threshold=0.5,max_evidence_char
s=40000). The platform, not the agent, chooses the criterion and fixes the
threshold. Calls are serialized through a single judge worker with a 900 s
call timeout, because an early concurrent workload crashed the worker with

                                      31
a torch paging-file error; every verdict is logged with its query, references,
scores, and decision.
    That placement makes verification a layer property rather than an agent
aspiration: any lane, harness, or external client inherits the gate, and none
can skip or re-threshold it per query. Enforcement has a second half in the
lane policy, a server-side per-lane tool whitelist: a librarian-lane agent is de-
nied the vector and two-stage tools and cannot reach around the catalog-first
path it was given. Denial is a property of that lane, not of the platform: the
same two-stage tool is scored directly as its own configuration in Section 8.5,
and the vector tool backs Lane A.
    Externalized verification also distinguishes this design from verifiers in-
side the generator. Self-RAG and CRAG verify during generation with the
generator itself [37, 38]; the gate sees only (query, document text) pairs and is
consumed as a tool. It is therefore reusable and inspectable, and equally fal-
lible: the LLM-judge critique applies here as much as anywhere [45], which is
why verdicts fail closed, are logged, and are reported as evidence rather than
as truth (Section 8.7). Deployment cost is paid once: a twelve-candidate ver-
ification adds ≈0.5 s to a call (Table 10), and a cold judge load costs 10–12 s
(Section 7).

6.7. Honest abstention
     A threshold that never rejects a candidate cannot refuse a query. On four
out-of-corpus probes the judged hybrid still keeps 9–11 of its 12 candidates
(Section 8.6, Fig. 5), and the graded probes behave no differently: prompts
with partial coverage and prompts with none both keep 10–12 of 12 candi-
dates, with top scores up to 0.983 (Table 14 and Section 8.8). Because the
[0, 1] projection saturates, no cut point separates a supported answer from
an unsupported one.
     Abstention therefore lives in the reading-and-answering layer, enforced by
the lane contract at answer time rather than advised in a prompt. A caller
with no surviving evidence must report a not-found with its search scope:
which libraries and how many documents were searched, how the query was
routed, and which topics sit outside the corpus. The escalation rule makes
that state terminal, so an exhausted budget produces a report and not a
guess.
     Abstention is thus a terminal state evaluated like any other outcome: all
six out-of-corpus probe runs returned explicit not-found reports with zero
fabricated citations, even though the tool layer had handed them candidates

                                       32
(Sections 8.6 and 8.6). The division of labour is the point: the gate decides
what may be cited, the reading layer decides whether to answer at all, and
the measurements put the reliability in the second and not the first.

7. Agent interface, implementation, and non-functional properties
7.1. The tool surface
    The platform is exposed to agents as a single Model Context Protocol
server [3] carrying 95 tools in eight groups: knowledge-base CRUD and
catalog; document read/write; file system; parsing and task status; tags;
search and vector index; knowledge graph; and experience and persona
management. The count is the number of registered tool decorators in
kb-mcp/server.py, checked one-to-one against the function definitions they
register; the project’s own documentation had drifted to a lower number,
so we report the measured one. The interface is not a reduced agent-only
view: it owns no private data path. Writes go through the same HTTP
services the user interface calls, reads read the same .tree-fs.json and
.knowledge-base.yml registries the web server reads, and the MCP process
itself contains no HTTP code, only a client module.
    Three governance rules make a surface this wide safe to hand to a model.
First, read-path tools and write-path tools are disjoint families, and retrieval
lanes are granted the read family only. Second, a stored parsed document
cannot enter a knowledge base through the generic document writer: it
must pass the parsed-document writer (kb_doc_save_parsed), the single
entry point that carries parse provenance and forces the ingestion quality
gates to run. Third, long-running operations—a parse batch, a re-index, a
graph build, a meditation pass (the scheduled pass that rewrites the expe-
rience store’s rolling summary from accumulated questions, retrievals and
feedback)—return a task id immediately instead of blocking the session, and
are polled through the task-status tools backed by an in-process task registry.

7.2. Harness integration and lane policy
    One chat endpoint adapts fourteen agent harnesses to a uniform message
stream, so a vendor CLI, an ACP-compatible agent, and the platform’s own
engine all reach the same retrieval lanes. The lane policy of Section 6 is
enforced here rather than by prompt discipline: each lane is attached to a
tool allow-list, of which 21 entries are read-only MCP retrieval tools, and to a


                                      33
deny-list of more than 40 harness-internal tools (file editors, web search, sub-
agent spawning). The gate was observed working in the harness campaign:
on its first turn a model attempted to end the session early, the attempt
was blocked by the deny-list, and the retrieval that followed was unaffected.
Sessions are budgeted as well, with per-lane turn and call caps (Table 7); the
campaign ran 18 end-to-end scenarios, all of which passed with no fabricated
answer.
    Three operational findings from that campaign matter to callers. A trivial
ping prompt triggered a six-skill pre-flight before any retrieval, and the probe
was killed by a 30 s timeout; the platform’s default caller timeout is therefore
420 s, not the few seconds a request such as “list my libraries” suggests. The
streaming endpoint emits bare data: frames with no event names, so a client
must parse data-only frames instead of waiting for a named event. Finally,
engine choice dominates cost by one to two orders of magnitude at the same
quality tier: $0.90–1.80 per question for the agent harness, $0.11–0.36 for
the second engine, and $0.03–0.07 for the third. Wall clock was 50–176 s,
23–52 s, and 34–171 s respectively, but the third engine’s engine-side time
was only 15–83 s: its extra wall clock is harness cold start of 50–90 s per
call, not retrieval. One residual gap appeared in the same campaign: a
document ingested through the third harness had no graph node, while the
same operation through the agent surface created one.

7.3. Implementation and runtime engineering
    The backend is Python 3.12 with FastAPI. ChromaDB [29] stores one
HNSW [30] collection per knowledge base in cosine space; embeddings are
BGE-M3 [24], 1024-dimensional and normalized, encoded in batches of 32 by
a lazy-loaded singleton with an LRU encode cache. Neo4j 5.20 [36] holds the
document graph, jieba [57] tokenizes the keyword lane, MinerU [7] performs
parsing and OCR, and MCP is spoken over stdio and SSE.
    The enforcement described in Section 6 depends on engineering invisible
in the interfaces. The judging model runs as a resident worker behind a
machine-level file lock, with an LRU verdict cache and explicit CUDA-cache
release between workloads. A cold judge load costs 10–12 s per batch; the
resident worker answers in 1–2 s. The worker also removes a failure mode
met in production: when judge and bulk-embedding workloads ran inter-
leaved without cache cleanup, one end-to-end run degraded from 68 s to 27
minutes under the Windows display driver model’s paging behaviour. Judge


                                      34
calls are serialized by one lock, because two concurrent judgments on a sin-
gle GPU raise a native allocator failure; a call is held to a 900 s ceiling
and fails closed beyond it. MinerU is launched as a hidden subprocess with
platform-specific parent-death cleanup—a Windows Job Object with kill-on-
close, Linux prctl(PR_SET_PDEATHSIG), and a process group plus atexit on
macOS—and its stdout is redirected to a log file rather than a pipe, because
the pipe variant failed with a spurious Errno 22 once the reader closed. Be-
fore start, the backend probes its own port with a bare socket bind and refuses
to start if the port is occupied, so a surviving process cannot be shadowed by
a second one. Vector writes are serialized per collection, after one production
incident traced to concurrent segment handles; the keyword index updates
incrementally. Every HTTP client is constructed with trust_env=False, so
a corporate proxy cannot intercept calls to localhost.

7.4. Configuration, deployment, and reproducibility
    A single config.yml is the source of truth for ports and thresholds; en-
vironment variables override it. The mechanism is used in practice, not only
documented: in the harness campaign the services ran on web 6789, backend
8771, and kb-mcp 8000, the backend port being an override of the shipped
development default. Rate limiting (a general bucket and a smaller bucket
for expensive routes), authentication tokens for the MCP, user, and legacy
clients, and the retrieval thresholds all live in the same file. The ragctl
command-line tool covers setup, start, status, dependency, configuration,
and update operations. The system runs self-hosted on a single worksta-
tion: ChromaDB runs in-process, Neo4j runs locally on bolt port 7687, and
MinerU runs as a local subprocess, so no external service is required for a
deployment. The same file carries the higher retrieval threshold (0.55) used
by the experience subsystem, whose rolling summary of operational lessons
is regenerated by a rules-based pass every fifth ingestion. That loop is imple-
mented but not evaluated: Section 8’s experience evidence is one scenario,
and scheduled distillation at scale, approval, decay and application feedback
remain unexercised (Section 8.4). Source code and all experiment scripts are
public at https://github.com/kingdol666/rag-knowledge.

8. Evaluation
   We evaluate the deployed system on three corpora that differ in origin,
language and scale, using three metric families, and we organise the evidence

                                      35
into eight groups. E1 and E2, reported here, cover the stages that precede a
question: acquisition and parsing, and organisation, consistency and manage-
ment operations. E3–E8, reported in Sections 8.4–8.9, cover retrieval quality
and the role of the verdict, the operational-knowledge store, end-to-end agent
behaviour, verifier failure, non-functional behaviour, and the component ab-
lations. Numbers are reported against the corpus that produced them, and
results from different corpora are never merged.

8.1. Experimental setup
    Corpora. (i) Deployed industrial corpus: five knowledge bases in a live
deployment hold 42 documents, one of them containing twenty near-duplicate
equipment-telemetry reports that share prefixes, vocabulary and structure,
a deliberate stressor for near-duplicate detection and confuser density [21].
Sixteen gold questions and four out-of-corpus probes were run against it.
(ii) arXiv corpus: 100 submitted papers spanning about 25 domains; at
evaluation time the state file records 91 paper names and 79 papers are re-
trievable. Content routing placed them in five domain libraries holding 322
stored parts, and ten gold questions (BQ01–BQ10) target books referenced
by these papers, keyed by arXiv identifier. The live environment held 19
libraries. (iii) Cross-lingual ingestion corpus: 400 Wikipedia pages per lan-
guage for English, Chinese and Japanese, with 40 extractive cloze queries
per language whose answers occur verbatim in the source page. It is used for
ingestion only; we report no retrieval-quality numbers for it.
    Questions and gold annotations. The sixteen industrial questions
(Table B.4) were written by the authors rather than sampled from logs, a
circularity we state rather than hide; each identifies one or two gold docu-
ments by a unique filename fragment and one to three fact markers. Q16
names the event heatdecay092, a token that also occurs in its gold doc-
ument’s filename, so a retriever ranking on filenames alone could match it
without reading content; we keep the question and report the weakness. On
the arXiv corpus a question passes only if the gold identifier is traceable in
the answer or the tool trajectory and at least 60% of the gold keywords occur
at word boundaries, evaluated deterministically with no language model in
the loop.
    Metric families. Retrieval results use Hit@5, Recall@5 and MRR@5,
following passage-ranking evaluation conventions [27]. A graded measure,
nDCG@5 [28], was computed only inside one exploratory arm of the cross-
lingual benchmark that we do not report; no nDCG@5 value appears here.

                                     36
Grounding and key-fact verification use deterministic string tests: whether
the answer cites the gold document, whether at least 60% of gold key facts
appear at word boundaries, and whether part, section and table citation
markers are present. These checks are mechanical, not model-based faithful-
ness estimates such as RAGAS [58] or ARES [59]. The third family covers
wall-clock time, agent turns, tool calls, monetary cost, and the correctness
of refused answers.
    Controls. Deterministic lanes were re-run and compared field by field
with latency excluded, so a re-run could disagree only on content and order-
ing; model-driven lanes were repeated with the same model version and are
reported as mean with the observed spread. A fixed retry policy applied: an
empty zero-turn response or a transient gateway timeout was retried once,
which fired on two runs and succeeded both times. Before measurement we
repaired two corpus-integrity faults through the platform’s own operations:
one document missing from its vector collection was re-indexed, and one li-
brary whose collection had been silently lost was rebuilt. All measurements
ran on one workstation whose RTX 5060 (8 GB) serves the embedding model
and the judge worker, with every service local under Windows.
    What is deterministic. The tool-layer retrieval lanes, the grading rule,
the audits, duplicate adjudication and the cross-lingual ingestion pipeline are
deterministic: repeating them reproduces the same values, and cross-lingual
round 2 was bit-identical to round 1. The agent lanes, and anything derived
from generated text, are not, and are reported with their repetition spread.

8.4. E3: Operational knowledge and automated distillation
    The experience store holds operational lessons as first-class retrievable
documents: an entry is addressed, indexed and searched through the same
interfaces as an ordinary document, and it carries a credibility tier that
decides whether it may be shown at all (Section 5.6, Table 6). The design
claim is that a lesson about practice should be governed like any other content
object. The evidence collected for that claim is case evidence and we report
it as such.
    Three measurements exist. (i) One extraction campaign over a sin-
gle small knowledge base produced thirteen candidate drafts (benchmar
k-suite/results/experience_smoke.json: extract_candidates 13,
drafts_created 13). (ii) One end-to-end scenario was sedimented into

same judged scores enter three configurations: not used (fusion-only, Hit@5

documents are evidence — they were wrong as an ordering. This matches
the judge-miscalibration pattern reported for model-generated preferences
[45, 48]: an individually plausible verdict can be ordinally unreliable, and the
fix is the placement of the verdict, not its existence or its strength. Its price
is measurable: judging twelve candidates adds well under a second with the
resident worker, and the hybrid call’s latency surcharge over fusion-only is the
observed difference between 542 ms and 62 ms per call. Two caveats travel
with the test. It is post-hoc and descriptive — one of nine configurations
evaluated on the same sixteen questions — and the gate is a single locally
fine-tuned checkpoint (Section 6.2), so every verifier finding in this paper
inherits that choice.

8.7. E6: When the verifier fails
     Same-domain score inflation. The 191 candidates judged across the
sixteen hybrid tool runs — twelve per run except Q14, whose merge produced
eleven — carry no usable separation between gold and non-gold. Every
candidate scored at least 0.589, far above the 0.5 acceptance threshold, so
on this corpus the threshold rejects nothing. The 17 in-pool gold instances
(Q16’s gold never entered its pool) have a median of 0.88 and the 174 non-
gold candidates 0.86, a difference a two-sided Mann–Whitney test cannot
distinguish from chance (p = 0.26; Figure 7). Only five of the 17 golds rank
in the top three of the judged ordering and nine rank eighth or lower. Re-
ranking with the verdict therefore loses gold: the judged ordering reaches
Hit@5 0.4375 (7/16; 0.44 in Table 10), where the verdict as a filter, or within
rank fusion, holds 0.94 (Section 8.5).
     The verdict is a model [44], and a second model fails the same
way. The same 191 logged (query, candidate) pairs — a replay, not a re-
retrieval — were replayed through a generic natural-language-inference check-
point: 330M parameters, one question-as-context template, premises trun-
cated to roughly the first 500 characters after tokenization, with 59 of the 191
pairs hitting the cap (paper-kbs/experiments/results/exp7_second_sig
nal.json). It scores below the verdict (Hit@5 0.3125, MRR 0.1375), with in-
verted separation: gold median 0.0113 against non-gold 0.05285 (p = 0.120).
Truncation is not the explanation, because six of the ten gold documents that
fit the context window whole still score below 0.01; nor is it an out-of-domain
effect, as unrelated topics still score corpus documents at up to 0.9973, so an
NLI threshold would abstain on nothing either. A span-level replay inverts
harder: over the 191 pairs and 3,489 span inferences at roughly 320 characters
per span (paper-kbs/experiments/results/exp9_span_nli.json), the
max-pooled score is 0.4728 for gold against 0.91855 for non-gold (p = 0.0074),
with Hit@5 0.1875. Rank fusion of the two signals repairs neither (Hit@5
0.3125, MRR 0.111458). Replacing the judge checkpoint is not the repair.

9. Discussion and lessons learned
    The evidence in Section 8 supports a distinction that the retrieval lit-
erature tends to blur: finding a document and being allowed to cite it are
different operations, and the second one is a system property. The lessons
below are ordered from the retrieval layer outwards, because that is the order
in which we learned them.
L1. Vectors are fast; content is accurate, and verification has two
costs. The tool-layer comparison is the cleanest statement of the retrieval
subproblem: on a curated corpus a millisecond-level BM25 beats an 18 ms
dense retriever, and neither knows whether a document answers the question.
The gate converts ranked candidates into accountable evidence with per-
citation provenance. Its costs are temporal (about 0.5 s per dozen candidates
end to end; 1–2 s per six-document batch for the resident worker) and, on this
corpus, accuracy-related: 0.94 → 0.44 Hit@5 under same-domain inflation
when the verdicts are used as an ordering. Auditability is what the gate
buys today; ranking quality is what calibration must earn back.
L2. Latency engineering is turn engineering. With retrieval sub-
second, the only lever that matters for interactive latency is the number of

L3. The verifier is a model too, so calibrate it. The inflation analy-
sis is the paper’s cautionary result: a verifier inherits domain-internal blind
spots, and a threshold calibrated on cross-domain negatives (0.5) does not
separate same-domain near-misses. Fail-closed semantics and forced read-

probes sharpen the point: a generic entailment model fails too, but differ-
ently, anti-separating golds while still rejecting no out-of-corpus topic, and
a symbolic rule decides the numeric case that the shipped judge mishandles.
Verifier choice changes which errors you get, not whether you get any.

                                          58
L4. Honesty needs an enforcement layer, not a prompt. Lane sep-
aration works because the server physically withholds tools: a librarian-lane
agent cannot call the vector tool even if instructed to. The out-of-corpus
probes show the same asymmetry for abstention. A threshold cannot refuse;
it accepted every out-of-corpus candidate it was shown. The reading-and-
answer layer refused all six probe prompts with disclosed searches and zero
fabricated citations. A bare agent without the platform refused explicitly in
two of four runs, and in the others produced confident, unsourced, partly
wrong answers whose wrongness no audit trail exposes. Unassisted honesty
is a coin flip; enforced honesty is inspectable.
L5. Production incidents taught honest observability. Three early
incidents shaped the implementation: a corrupted index segment that made
one library silently un-retrievable across lanes, an out-of-memory indexing
run that returned success with an empty payload, and judge failures that
had to reject candidates rather than pass them. The common thread is that
every layer reports what it actually verified and every consumer treats the
unverifiable as failed. Table 16 consolidates the incident log into the failure-
mode catalogue we wish we had on day one, extended with the three defects
the organization and functional campaigns surfaced.
L6. The acquisition gate sets the ceiling for everything above it.
In the 100-document campaign, 21 submitted documents never became re-
trievable — the campaign record does not separate parse losses from record-
keeping losses, and the material is dense scanned work with formulas and
tables. The system’s value here is not that the rate is low, but that a loss
becomes a reported outcome: a document that fails a gate and is nevertheless
stored as empty or garbled would enter the catalog, receive a description, be
indexed, and be retrieved and cited by the same machinery that cites good
documents, with no signal anywhere that anything was lost. The same ar-
gument applies to a missing index or a stale description. This is why the
acquisition layer owns a post-condition rather than a warning, and why the
knowledge layer is a component of the system rather than a precondition to
retrieval.
L7. Organization is a retrieval budget. Content routing decides what
the candidate generator can see. A catalog that is curated and consistent
lets an agent choose a library and a document set from metadata alone,
which is the mechanism behind the catalog-first lane and behind cross-library
discovery in a deployment with nineteen libraries. An ungoverned catalog
produces the opposite: candidate generation degenerates into a global vector

10. Threats to validity
Scale. The retrieval corpus is 42 documents across five libraries; the cam-
paigns scale it to 322, and a second corpus adds 79. Absolute Hit@5 values
(BM25 at 1.00 on the industrial corpus) do not transfer to much larger col-
lections, where the hybrid candidate cap and the librarian’s shelf pruning
become load-bearing. The shipped benchmark suite is the scaling path; a
hundreds-of-documents run with an author-independent question set remains
the full answer.
Question set. Sixteen tool-layer and four agent-layer questions on the
industrial corpus, ten on the second corpus and forty cloze per language in
the campaign were developer-authored. Gold documents and fact markers
are released, but the samples are small and favour the corpora’s vocab-
ulary. Q16’s question embeds its gold document’s filename token, guar-
anteeing lexical success; the fix is scheduled, and because Q16 is the one
question on which the fusion stage loses a gold, the correction can only
tighten the reported decomposition. The arXiv documents are named
{domain}__{identifier}__{slug}, so routing accuracy against that prefix
is not an independent measurement, and we report coverage rather than
routing accuracy (Section 8.3).
Experimental controls. The tool layer isolates the gate: among its nine
configurations are retrieval without verification and the same retrieval with
the gate in either role, so the ranking-layer claim rests on a matched control.

                                      61
The agent layer lacked one: its four arms are three grounded lanes (retrieval
and verification) plus a bare agent (neither), which cannot separate them.
The missing arm, a grounded lane with the gate disabled and the fusion
order returned, was added late in revision, its protocol fixed before the run
(Section 8.6), plus a second ablation varying the search partition rather than
the gate (Section 8.3). Both inherit the same questions, libraries, engine and
turn budget; neither was tuned after seeing its results.
Evidence provenance is uneven. The acquisition, organization and func-
tional campaigns are audited runs with released per-check outcomes. The
experience subsystem rests on case evidence: a distillation campaign with
thirteen drafts, one sedimented experience, and an approval path never ex-
ercised. The 100-document ingestion is a derived state file, not a timed
per-document log, and its accounting is not closed: 100 documents were
submitted, 91 names appear in the derived state, and 79 are present in the
library. The whole gap is unattributed: no artifact records a parse status
per paper, so the 21 missing papers may have failed parsing, may have failed
a later gate, or may never have been recorded. A 21% retrievability gap is
therefore a bounded estimate of the parse-failure rate, not a measurement of
it, and no claim in this paper depends on the distinction. A system paper’s
most useful contribution may be the honesty of its ledger.
Judge and engine dependence. Scores and turn counts use one judging
checkpoint and one agent engine per campaign; lanes rather than models are
compared, but absolute numbers move with engine versions. The deployed
gate is still a single checkpoint, although the two strongest verifier findings are
no longer single-signal: a generic entailment model also fails (it anti-separates
golds and rejects no out-of-corpus topic), and a symbolic rule decides the
numeric case the shipped judge mishandles. Trained evidence-verification
models and learned signal ensembles remain open; the naive span-max probe
and the rank-fusion ensemble answer the two easiest upgrades negatively.
Design surface. Three lanes triple the policy surface an adopter must test.
The evidence supports two operating points: the catalog-first lane as the
exhaustive option, the orchestrated lane as the latency-tuned default. A
two-lane deployment is defensible today; query-shape auto-selection is future
work.
Consumer-side evidence. The honesty contract is evaluated on the system
only, that not-found reports are well formed and non-fabricated. Whether
an operator acts on a not-found report, escalates to a human, or ingests a
recommended source is not measured: the paper’s main missing external-

                                        62
validity leg. A study is a small operator trial over a fixed probe set with two
conditions, platform reports versus the same model’s unaided answer, scored
on the downstream action taken; probe set and transcripts are released so it
can be run independently.
Nondeterminism. Agent runs are stochastic; we report two repetitions
of the sixteen-cell grid (citation agreement 16/16, with wall time and turn
counts fluctuating substantially and not a distribution), retry only zero-turn
transport flakes, and release all per-run logs including timeout re-runs. Fact-
marker checks are automated substring matches; we additionally report de-
contaminated checks computed after removing answer lines that name a gold
document, and the out-of-corpus answers were audited manually. Determin-
istic layers are bit-identical across two rounds except for latency fields.

11. Conclusion
    QDCVR turns “the retriever found it” into “the system verified it”. Its
design position is that the governed object of a knowledge-base management
system is content: content decides where a document belongs and whether a
candidate may be cited, and the absence of content that answers a question
is itself an outcome the system must report. That position is implemented
as contracts rather than conventions (RQ1): parse gates that reject visibly,
placement rules that read the document, a three-source consistency audit
over disk, tree registry and library metadata, tool whitelists enforced server-
side, and verdicts that fail closed.
    The five research questions are answered by measurement rather than
assertion. Acquisition and content routing hold at the scale we tested, while
21 of the 100 submitted papers never became retrievable and the gap between
the submitted, recorded and retrievable counts is reported as an observability
gap rather than explained away (RQ2). Staged, multi-channel retrieval with
content adjudication reaches documents that one-shot vector top-k misses,
and the benefit comes from staged recall plus content verification, not from
the fusion of the two retrieval stages (RQ3). Verification costs well under
a second and buys per-citation provenance, but as an ordering it inherits
a same-domain blind spot that we quantify and partially repair; the failure
tracks confuser density rather than the checkpoint, domain, language or scale
(RQ4). Honest abstention lives in the reading and answering layer, never in
the threshold; end-to-end latency belongs to model turns; and engine choice
moves cost by two orders of magnitude at the same quality tier (RQ5).

                                      63
    The roadmap follows from the same ledger. Verifier calibration against
per-library null distributions, trained evidence-verification models, and the
verdict-as-filter role that the tool layer already supports are the immediate
next steps; larger-corpus evaluation with an author-independent question set,
consumer-side evidence that not-found reports serve their readers, and lane
auto-selection from query shape are the open empirical ones. The direction
is towards retrieval systems whose trustworthiness is an inspectable property
of the pipeline rather than a hope about the model.

aggregate number in the paper: BM25 and RRF miss at most one question
(Q16’s confuser family and Q7’s telemetry cluster account for the remaining
sub-1.0 MRRs); the judged hybrid’s nine misses are eight questions where
same-domain confusers outranked the gold (Q2, Q4, Q5, Q6, Q8, Q9, Q11,
Q12) plus Q16, where the confuser family crowded the gold out upstream;
and judge-as-filter restores the gold on every one of the eight judging losses
while inheriting Q16’s upstream failure — the decomposition that Fig. 4(b)
aggregates and Fig. 8 dissects one query at a time.
