Abstract

Enterprises accumulate process regulations, failure analyses, and operational
lessons as unstructured documents spread across shared drives, and an-
swering one engineering question requires locating, reading and citing the
right document. Retrieval-augmented generation addresses the locating step,
but its dominant ranking signal measures topic relatedness rather than evi-
dence presence. We present QDCVR, a self-hosted knowledge-base manage-
ment system that governs content across all three actions. Six functional
subsystems--a quality-gated parsing and ingestion pipeline, content-routed
organization, life-cycle management with three-source consistency audits, a
document graph, an operational-knowledge store with credibility tiers, and
multi-lane content-verified retrieval--expose 95 Model Context Protocol tools
over one storage layer, and every subsystem terminates at an explicit contract
rather than an implicit prompt. We evaluate the system on a deployed indus-
trial corpus, a 322-document organization campaign, a 100-paper acquisition
campaign, a cross-lingual corpus, and functional and non-functional cam-
paigns. Twenty-one of 100 submitted papers were never retrievable from the
arXiv corpus, and the accounting gap between submission, storage and in-
dex coverage is reported rather than hidden. The verification gate exposes a
same-domain score-inflation failure consistent with known LLM-judge mis-
calibration: non-gold scores are indistinguishable from gold (Mann­Whitney
p = 0.26), judged re-ranking lowers Hit@5 from 0.94 to 0.44, while reusing
the same verdicts as a filter restores 0.94 (sign test p = 0.008). Functional

1. Introduction

    Enterprises accumulate process regulations, failure analyses, and opera-
tional lessons as unstructured documents spread across shared drives. An-
swering a concrete engineering question -- why did edge delamination appear
in an electroluminescence inspection of a laminated photovoltaic module? --
requires locating the one report among thousands that records the decision,
reading it, and citing it. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) addresses
the location step by embedding documents and the query into a shared vec-
tor space [1, 2], and agentic stacks let a large language model orchestrate
retrieval tools iteratively [3, 4]. Yet the dominant ranking signal, dense co-
sine similarity, measures topic relatedness rather than evidence presence. A
document that merely discusses similar materials can outrank the one doc-
ument that contains the answer, and a fluent generator can synthesize an
answer from the wrong source [5, 6].

    Three research communities attack the three actions this task requires,
and each leaves the others' problems intact. Retrieval research optimizes find-
ing: sparse, dense and hybrid rankers, and more recently graph-based and
hierarchical indexes, are evaluated by whether a relevant passage appears
in a top-k list. Knowledge management addresses organizing: classification
schemes, controlled vocabularies and lessons-learned systems govern how an
organization's documents are arranged and reused, but they do not close the
loop to an answer that a machine must justify. Document AI addresses read-
ing: layout-aware parsing recovers text, tables and formulas from scanned
pages [7], but a parsed document still has to be placed, indexed, governed
and trusted. In a deployment these three concerns are not independent: a
parse failure that is not treated as a failure becomes a silently missing doc-
ument; a catalog that is not governed makes candidate generation arbitrary;
and a ranking score that is treated as evidence produces confident citations

                                                2
to documents that were never read.
    This paper takes the position that the object all three actions should

govern is content, and that a knowledge-base management system should
express that position as enforceable contracts rather than as good practice.
Three commitments follow, and each one is implemented and measured here.
First, content decides placement: library membership, tags, descriptions and
graph edges are derived from what a document says, not from the folder it
arrived in, and the resulting catalog is the system's first retrieval instrument.
Second, content decides admissibility: no similarity score, however high, pro-
motes a candidate into an answer, because the candidate's fetched content
must be judged to contain direct evidence first, and the platform fetches
that content itself. Third, absence of evidence is an answer : when verifi-
cation eliminates every candidate, the system returns an explicit not-found
report rather than the closest text. The consequence of the three commit-
ments is that the system's claims are auditable: every placement, verdict, cut
and lifecycle operation leaves a record, and the collection can be re-verified
afterwards.

    We built QDCVR (v2.3), a self-hosted knowledge-base management sys-
tem that implements this position end to end, and we evaluate it on a de-
ployed industrial corpus and on acquisition and functional campaigns. The
system spans six functional subsystems -- a quality-gated parsing and in-
gestion pipeline, knowledge organization with content routing, tags and a
document graph, life-cycle management with three-source consistency audits,
an operational-knowledge store with credibility tiers, a multi-lane content-
verified retrieval layer, and a tool interface that exposes the whole platform
as 95 Model Context Protocol tools [3] -- and it runs on a single workstation.
Two results frame the paper. On the acquisition side, the pipeline is where
trust is won or lost: of 100 papers submitted to a 100-document campaign,
21 failed to parse, and the design decision that matters is that a failed parse
is reported rather than stored as an empty document (Section 8.2). On the
retrieval side, the content-verification gate that the system is built around
turns out to be a better filter than a ranker: reusing the same verdicts as
a re-ranking signal lowers Hit@5 from 0.94 to 0.44, while using them as a
binary filter restores 0.94 (sign test p = 0.008), and the underlying cause is
a same-domain score inflation in which non-gold candidates are statistically
indistinguishable from gold ones (Mann­Whitney p = 0.26) (Section 8.7).
We report both outcomes, including the one that is negative for our own
design.

1.1. Research questions and reading guide
    The paper is organized as a design-to-evidence arc around five research

questions, each answered by one evidence group and reported in one place
(Table 1).

RQ1 (Enforceable contracts). How can the three content commitments
       be turned into system contracts that hold across retrieval strategies and
       independent agent harnesses, rather than into prompt conventions?

RQ2 (Acquisition and organization at scale). Do quality-gated acqui-
       sition and content-routed organization produce collections that are us-
       able and internally consistent when they grow, and what does the gate
       reject?

RQ3 (Content-verified retrieval). Does staged, multi-channel retrieval
       with content verification outperform one-shot vector top-k and unver-
       ified fusion -- and by which mechanism?

RQ4 (Cost and benefit of verification). What does the gate cost and
       buy, and is its failure mode structural rather than an artefact of one
       checkpoint, domain or language?

RQ5 (Operational contract). Where do honest abstention, latency, cost
       and stability actually live in a running system?

Section 6 presents the retrieval lanes and the verification gate, walking one
real question end to end before the measurements begin, and states what the

2. Related work

2.1. Knowledge bases, knowledge organization, and knowledge management
    Large public knowledge bases such as DBpedia and YAGO are built by

extracting structured facts from semi-structured sources and maintaining
them under an ontology [9, 10]. The enterprise content management liter-
ature treats organizational content as a management problem rather than
an extraction problem: ECM integrates content, process, and records per-
spectives [11], and later work characterizes the field's research agenda [12].
Organizational knowledge creation describes how tacit experience becomes
explicit, shareable knowledge through socialization, externalization, combi-
nation, and internalization, sustained by a shared context [13, 14]. Com-
plementary strands study metadata quality in digital repositories [15], the
emergent vocabulary of collaborative tagging [16], human-in-the-loop review
of machine-produced labels [17], and the inspectability of automated deci-
sions [18].

    This literature names the problems our design must answer -- inconsis-
tent metadata, uncontrolled vocabulary, unverified labels, experience that
never becomes reusable -- but it treats them as management and social
problems and rarely closes the loop to machine answering. That loop is the
contribution here: the content-derived metadata that organizes a base also
constrains retrieval, verification, and abstention (Sections 5­6.2).

2.2. Document parsing and acquisition pipelines
    Document AI supplies the components of an acquisition pipeline. Pub-

LayNet established large-scale layout analysis [19], and unified text­image
pre-training improved layout-aware understanding of tables and figures [20].
MinerU provides an open extraction pipeline for scientific and scanned docu-
ments and is the parser this platform drives [7]. Near-duplicate detection by
locality-sensitive hashing over shingles is the standard mechanism for redun-
dancy control at crawl scale [21], and provenance has mature foundations in
the W3C PROV family [22] and in database provenance [23].

                                                7
    Parsing is usually presented as preprocessing. We treat it as a precon-
dition: an extraction error propagates into chunking, description, indexing,
and every later citation, and it is invisible in retrieval metrics. In our own
campaign 21 of 100 submitted arXiv papers never reached a retrievable state
(Section 4). Acquisition therefore carries its own quality gate, duplicate
check, index-verification step, and per-document provenance record.

2.3. Retrieval: sparse, dense, hybrid, and RAG
    Retrieval-augmented generation grounds generation in retrieved passages

[1]; dense retrievers learn a shared query­passage space [2]; multi-lingual,
multi-granularity embedders such as BGE-M3 combine dense and sparse
signals [24]. Lexical probabilistic ranking remains a strong baseline [25],
reciprocal-rank fusion merges ranked lists without training [26], and rank-
ing in the large-data regime is benchmarked on MS MARCO [27]. Graded-
relevance metrics are the standard evaluation instrument [28], and surveys
systematize the design space as retrieve-then-read pipelines [6]. The platform
stores vectors in a persistent store with graph-based approximate nearest-
neighbour search [29, 30].

    On our gold-annotated corpus the lexical baseline is not the weak part:
BM25 reaches Hit@5 of 1.00. This paper does not compete on ranking quality.
It treats all of these signals as candidate generators whose output must still
be confirmed against content (Section 6).

2.4. Structured and graph-based organization
    GraphRAG builds entity­relation graphs and community summaries

for query-focused summarization [31]; LightRAG reduces the idea to
dual-level retrieval [32]; RAPTOR recursively abstracts document trees
[33]. Knowledge-graph work supplies the representational vocabulary --
embedding-based representation and link prediction [34], and the acquisi-
tion, representation, and application taxonomy [35] -- with property-graph
stores as the common substrate [36]. Structured organization is also moti-
vated as a mitigation for ungrounded generation [5].

    Our graph layer is deliberately thinner than these systems: Document,
KnowledgeBase, and Tag nodes with membership, tag, and weighted relat-
edness edges, built from metadata that acquisition already verified. It navi-
gates and expands candidates and never answers alone. The measured basis
is that keyword-limited graph search is the weakest standalone signal on our

                                                8
corpus (Hit@5 0.56), so the platform exposes it as one channel among several
(Section 6).

2.5. Content verification, correction, and abstention
    Self-RAG trains a generator to critique retrieved evidence and its own

output [37]; CRAG adds a corrective retriever that re-evaluates and re-
queries when retrieval quality is low [38]; FLARE interleaves generation with
forward-looking retrieval triggers [39]; Kamath et al. [40] formalize selective
prediction under domain shift; and CyberBOT applies ontology-grounded
retrieval in a reliability-critical domain [41]. Verifiability measurement in
generative search [42] and citation-capable generation [43] frame the prob-
lem this paper attacks: citations that are present but not supportive.

    LLM-as-a-judge. The verification gate is a locally hosted judgement
model [44], and the judging literature documents exactly the failure modes
our measurements expose. MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena established the
paradigm and its bias taxonomy [45]; LLM relevance judges agree imper-
fectly with human relevance [46]; listwise LLM re-rankers trade quality for
instability at scale [47]; evaluators favour generations that resemble their own
distribution [48]; and verifiers can be gamed by optimization pressure [49].
Our contribution is not the discovery that judges miscalibrate. It is the op-
erational quantification of same-domain inflation inside a deployed pipeline
and the decomposition of which judge role -- filter, ordering, or threshold
-- is safe under which pool conditions (Sections 8.7 and 6.2).

    These approaches verify during generation with the generator itself.
This platform externalizes verification into a dedicated model that sees only
(query, document text) pairs and is consumed as a tool: any lane, agent,
or external system can call it, and its verdicts fail closed. Verification thus
becomes an inspectable, reusable service rather than a property of one
generator's prompting.

    Abstention. Beyond selective prediction under domain shift [40], recent
work probes whether LLMs know what they do not know [50] and orches-
trates models to abstain on knowledge gaps [51]. Section 8.6 locates the
abstention of this platform not in calibrated uncertainty but in the reading-
and-answer structure -- a complementary, architectural answer to the same
problem.

2.6. Agentic retrieval and tool protocols
    ReAct established interleaved reasoning and acting over tools [52]; tool-

learning extended it to self-supervised tool use [53]; the Model Context Proto-
col standardizes tool servers as a machine interface [3]; agentic-RAG surveys
systematize the design space and list efficiency among its open challenges [4];
and Modular RAG frames retrieval systems as reconfigurable modules [54].
DeepRead shows that structure-aware navigation over long documents im-
proves agentic search [55], which matches the document-coordinate reading
of the librarian lane. The contribution here is a policy layer rather than a new
reasoning scheme: per-lane tool whitelists enforced by the server, hard call
budgets, and single-call server-side orchestration for the hybrid lane, aimed
at the turn count that dominates agentic latency (Section 8.6).

3.1. Requirements
    The requirements below were derived from the deployment rather than

postulated; each one names the failure it prevents.

R1 -- Parse heterogeneous enterprise documents. Process regula-
       tions, failure reports, scanned inspection records and journal articles
       arrive as PDFs, Office documents and images. The system must
       convert them to text it can govern, including scanned pages, tables
       and formulas, and it must make a parse failure visible rather than
       storing an empty or garbled document. The measured failure rate on
       a 100-paper acquisition was 21% and is reported as a first-class result
       (Section 8.2).

R2 -- Organize by content, not by folder. A document's home must
       be decided by what it says. Library membership, tags, descriptions
       and graph edges are derived from the content, because folder paths
       encode who filed a document rather than what it contains.

R3 -- Manage the collection over its lifetime. Libraries and docu-
       ments are created, moved, renamed, merged, split, deleted and re-
       indexed. Every such operation must preserve agreement between the
       document on disk, the tree registry and the library metadata, and the
       agreement must be verifiable after the fact.

R4 -- Retrieve with several independent channels. Lexical, dense,
       catalog and graph channels fail differently, so the system must be able
       to generate candidates with each of them, under explicit budgets, and
       expose which channel proposed what.

R5 -- Do not promote a similarity score into a citation. A candi-
       date becomes an answer only after its fetched content has been judged

                                               12
       to contain direct evidence for the question. When no candidate sur-
       vives, the system returns an explicit not-found report instead of the
       closest text.

R6 -- Be operable and engine-agnostic. The platform runs on a single
       local workstation (RTX 5060, 8 GB, Windows), degrades rather than
       crashes when a model or a subprocess fails, exposes long-running work
       as pollable tasks, and drives whichever agent harness the operator has
       configured rather than being bound to one vendor.

3.2. Design principles
    Six principles shape the architecture. They are stated as commitments

because each one is enforced somewhere in the implementation rather than
left to prompt discipline.

P1 -- Content-routed organization. Documents are organized by what
       they say, not by where they sat: placement, tagging, description and
       graph edges are derived from the parsed content, and retrieval starts
       from a library catalog rather than a directory path (§5).

P2 -- Content outranks similarity. No similarity score, however high,
       promotes a document into an answer. A judging model must read the
       candidate's fetched content and vote, and the platform fetches that
       content itself so an agent cannot argue for a document it has not read
       (§6.2).

P3 -- Every module terminates at a contract. Ingestion ends in a
       checked post-condition, retrieval in a budgeted return value, verifica-
       tion in a rubric score, and management in an audit. A module whose
       result cannot be checked is a module that cannot be trusted.

P4 -- Fail closed. Missing text, an engine error, a timeout or an un-
       parseable verdict rejects the candidate rather than passing it, and a
       document that cannot be retrieved after indexing is a failed ingest
       rather than a warning.

P5 -- Leave an audit trail. Every candidate, verdict, cut decision, tool
       call and lifecycle operation is recorded, so that a claim about the system
       can be re-examined from evidence instead of re-argued from memory.

P6 -- Engine-agnostic agency. Answer agents are pluggable harnesses
       (14 adapters, including omp, Claude and ACP-compatible CLIs); the

3.3. Architecture and subsystems

    Fig. 1 shows the four layers. The client and agent layer contains the
web interface and the chat endpoint that adapts external agent harnesses;
requests are annotated with a lane policy before they leave this layer. The tool
layer is an MCP server that exposes the platform's capabilities as 95 tools in
eight groups (knowledge-base CRUD and catalog, document read/write, file
system, parsing and task status, tags, search and vector index, knowledge
graph, and experience and persona management); the lane policy is a server-
side whitelist, so a lane physically cannot call a tool outside its set. The
domain service layer holds the tree and catalog services, the parsing scheduler
that manages the OCR engine as a supervised subprocess, the vector and two-
stage candidate services, the experience service, the graph service and the
resident judging worker. The storage layer holds the document tree with its
registries, the vector collections, the graph database and the task and session
databases.

    Three properties of this arrangement matter for the rest of the paper.
First, the retrieval lanes of Section 6 are not separate programs: they are
policies over one tool layer, which is why a new capability is available to
every lane at once and why lane behaviour is comparable across harnesses.
Second, long-running work (parsing, indexing, graph construction, distilla-
tion) is exposed as a pollable task rather than a blocking call, so a slow
operation cannot stall an agent's turn. Third, the judging service is a resi-
dent worker for engineering reasons, not architectural elegance: loading the
verifier per request cost 10­12 s against 1­2 s for a warm verdict, which is
why the worker, its file lock and its verdict cache exist (§7).

ment it, the design decision that shapes it, and the experiment that provides
evidence for it. The table is the paper's reading guide: Sections 4­7 describe
these rows in order, and Section 8 evaluates them.

3.5. Object life cycle

    A document moves through five states. It is raw when it arrives, with a
content hash recorded. It is parsed when text has been extracted and has
passed the parse-quality checks. It is stored when the body, its description
and its tags have been written to disk and registered in both registries. It
is indexed when the vector store and the retrieval indexes contain it, which
requires an explicit indexing call. It is verified when a retrieval probe returns
the document for a query derived from its own content. Only the last state

                                    16
counts as a successful ingest; a document that stalls at stored is an incomplete
operation, not a warning.

    Libraries have a simpler life cycle: a library is active while it is addressable
and searchable, and it becomes merged or split when an operator consolidates
or divides it. Library metadata travels with the library when it moves, in-
cluding its distillation configuration, which is why the configuration is stored
per library rather than in the deployment file. Platform-level versioning is
explicit: the platform reports its version (2.3.0 in this deployment) and can
update itself from its repository through the same command-line interface
that performs the other operations.

    Re-indexing deserves its own paragraph, because it is where the object
life cycle meets retrieval quality. Re-indexing is explicit, asynchronous and
observable: it returns a task identifier, it can be forced, and its post-condition
is a segment count that matches the document content. Nothing in the write
path re-indexes as a side effect. This is a deliberate trade of convenience for
the property that no document can silently be missing from the vector store:
the cost is that a writer who forgets the call leaves a stale index, and the
mitigation is the audit of §5.8, which detects exactly that condition.

4. Knowledge acquisition: parsing, ingestion, and index verifica-
    tion

    Acquisition is where a document becomes knowledge the system may
later answer from, and it is the stage this design treats most defensively. The
pipeline runs its gates in a fixed order, and a document that fails one is refused
rather than stored and indexed anyway. This section follows the stages in
execution order, with the audit numbers we have and the measurement gaps
we do not.

4.1. Multi-format intake and parsing
    Upload starts in the browser at POST /api/parse/file-vt. The

Nuxt server forwards the payload to the backend parser endpoint POST
/api/v1/parse/file/vt, which is asynchronous and returns a task iden-
tifier rather than a result. The backend then runs MinerU [7] as a hidden
subprocess on an ephemeral port to convert the source into structured
Markdown: OCR for scanned pages, layout-aware recovery of tables, and
formula extraction. The subprocess is reachable only through the manager

                                               17
object, which resolves the assigned port at runtime; the service never hard-
codes it. Its combined standard output and standard error are redirected
to backend/logs/mineru-api.log and never to a pipe--an earlier build
failed on pipe closure with a bare I/O error, and appending to a file removed
the failure mode. Parse jobs are polled with a 600 s timeout, so a stuck
conversion terminates in a recorded failure instead of blocking the caller.

    The backend returns a markdown_path--a filesystem location, not doc-
ument text. The proxy layer reads that file back and writes the document
into the knowledge base through the tree filesystem service, which updates
the on-disk Markdown, the tree index, and the per-library metadata file in
one operation. Keeping the transfer as a path rather than a response body
matters for large scanned documents: the parse output can exceed what a
single JSON response should carry, and the proxy already needs filesystem
access to place extracted images.

4.2. Quality gates before storage

    Before any parse is scheduled, exact-duplicate detection hashes the source
with SHA-256 and compares it against the stored records. Near-duplicates
are then detected in vector space: kb_find_duplicates(threshold=0.90).
A manual fallback channel runs the same check at a 0.85 similarity threshold,
combining a filename and file-size match with a vector probe when the tool is
unavailable. On the 322-part corpus the audit recorded 0 true duplicates and
35 near-duplicate pairs, all of them adjacent parts of the same paper, which
is the expected shape when a split document is compared against itself.

    The parse-quality gate inspects the first 1,500 characters of the markdown
and refuses the parse on any of five signatures: OCR garbage (more than 30 %
garbled), binary residue such as null bytes or base64 fragments, headings
without body text, whitespace collapse, or a language mismatch between
the source and the extracted text. Refusal is terminal for that attempt; the
operator either re-parses with a different OCR mode or reports the source as
unusable.

    Storage has one rule: parsed documents are written with kb_doc_save_parsed,
never kb_doc_create. The parsed path transfers the complete Markdown
and copies all extracted images; the generic create path truncates long
content and drops images, so using it on a parsed document silently loses
both. Parts produced by the split gate are stored individually through the
create path because each part is already a complete, size-bounded unit.

4.3. Structure-aware splitting and addressability

    Documents longer than a 30,000-character ceiling do not enter the knowl-
edge base as single files. A splitting pass proposes boundaries from the doc-
ument's own structure and a validator enforces a target utilisation of 0.85
and refuses oversized atomic units: a block that cannot be divided without
cutting an equation or a table stays whole. Each surviving part is written
as its own file labelled part k of N , and every part is read through a line-
offset address rather than from its beginning, so retrieval can open a section
without paying for the whole document. The ceiling itself is a budget, not a
format limit. It keeps a stored unit inside the context window of the agents
that later read it, and it bounds the per-query read cost of a single hit.

    The measured outcome for the 100-paper arXiv corpus is 322 stored parts,
which is the corpus size the audits and the regression probes later operate on.
A separate 50-paper campaign under the same 30,000-character setting pro-

2,672 s of parsing; the same campaign at a 2,000-character setting produced
2,713 fragments, which is what the ceiling exists to prevent. The vector store
chunks what is stored at 500 characters with 50 characters of overlap [29, 30];
a separate dense replica index for comparison uses 800-character chunks and
produced 4,528 chunks for the same corpus.

4.4. Content-derived descriptions and tags

    Descriptions and tags are generated from the body text that the inges-
tion agent has actually read, not from filenames or directory positions. A
description must carry four elements--subject, method or technology, sce-
nario or problem, and key data or conclusion--with at least two concrete
nouns among them, and every claim is read back against the body before
storage; a mismatch rewrites the description, never the body. Tags pass a
blacklist filter, are normalised for case and language, and are then re-checked
against a sample of the body: a tag that does not appear in the text is
dropped rather than kept for vocabulary reuse.

    This gate exists because of what an earlier audit found. Of 26 document
descriptions checked against their own bodies, 24 did not match: the boiler-
plate "Gutenberg front/back matter" had been applied to real chapters across
a batch, and the librarian that reads part labels selected two parts instead
of seven. The wider catalog carries the same class of defect, with 156 of 322
documents flagged for a broken part prefix. Description quality is therefore
an empirical result of this work, not an assumption: a description is a claim
about content, not a fact about it. The consequence is architectural rather
than editorial. Because a claim can be wrong at ingest scale, it must be
cheap to verify and cheap to repair--which is why verification later became
a platform primitive (Section 6) and why the acquisition audit ends with
descriptions upgraded 165/165 rather than with a generation count.

4.5. Index construction and retrievability verification

    Indexing writes to three channels: a per-library vector collection, a lex-
ical BM25 index [25] over chunked documents, and a document graph in
Neo4j [36]. Ingestion success is judged by observable effects rather than by
the tool's success envelope. A path that reported success once returned an
empty index payload after a GPU out-of-memory failure, so the verifica-
tion gate now requires a non-empty collection name, the collection identifier
expected for the target library, and total_chunks  1 before the docu-
ment counts as indexed. Graph construction is asynchronous and returns a

                                               20
task identifier that is polled, but task status is not accepted as completion
evidence--an earlier graph build left a task row stuck in running while its
artifacts were already queryable. The step closes with an eight-point final
check that includes the stored copy read back at 500 characters, the chunk
and graph node counts, and three-layer metadata consistency across the tree
index, the library metadata file, and the files on disk.

4.6. Asynchronous execution, failure recovery, and observability

    Parsing and graph construction are non-blocking: each returns a task
identifier immediately, and a task registry resolves that identifier across tool
processes, so a caller polls status instead of holding a connection open. Batch
ingestion is resumable, and it was resumed in practice: an interruption dur-
ing a 100-paper run was recovered by restarting the client, which skipped
documents already present and continued from the recorded position. Per-
paper parse time in the 50-paper campaign ranged from 16.3 s to 40.4 s over
26 subject domains, 1,115.5 s in total--an average of 22.3 s per paper.

    The same campaign exposes the limit of that bookkeeping. Of 100 sub-
mitted papers, the state file records 91 names, 79 were retrievable in the
knowledge base at evaluation time, and 21 never became retrievable. The
reported failure mode is formula-dense scanned documents, and a mid-run
client crash was recovered by restart, but no artifact counts parse attempts,
parse failures, or the error text of a failed document. The 91-versus-79 dif-
ference is therefore an observability gap: we can report what the artifacts
show, and we cannot attribute the difference from them.

    Cross-lingual ingestion shows the same gates holding without language-
specific tuning. The corpus is 400 Wikipedia pages per language in English,
Chinese, and Japanese, ingested in two rounds; the second round was bit-
identical to the first (Table 6). Ingestion success rate was 1.0 for all three
languages in both rounds. Membership asks where each page landed, and the
recorded knowledge-base membership accuracy is 0.875 for English, 1.0 for
Chinese, and 1.0 for Japanese. Storage completeness sums the split parts of
large documents back to the source length and is 0.975, 0.9559, and 0.9402 re-
spectively. Self-retrieval Hit@1--the score for the page's own indexed text--
is 0.975, 1.0, and 1.0.

5. Knowledge organization and lifecycle management

    Acquisition decides what enters the system; organization decides what
the system is. In this platform organization is not a folder convention that
a human maintains by hand, nor an emergent property of embedding simi-
larity. It is a governed process with three invariants: content decides where
a document belongs, the disk, the tree registry and the library metadata
must agree about every document, and every operation that removes or relo-
cates knowledge is preceded by a survey and followed by a verification. This
chapter describes how those invariants are implemented, which of them are
enforced by the interface rather than by convention, and where the current
implementation still allows the three stores to diverge.

5.1. Content-routed placement
    Placement reads the parsed document, not its filename or its folder of

origin. For each arriving document the pipeline selects the library whose
stated scope covers the content, or proposes a new library when no existing
scope does; it then writes the document, derives a description and a tag set,
and runs the same final check used by acquisition before the document counts
as placed. Two rules keep the catalog usable as a retrieval instrument. First,
an existing library is preferred over a new one: the catalog stays shallow,

                        22
and the number of libraries an agent must inspect before searching stays
small. Second, a document is placed once; re-routing is an explicit lifecycle
operation, not a side effect of a later ingest.

    The library tree supports one level of content grouping in which a parent
library contains child libraries, and the metadata of a child is addressable
through the parent. The implementation carries a caveat that we report
rather than hide: the vector segments of child documents are stored in the
parent library's collection, so a search that targets a child library identifier
returns no candidates. The shipped operational guidance is therefore to
search the parent collection and to use the child libraries for navigation and
scoping rather than as search endpoints. This is a limitation of the current
storage mapping, not of the routing rule, and it is the kind of coupling
between an organizational decision and a retrieval path that a system paper
has to expose.

5.2. Catalog and metadata registries

    Two registries describe the same collection, and Table 4 lists their fields:
the global tree (.tree-fs.yml) is the authoritative structure, and each li-
brary carries a .knowledge-base.yml that indexes its documents. Docu-
ments themselves are Markdown files with an accompanying image directory.

    Agents read this catalog through a progressive-disclosure interface: the
library listing returns names, descriptions and document counts; the docu-
ment listing returns metadata without bodies; only an explicit read returns
content, and a read is attributed to a specific document. This design choice
is what makes the catalog-first retrieval lane viable: a caller can narrow a
search to a handful of candidate documents without ever loading a body,
which is why the catalog lane completes in 5­6 turns and 54­91 s (Table 9).

    Correctness of the catalog is defined as three-source consistency: for every
document, the file on disk, its entry in the tree registry, and its record in the
library metadata must agree on existence, path, size and index state. The
platform verifies this agreement rather than assuming it, and the audit is
productive: in the acceptance campaign a renamed document kept its old
vector segments, so the recorded chunk count read 18 where 9 chunks existed,
and the document matched its own former path at 0.9738 similarity, which
is high enough to be reported as a near-duplicate pair. Only an explicit force
re-index repaired it (18  9). The design lesson is that metadata consistency
and index consistency are two different obligations: the first can be satisfied

                                               23
by a single write operation, the second cannot, because the vector store is
updated by a separate call.

5.3. Tag taxonomy and governance
    Tags are the coarse, human-legible axis of the collection. A tag registry

holds the vocabulary; each document's tags are normalized before they are
written, filtered against a blacklist of structural strings (section headings,
generic words such as "introduction"), and re-checked against the document
body so that a tag must actually occur in the content it labels. Retrieval
by tag is a first-class operation, which makes tag quality a retrieval concern
rather than a cosmetic one.

    Governance is a maintenance loop rather than a schema. The registry
is curated, not centralized: no taxonomy is imposed on authors, and the
vocabulary grows with the corpus. The audit therefore treats the registry
as a health surface: it reports tags with zero referencing documents, which
accumulate from test documents, abandoned naming experiments and chap-
ter headings, and it removes them only when instructed. In the audited
campaign 165 documents passed the tag gate with no failures, and the live
registry held 316 tags. We do not claim a controlled vocabulary; we claim
that the vocabulary is inspectable and that orphaned entries are detectable.

5.4. Duplicate detection and collection hygiene
    Acquisition refuses exact duplicates by content hash and flags near du-

plicates by vector similarity; at the collection level the question is different,
because the same content can legitimately arrive through two ingest paths
or into two libraries. The hygiene procedure is survey-then-act: enumerate
candidate duplicate groups at a similarity threshold (0.90, with a manual
fallback channel at 0.85), inspect each group, and delete nothing automati-
cally.

    The audited run illustrates why the survey step matters more than the
threshold. On the 322-document corpus the detector reported 35 candidate
groups with similarities between 0.90 and 0.975; every one of them turned
out to be a pair of adjacent parts of the same long paper rather than a true
duplicate, and no deletion was performed. A policy that deleted on detection
would have destroyed 35 legitimate document parts. The same run confirmed
zero true duplicates, which is the expected outcome when acquisition gates
run before storage. Cross-library duplication is detected but not merged

                                               24
automatically: merging two libraries is a lifecycle decision with provenance
consequences, so it stays with the operator.

5.5. Library lifecycle operations
    Table 7 lists the operations the platform exposes, the interface that per-

forms them, and the consistency obligation each one carries. All of them are
available to agents through the same tool surface that the user interface uses,
which is deliberate: an agent that may read a collection should be able to
maintain it under the same rules a human operator follows.

    Two properties of this table carry the argument of the chapter. The first
is that re-indexing is not implicit. Creating, moving or rewriting a document
does not update the vector store; an explicit index operation does, and the
audit treats a missing or stale index as an incomplete operation rather than as
a warning. This is the single rule we would carry into any successor system:
an index that is updated by side effect is an index that silently disagrees
with the truth. Keeping an approximate index coherent with a mutating
document store is itself an active research problem [56]; the contribution here
is not a new maintenance algorithm but a contract under which disagreement
becomes detectable. The second is the honest boundary of the evidence.
Move, rename, update, delete and re-index were exercised with real tool
calls and audited afterwards; merge and split are implemented and were
demonstrated on live libraries, but they have no controlled evaluation, and
the sub-library hierarchy was analysed and deliberately not executed pending
an operator decision. No concurrent organizational operation was tested. We
prefer to state this than to imply that a lifecycle operation is verified because
it has an interface.

5.6. The document graph
    The platform maintains a property graph over the collection with docu-

ment, library, tag, folder and entity nodes and membership, tagging, related-
ness, identity and containment edges. Edges are derived from three signals:
co-membership of tags, vector similarity above a threshold, and the verdicts
produced by content verification during retrieval. Adding one document to a
library added four edges in the audited run (4,675  4,679), one per assigned
tag; a full build over the 100-paper corpus produced 658 nodes covering 333
documents and 316 tags, and the 165-document intermediate state held 335
nodes and 1,671 edges.

                                               25
    The graph has a deliberately restricted role. It is a navigation instrument:
it expands a candidate set, surfaces documents that share context across
libraries, and identifies central documents in a collection. It is never evidence.
Every candidate a graph traversal proposes still has to pass the same content-
verification gate as a candidate proposed by a dense retriever, and the graph's
own contribution to retrieval is measured separately: as a standalone retriever
it reached Hit@5 of 0.5625 on the industrial corpus and 0.2 on the second
corpus, well below the lexical and dense channels (Table 14). We report this
because it is the honest status of graph-based organization in this system:
useful for discovery, weak as a ranker.

    Two implementation gaps belong here as well. Graph construction is not
yet guaranteed by the ingestion path: a document ingested through one agent
harness received no graph node, so a library reported two of three documents,
which is a silent incompleteness of the kind the three-source audit is designed
to catch elsewhere. And batch graph builds have been observed to leave a
task record in the running state after the work completed, so task status
cannot be used as completion evidence; the graph itself must be queried.

5.7. Operational knowledge as a first-class collection

    Process documents describe what the organization intends; operational
lessons describe what actually happened. The platform stores the second
kind as a first-class collection with the same addressing, indexing and re-
trieval interface as ordinary documents, plus two differences that reflect the
epistemic status of a lesson.

    First, an experience carries a credibility tier, and the tier controls whether
it may be shown (Table 8). Knowledge that is a claim about practice is held
to a stricter bar than a document: the experience channel's similarity thresh-
old is 0.55 against the document channel's 0.35, and an unvetted or disputed
entry is capped even when it scores well. Second, entries are drafted before
they are served. Extraction produces candidates from ingested content or
from recurring question-answer pairs; automated distillation runs as a sepa-
rate agent process on a schedule, guarded by a circuit breaker (three consec-
utive failures suspend it for 24 hours) and a heuristic fallback whose output
is marked low-confidence. Nothing reaches the served collection without an
explicit approval step, which is the same fail-closed posture the retrieval gate
applies to citations.

    The evidence for this subsystem is deliberately presented as case evidence.
One extraction campaign produced thirteen candidate drafts; one end-to-

                                               26
end scenario sedimented a single experience, which a later global experience
search returned first with a local similarity of 0.711; and the approval path
was never exercised, because the draft pool was empty when it was tested.
A single scenario and thirteen candidates cannot support an effectiveness
claim, and we do not make one. What the campaign does establish is that
operational knowledge travels through the same pipeline as documents --
addressed, indexed, gated and auditable -- rather than living in a wiki or a
prompt.

5.8. Integrity verification as routine

    Every property claimed in this chapter is checked by a verification pass
that runs against the live collection: three-source consistency per document,
document integrity, parse quality, index coverage with optional repair, tag
health, experience health, and a scorecard that summarizes the findings. The
pass is read-only by default; repairs require an explicit instruction, and the
report is produced before any repair. This is a deliberate ordering: an audit
that repairs as it goes cannot report what it found.

    The pass is not ceremonial. It is how the two index-integrity incidents
of this deployment were found -- a silently lost collection and a stale in-
dex -- and how the reorganization campaign was certified after the fact
(322/322 metadata checks, 165/165 description checks, 33/33 sampled con-
sistency checks, 15/15 retrieval regression probes with no degradation). In
the same campaign the audit also surfaced the failures reported above: the
rename-time chunk duplication, the missing graph node, and a task-status
record that outlived its task. A collection whose health is a command is a
collection whose health can be argued about with evidence.

5.9. What organization buys, and what it does not

    Organization buys candidate quality and auditability. Because the cata-
log is curated and consistent, an agent can select a library and a document
set without loading bodies, which is the mechanism behind the catalog-first
lane and behind cross-library discovery in an environment with nineteen li-
braries. Because every placement, tag, description and deletion left a record,
the collection can be re-verified after the fact; the numbers in this chapter
are not survey estimates but audit outputs.

    Three things organization does not do, and the paper does not claim. It
does not repair a bad parse: a document whose parse lost its tables cannot be
recovered by placing it well. It does not prevent divergence by construction:

                                               27
the write path can leave the vector index behind, and only an audit and an
explicit re-index restore agreement. And it does not scale without limits: the
largest collection we report is 322 documents in five libraries, the merge and
split operations are unevaluated, and no organizational operation was tested
under concurrency. These are engineering boundaries, and the next chapter
describes the retrieval contract that is built on top of them.

6. Content-verified retrieval

    Every citation a lane proposes must pass a content-verification gate before
it may appear in an answer. The gate scores each retrieved segment on a 0­8
rubric (topic 0­3, scenario 0­3, evidence 0­2) and keeps a document only if
at least one of its segments reaches the acceptance threshold: 0.5 in general,
0.3 for enumeration questions. It processes at most six references per call,
takes the matched text from the server side rather than from the caller, and
fails closed. No surviving evidence means the caller must answer that the
corpus does not contain the answer.

6.1. One query through the gate
    Q2 of Section 8.1 is "EL inspection shows edge delamination: root cause

and corrective actions", asked through the public chat API with the failure-
analysis library pinned and the Hybrid lane selected. Its timeline shows
where candidates come from, what the gate decides, and how much text the
agent may read.

   1. Policy. The chat endpoint attaches the Hybrid lane's tool whitelist
       and instructions server-side, so the agent cannot call a tool outside it.

   2. Candidate generation (one call). The agent issues a single
       kb_hybrid_search. Two channels run server-side: a dense vector
       channel (top-30, cosine threshold 0.35) and a catalog keyword channel
       over the pinned library's descriptions. Candidates merge on (library,
       document), documents agreed by both channels are ordered first, and
       the pool is capped at twelve. On Q2 the pool is the gold root-cause
       report plus eleven same-domain siblings.

   3. The gate. The server fetches each candidate's full text itself, segments
       it, and scores does this text contain direct evidence that answers the
       query in batches of at most six. The agent never supplies text, so it
       cannot argue for a document it has not read. On Q2 the gold report
       scores 0.846 while five extruder-telemetry reports score 0.887­0.926.

                                               28
   4. Cut and read budget. A document-level relative cut keeps scores
        best - 0.15 with a top-5 floor and a cross-domain anchor guard, so
       an inflated out-of-domain score cannot set the cut line; the top five
       survivors are returned in full, at most 2 000 characters each. That read
       window is the only full-content channel the agent sees; the rest of the
       pool survives as scores in the audit trail.

   5. The answer. Citations name the document path, the lane, and the
       engine score, graded P0 (direct evidence) through P2 (weak).

   6. The terminal paths. If the cut leaves no survivor, Lane A escalates
       once to Lane B; if Lane B also exhausts its budget, the run ends in an
       explicit not-found report instead of a speculation.

    The trace also shows where the design fails. Under the shipped re-ranking
role, Q2's gold (0.846) is pushed to rank eight by the five higher-scoring
telemetry reports, outside the five-document read window, so the agent reads
confusers (Fig. 8). Under the filter role the same pool, the same scores, and
the same verdicts leave the gold first: same gate, same evidence, only the
role of the induced ordering changes. This one trace foreshadows the paper's
central result (Section 8.7).

6.2. The gate contract
    The gate is a locally hosted evidence-classification model, the Laya judg-

ing service [44], invoked through a kb_laya_judge tool. Given a query and
a batch of document references, the server fetches each document itself, seg-
ments it, and scores every segment under one of three criteria: evidence
(specific substantiation; a mere topic match is insufficient), instance (the
text contains at least one member of the requested class, routed automati-
cally for enumeration questions), or auto. A document survives only if its
best segment reaches the threshold, so a document at vector score 0.40 with
one yes verdict survives while a document at 0.95 that the judge rejects is
dropped.

    At the protocol layer the platform documents the score as a 0­8 rubric
(topic 0­3, scenario 0­3, evidence 0­2; 6 answers directly, 5 escalates to the
librarian lane, 4 drops). The [0, 1] score is the machine-readable projection
of that rubric. The projection can saturate -- the logged maximum score
is 1.0000 -- which is one reason thresholds carry little discriminating power
between golds and same-domain confusers (Section 8.7).

                                               29
    Every operating constant is a shipped production default: the thresholds,
the six-reference judge batch, the hybrid candidate cap of twelve, and the
0.15 relative-cut margin with its top-5 floor. They were fixed during earlier
deployments on a different corpus and were not tuned on the evaluation
questions of Section 8.1. The classifier is a single, locally fine-tuned evidence
classifier whose checkpoint reference ships with the platform artifacts; it is
deterministic in our replays (repeat calls with identical inputs reproduced
identical orderings), and every verifier finding in this paper inherits that
choice, a scope limit noted in Section 10.

    Fail-closed semantics apply to every failure mode: fetch errors, engine
errors, and timeouts count as rejection. Survivors are aggregated into an
evidence pack with per-passage provenance.

6.3. Three lanes

    The three lanes are not three implementations of one idea; they are three
points on a latency/coverage frontier (Table 9; Fig. 2). The Hybrid lane is
the fastest and the most auditable, because it moves orchestration server-side
and ships its audit trail in one response; it is the interactive default. The
Librarian lane is the one that must not miss a document: it spends its twelve-
call budget on catalog navigation and buys exhaustive coverage with explicit
blind-spot disclosure. The Search lane is the latency-tuned interactive path
for pinned libraries.

    Lane A (Search). Phase A0 rewrites the query into (subject, attribute,
constraints) form, splits multi-entity and comparison questions into parallel
sub-queries, and routes incident-style queries to the experience store (Sec-
tion 3) first. Phase A1 issues one wide dense query (top-30, threshold 0.35,
cross-library balancing), deduplicates by document, and keeps all candidate
documents rather than truncating to the top chunks. Phase A2 submits
citation-only references (library id plus document path) to the gate in batches
of at most six, and Phase A3 returns the judge's evidence pack, the only full-
content channel in this lane, from which the agent composes an answer whose
citations carry confidence levels (P0 direct evidence, P1 partial, P2 weak).

    Lane B (Librarian). A fixed spine under a hard budget of twelve tool
calls. L0 reads the library catalog (ids, names, descriptions, counts) and
prunes shelves by the rewritten query; L1 lists each surviving shelf's docu-
ments with descriptions; L2 routes by query shape, sending literal and enu-
meration questions ("which documents mention X") to a machine-side scanner
(0.5 s per document, no LLM turns) and semantic questions to judging; L3

                                               30
judges in batches of at most six, because larger batches historically trig-
gered transport timeouts and fail-closed batch loss; L4 reads only survivors,
bounded to 2 000 characters and starting at the judge-reported evidence line;
L5 emits a five-part report (search paths, answer, sources, confidence, blind
spots). Two rules are absolute: catalog descriptions never prune documents
by themselves, and an exhausted budget terminates in blind-spot disclosure,
not in a guessed answer.

    Lane C (Hybrid). Agentic latency is dominated by LLM turns, not by
tools (Section 8.6), so Lane C spends exactly one call by moving the whole
pipeline into the server. A single kb_hybrid_search call executes P1 (a
parallel block of a vector channel, top-30 at threshold 0.35, and a catalog
keyword channel); P3 merges candidates by (library, document), agreed-by-
both documents first, capped at twelve; P4 judges every candidate with the
gate in batches of six (criterion evidence, threshold 0.5); P4b applies the
document-level relative cut; and P5 reads the top five documents in full
(2 000 characters each). The response carries the complete audit trail (per-
lane candidates, merge counts, judge statistics, cut decisions, read contents),
so a one-call lane is still an inspectable lane.

6.4. Selecting a lane is a budget decision
    The three lanes trade latency for recall under an enforced call and turn

budget, so the choice is not which retriever is better but how much of the
budget to spend before the gate runs. Hybrid is the interactive default,
Librarian the must-not-miss lane, Search the pinned-library path.

    What makes the lanes interchangeable is the gate. All three terminate in
the same contract -- one gate, one fail-closed semantics, one evidence pack
-- so replacing a lane changes the retrieval affordance and the budget, not
the meaning of a citation. Escalation follows a trigger rather than a hunch:
zero verified evidence escalates Lane A to Lane B exactly once. Query-shape
auto-selection is future work (Section 10).

    One caveat is honest to record. On the evaluated corpus Lane A occu-
pies no distinct operating point of its own: the Hybrid lane is faster and
the Librarian lane more exhaustive. A two-lane deployment (Hybrid plus
Librarian) is therefore defensible today, with the Search lane retained as the
pinned-library interactive default; because the lanes share one gate and one
abstention semantics, that reduction costs coverage, not trust.

                                               31
6.5. The gate as a platform primitive
    The gate is not a prompt. It is a server-side tool reachable only through

the platform's own interface, and its operating point is an explicit argument
rather than a sentence in an instruction: kb_laya_judge(query,documents,
criterion=auto,threshold=0.5,max_evidence_chars=40000). The plat-
form, not the agent, chooses the criterion and fixes the threshold. Calls
are serialized through a single judge worker -- an early concurrent work-
load crashed the worker with a torch paging-file error, so verdicts take a
lock -- with a 900 s call timeout, and every verdict is logged with its query,
references, scores, and decision.

    That placement makes verification a property of the layer rather than an
aspiration of the agent. Any lane, harness, or external client that answers
through the platform inherits the gate; none can skip it, and none can re-
threshold it per query. Enforcement has a second half in the lane policy,
which attaches a per-lane tool whitelist server-side: a librarian-lane agent is
denied the vector and two-stage tools at the protocol level, so it cannot reach
around the catalog-first path it was given.

    Externalized verification also distinguishes this design from verifiers that
run inside the generator. Self-RAG and CRAG verify during generation with
the generator itself [37, 38]; the gate sees only (query, document text) pairs
and is consumed as a tool. That is what makes it reusable and inspectable,
and, by the same placement, fallible: the LLM-judge critique applies here
as much as anywhere [45], which is why verdicts fail closed, are logged, and
are reported as evidence rather than as truth (Section 8.7). The cost is paid
once per deployment: a twelve-candidate verification adds 0.5 s to a call
(Table 14), tens of milliseconds per verdict once the worker is resident, while
a cold judge load costs 10­12 s (Section 7).

6.6. Honest abstention
    A threshold that never rejects a candidate cannot refuse a query. On four

out-of-corpus probes the judged hybrid still keeps 9­11 of its 12 candidates
(Section 8.6, Fig. 5), and the graded probes behave no differently: prompts
with partial coverage and prompts with none both keep 10­12 of 12 candi-
dates, with top scores up to 0.983 (Table 18 and Section 8.8). Because the
[0, 1] projection saturates, no cut point separates a supported answer from
an unsupported one.

    Abstention therefore lives in the reading-and-answering layer, enforced by
the lane contract rather than advised in a prompt. A caller with no surviving

                                               32
evidence must report a not-found with its search scope: which libraries and
how many documents were searched, how the query was routed, and which
topics sit outside the corpus. The escalation rule makes that state terminal,
so an exhausted budget produces a report and not a guess.

    Because abstention is a terminal state enforced server-side, it can be
evaluated like any other outcome: all six out-of-corpus probe runs returned
explicit not-found reports with zero fabricated citations, even though the
tool layer had handed them candidates (Sections 8.6 and 8.6). The division
of labour is the point: the gate decides what may be cited, the reading layer
decides whether to answer at all, and the measurements put the reliability
in the second and not the first.

7. Agent interface, implementation, and non-functional properties

7.1. The tool surface
    The platform is exposed to agents as a single Model Context Protocol

server [3] carrying 95 tools in eight groups: knowledge-base CRUD and
catalog; document read/write; file system; parsing and task status; tags;
search and vector index; knowledge graph; and experience and persona
management. The count is the number of registered tool decorators in
kb-mcp/server.py, checked one-to-one against the function definitions they
register; the project's own documentation had drifted to a lower number,
so we report the measured one. The interface is not a reduced agent-only
view: it owns no private data path. Writes go through the same HTTP
services the user interface calls, reads read the same .tree-fs.json and
.knowledge-base.yml registries the web server reads, and the MCP process
itself contains no HTTP code, only a client module.

    Three governance rules make a surface this wide safe to hand to a model.
First, read-path tools and write-path tools are disjoint families, and retrieval
lanes are granted the read family only. Second, a stored parsed document
cannot enter a knowledge base through the generic document writer: it must
pass the parsed-document writer (kb_doc_save_parsed), the single entry
point that carries parse provenance and forces the ingestion quality gates
to run. Third, long-running operations--a parse batch, a re-index, a graph
build, a meditation pass--return a task id immediately instead of blocking
the session, and are polled through the task-status tools backed by an in-
process task registry.

                                               33
7.2. Harness integration and lane policy
    One chat endpoint adapts fourteen agent harnesses to a uniform message

stream, so a vendor CLI, an ACP-compatible agent, and the platform's own
engine all reach the same retrieval lanes. The lane policy of Section 6 is
enforced here rather than by prompt discipline: each lane is attached to a
tool allow-list, of which 21 entries are read-only MCP retrieval tools, and to a
deny-list of more than 40 harness-internal tools (file editors, web search, sub-
agent spawning). The gate was observed working in the harness campaign:
on its first turn a model attempted to end the session early, the attempt
was blocked by the deny-list, and the retrieval that followed was unaffected.
Sessions are budgeted as well, with per-lane turn and call caps (Table 9); the
campaign ran 18 end-to-end scenarios, all of which passed with no fabricated
answer.

    Three operational findings from that campaign matter to callers. A trivial
ping prompt triggered a six-skill pre-flight before any retrieval, and the probe
was killed by a 30 s timeout; the platform's default caller timeout is therefore
420 s, not the few seconds a request such as "list my libraries" suggests. The
streaming endpoint emits bare data: frames with no event names, so a client
must parse data-only frames instead of waiting for a named event. Finally,
engine choice dominates cost by one to two orders of magnitude at the same
quality tier: $0.90­1.80 per question for the agent harness, $0.11­0.36 for
the second engine, and $0.03­0.07 for the third. Wall clock was 50­176 s,
23­52 s, and 34­171 s respectively, but the third engine's engine-side time
was only 15­83 s: its extra wall clock is harness cold start of 50­90 s per
call, not retrieval. One residual gap appeared in the same campaign: a
document ingested through the third harness had no graph node, while the
same operation through the agent surface created one.

7.3. Implementation and runtime engineering
    The backend is Python 3.12 with FastAPI. ChromaDB [29] stores one

HNSW [30] collection per knowledge base in cosine space; embeddings are
BGE-M3 [24], 1024-dimensional and normalized, encoded in batches of 32 by
a lazy-loaded singleton with an LRU encode cache. Neo4j 5.20 [36] holds the
document graph, jieba [57] tokenizes the keyword lane, MinerU [7] performs
parsing and OCR, and MCP is spoken over stdio and SSE.

    The enforcement described in Section 6 depends on engineering invisible
in the interfaces. The judging model runs as a resident worker behind a
machine-level file lock, with an LRU verdict cache and explicit CUDA-cache

                                               34
release between workloads. A cold judge load costs 10­12 s per batch; the
resident worker answers in 1­2 s. The worker also removes a failure mode
met in production: when judge and bulk-embedding workloads ran inter-
leaved without cache cleanup, one end-to-end run degraded from 68 s to 27
minutes under the Windows display driver model's paging behaviour. Judge
calls are serialized by one lock, because two concurrent judgments on a sin-
gle GPU raise a native allocator failure; a call is held to a 900 s ceiling
and fails closed beyond it. MinerU is launched as a hidden subprocess with
platform-specific parent-death cleanup--a Windows Job Object with kill-on-
close, Linux prctl(PR_SET_PDEATHSIG), and a process group plus atexit on
macOS--and its stdout is redirected to a log file rather than a pipe, because
the pipe variant failed with a spurious Errno 22 once the reader closed. Be-
fore start, the backend probes its own port with a bare socket bind and refuses
to start if the port is occupied, so a surviving process cannot be shadowed by
a second one. Vector writes are serialized per collection, after one production
incident traced to concurrent segment handles; the keyword index updates
incrementally. Every HTTP client is constructed with trust_env=False, so
a corporate proxy cannot intercept calls to localhost.

7.4. Configuration, deployment, and reproducibility
    A single config.yml is the source of truth for ports and thresholds; en-

vironment variables override it. The mechanism is used in practice, not only
documented: in the harness campaign the services ran on web 6789, backend
8771, and kb-mcp 8000, the backend port being an override of the shipped
development default. Rate limiting (a general bucket and a smaller bucket
for expensive routes), authentication tokens for the MCP, user, and legacy
clients, and the retrieval thresholds all live in the same file. The ragctl
command-line tool covers setup, start, status, dependency, configuration,
and update operations. The system runs self-hosted on a single workstation:
ChromaDB runs in-process, Neo4j runs locally on bolt port 7687, and MinerU
runs as a local subprocess, so no external service is required for a deployment.
The same file carries the higher retrieval threshold (0.55) used by the experi-
ence subsystem, whose rolling summary of operational lessons is regenerated
by a rules-based pass every fifth ingestion. Source code and all experiment
scripts are public at https://github.com/kingdol666/rag-knowledge.

8. Evaluation

    We evaluate the deployed system on three corpora that differ in origin,
language and scale, using three metric families, and we organise the evidence
into seven groups. E1 and E2, reported here, cover the stages that precede a
question: acquisition and parsing, and organisation, consistency and manage-
ment operations. E3­E7, reported in Sections 8.4­8.8, cover retrieval-quality
ablations, agent behaviour and verifier behaviour. Numbers are reported
against the corpus that produced them, and results from different corpora
are never merged.

8.1. Experimental setup

    Corpora. (i) Deployed industrial corpus: five knowledge bases in a live
deployment hold 42 documents, one of them containing twenty near-duplicate
equipment-telemetry reports that share prefixes, vocabulary and structure,
a deliberate stressor for near-duplicate detection and confuser density [21].
Sixteen gold questions and four out-of-corpus probes were run against it.
(ii) arXiv corpus: 100 submitted papers spanning about 25 domains; at
evaluation time the state file records 91 paper names and 79 papers are re-
trievable. Content routing placed them in five domain libraries holding 322
stored parts, and ten gold questions (BQ01­BQ10) target books referenced
by these papers, keyed by arXiv identifier. The live environment held 19
libraries. (iii) Cross-lingual ingestion corpus: 400 Wikipedia pages per lan-
guage for English, Chinese and Japanese, with 40 extractive cloze queries
per language whose answers occur verbatim in the source page. It is used for
ingestion only; we report no retrieval-quality numbers for it.

    Questions and gold annotations. The sixteen industrial questions
(Table 10) were written by the authors rather than sampled from logs, a cir-
cularity we state rather than hide; each identifies one or two gold documents
by a unique filename fragment and one to three fact markers. Q16 names
the event heatdecay092, a token that also occurs in its gold document's
filename, so a retriever ranking on filenames alone could match it without
reading content; we keep the question and report the weakness. On the arXiv
corpus a question passes only if the gold identifier is traceable in the answer
or the tool trajectory and at least 60% of the gold keywords occur at word
boundaries, evaluated deterministically with no language model in the loop.

    Metric families. Retrieval results use Hit@5, Recall@5 and MRR@5,
following passage-ranking evaluation conventions [27]. A graded measure,

                                               36
nDCG@5 [28], was computed only inside one exploratory arm of the cross-
lingual benchmark that we do not report; no nDCG@5 value appears here.
Grounding and key-fact verification use deterministic string tests: whether
the answer cites the gold document, whether at least 60% of gold key facts
appear at word boundaries, and whether part, section and table citation
markers are present. These checks are mechanical, not model-based faithful-
ness estimates such as RAGAS [58] or ARES [59]. The third family covers
wall-clock time, agent turns, tool calls, monetary cost, and the correctness
of refused answers.

    Controls. Deterministic lanes were re-run and compared field by field
with latency excluded, so a re-run could disagree only on content and order-
ing; model-driven lanes were repeated with the same model version and are
reported as mean with the observed spread. A fixed retry policy applied: an
empty zero-turn response or a transient gateway timeout was retried once,
which fired on two runs and succeeded both times. Before measurement we
repaired two corpus-integrity faults through the platform's own operations:
one document missing from its vector collection was re-indexed, and one li-
brary whose collection had been silently lost was rebuilt. All measurements
ran on one workstation whose RTX 5060 (8 GB) serves the embedding model
and the judge worker, with every service local under Windows.

    What is deterministic. The tool-layer retrieval lanes, the grading rule,
the audits, duplicate adjudication and the cross-lingual ingestion pipeline are
deterministic: repeating them reproduces the same values, and cross-lingual
round 2 was bit-identical to round 1. The agent lanes, and anything derived
from generated text, are not, and are reported with their repetition spread.

8.2. E1: Acquisition and parsing

    One hundred submitted papers were parsed and routed by content into
five domain libraries, producing 322 stored parts after the 30,000-character
split. Table 11 lists the acquisition audit: nine per-paper ingest checks passed
100/100, no true duplicate was found against 35 near-duplicate pairs, 165
document descriptions were upgraded, the content and storage audits passed
322/322 and 33/33, all 15 retrieval regression probes passed, and the dense
replica index holds 4,528 chunks. Values are asserted by the run scripts from
saved artifacts rather than hand-counted; the content audit samples the first
1,000 characters of each part and the storage audit covers 33 storage units.

    Accounting gap. This campaign does not support the claim that all
100 papers became usable, and we do not make it: 100 papers were sub-

mitted, the state file exp10_ingest_state.json records 91 names, and 79
were retrievable at evaluation time, so 21 papers were never retrievable. The
reported failure modes are formula-dense scanned papers, which the parser
handled poorly, and one crash of the tool layer during batch parsing that was
restarted from a checkpoint. No artifact records a count of attempted against
failed papers, and the state file has no per-paper parse status, so we cannot
say whether the nine papers missing between submission and the recorded

reconciliation is possible only against the artifacts that survive [22].

    Cross-lingual ingestion. The benchmark ran twice over 400 pages per
language. The ingest success rate was 1.0 in both rounds for all three lan-

    Smaller campaigns. A 50-paper, 26-domain campaign parsed and veri-

all 50, so the tag-quality check failed often without blocking ingestion. Its
30,000-character tier turned 50 papers into 165 documents (nine whole doc-
uments and 156 parts), passed the storage check 50/50 and the description-
and-tag check 165/165 with zero failures, stored 3,798,446 characters and
took 2,672 s to parse. Two caveats attach: one stage re-ran because of inbox
ordering, and more than 800 extracted images were not uploaded. Descrip-
tions and tags are checked against document content before storage [15].

on disk, the tree index and the per-library metadata must agree about which
documents exist, where they live and how much indexed content each has.
Under that definition we found a real defect in a disposable test library. Af-

8.4. E3: Operational knowledge and automated distillation

    The experience store holds operational lessons as first-class retrievable
documents: an entry is addressed, indexed and searched through the same
interfaces as an ordinary document, and it carries a credibility tier that
decides whether it may be shown at all (Section 5.7, Table 8). The design
claim is that a lesson about practice should be governed like any other content
object. The evidence collected for that claim is case evidence and we report
it as such.

    Three measurements exist. (i) One extraction campaign over a single
small knowledge base produced thirteen candidate drafts (benchmark-suite/results/experience_
extract_candidates 13, drafts_created 13). (ii) One end-to-end sce-
nario was sedimented into one experience (exp-d6f4d47ae3d6) and a later
global experience search returned that entry first, with a local similar-
ity of 0.711 (review/fulltest-20261012, scenario A6). (iii) The draft-
approval path was never exercised: the draft pool was empty when it was
queried (experience_drafts_list returned count 0 with no drafts), so
experience_draft_read and experience_draft_approve could not run at
all (benchmark-suite/results/SKILL-E2E-REPORT.md, lines 27 and 103).
The heuristic extraction path was exercised only in dry-run mode, where it
returned a raw document dump that the quality gate would reject (line 100).

    One scenario and thirteen candidates cannot support an effectiveness
claim, and we do not make one. The larger part of the automated-distillation
pipeline is unverified: scheduled distillation, review and approval at scale, ap-
plication feedback, and credibility decay were not exercised end to end. What
the measurements show is narrower and sufficient for the design argument:
an operational lesson can travel the document path -- created, indexed, re-
turned first by search, with its tier attached -- and nothing more.

8.5. E4: Retrieval quality and the role of the verdict
    Table 14 compares nine configurations on the sixteen gold-annotated

questions of the deployed industrial corpus (42 documents; paper-kbs/experiments/results/exp1
and exp5_judge_filter.json). The tool-layer arms retrieve and never an-
swer: their latencies are retrieval latencies, and, because no generation
follows, they carry no token cost. With sixteen questions the Wilson 95%
intervals on the headline proportions are wide (judged hybrid [0.23, 0.67];
judge-as-filter [0.72, 0.99]; BM25 and RRF [0.81, 1.00]), so the decomposi-
tion below, not the point estimates, is the claim. A separate six-method
retrieval-mode ablation exists in the released logs but covers two questions;
it is a smoke check at n = 2 and no claim rests on it.

    On this corpus, lexical retrieval is strong. BM25 [25] reaches Hit@5
1.00 (MRR 0.958) at about a millisecond per call; reciprocal rank fusion [26]
matches it in Hit@5 and follows in MRR (0.927); the two-stage BM25+graph-
then-dense pipeline reaches Hit@5 1.00 with Recall@5 0.97, one gold of a two-
gold question falling outside the top-5. Dense retrieval with BGE-M3 [24]
misses exactly one question (Q16), where four near-duplicate sibling reports
from one incident family crowd the top-5 -- a confuser-density failure, not
a vocabulary one. Graph search over the document graph [31], limited to
node-name and tag matching, is the weakest standalone signal (Hit@5 0.56,
MRR 0.562). Nothing here is a retrieval advance: the corpus is curated,
the questions are written in its vocabulary, three configurations tie at Hit@5
1.00, and the one that uses only words leads on MRR.

    The central result is the verdict's role, not its existence. The
same judged scores enter three configurations: not used (fusion-only, Hit@5
0.94), used to re-rank candidates (QDCVR-Hybrid, 0.44), and used as a filter
that keeps survivors in their fusion order (judge-as-filter, 0.94). On the eight
questions where re-ranking and filtering differ (Q2, Q4, Q5, Q6, Q8, Q9,
Q11, Q12), filtering wins all eight and loses none, with eight further ties on
Hit@5, and it restores MRR to 0.856 (exact binomial sign test, two-sided
p = 0.008); the only ranking movement is Q7, where the gold slips from
third to fifth inside the top-5. The verdicts were not wrong about which
documents are evidence -- they were wrong as an ordering. This matches
the judge-miscalibration pattern reported for model-generated preferences
[45, 48]: an individually plausible verdict can be ordinally unreliable, and the
fix is the placement of the verdict, not its existence or its strength. Its price
is measurable: judging twelve candidates adds well under a second with the
resident worker, and the hybrid call's latency surcharge over fusion-only is the

                                               41
observed difference between 542 ms and 62 ms per call. Two caveats travel
with the test. It is post-hoc and descriptive -- one of nine configurations
evaluated on the same sixteen questions -- and the gate is a single locally
fine-tuned checkpoint (Section 6.2), so every verifier finding in this paper
inherits that choice.

    A second corpus reproduces the baselines, not the collapse. Ten
questions over the 79 retrievable papers of the 100-paper arXiv ingestion
(8,031 chunks; paper-kbs/experiments/results/exp10_papers_toollayer.json)
are answered at Hit@5 0.9 by every configuration except graph search, which
reaches 0.2. One question (BQ03) is missed by all eight configurations,
and its gold paper is one of the 21 papers that never became retrievable in
that campaign: the single shared miss is an ingestion failure, not a retrieval
failure. We therefore report both denominators -- 0.9 over the ten questions
as asked, 1.00 over the nine answerable ones -- and say which is which. The
judged collapse of the industrial corpus does not reproduce on this corpus,
where the same-family confuser cluster that drives it is absent; the graph
configuration's 0.2 tracks absent relation tagging, since the bulk import
bypassed the tagging path.

    Thresholds and margins. All 191 judged candidates from the sixteen
hybrid runs passed the 0.5 acceptance threshold, the lowest logged score be-
ing 0.589, so at the threshold layer the gate filtered nothing on this corpus
(exp1_retrieval.json). The margin sweep that follows replays these logged
candidate pairs rather than re-retrieving them: with the top-5 floor un-
changed, a margin of 0.15 keeps the gold in 16 of the 17 in-pool gold instances
and 0.20 keeps it in all 17 (paper-kbs/notes/review-2026-10-08.md). A
leave-one-out procedure that selects the margin per question from the other
fifteen reaches a gold survival of 0.9375, its single miss being Q11, which
the shipped 0.15 margin also loses, while a fixed 0.20 floor reaches 1.00
(exp6_loo_margin.json). The shipped floor is thus an engineering con-
stant, not a fitted parameter, and the calibration that would justify it has
not been run.

    Rephrasing shows where the fragility sits. On a grid of sixteen para-
phrase questions (exp6_paraphrase.json) the judged configuration falls to
Hit@5 0.5 (MRR 0.189), while judge-as-filter and fusion-only hold at 0.9375
(MRR 0.833) and dense retrieval stays at 0.9375; BM25 falls from 1.00 to
0.875 (MRR 0.958 to 0.755). Both the lexical baseline and the judged re-
ranking lose ground when the wording changes, and the configuration that
refuses to let the verdict order the list does not. The gate's weakness is in

                                               42
the ordering it induces, not in the verdicts it returns.
    End to end, the modes differ in checkability. Ten fixed questions

ran through three modes that share one harness, one model and one neutral
prompt template and differ only in retrieval affordance: the platform's full
read surface, a bare agent with file tools over the exported papers, and dense
retrieval over an 800-character replica index of the corpus (4,528 chunks).
Table 15 reports the outcome. All three modes produce answers, and the bare
agent grounds as many of them as the platform (10/10 vs. 10/10), but its
answers name files where the platform names checkable addresses, and dense
retrieval cites an address once in ten questions. The modes differ mainly in
checkability rather than in whether an answer was produced. Three caveats
belong to this comparison: both dense grounding misses were tool-availability
aborts; grading is conservative, and word-boundary matching zeroes the bare
agent's plural "cathodes" on BQ08; and two platform cells re-ran once after a
harness timeout, correctness calls are the authors' trace readings, and cache
state was uncontrolled.

    E4 establishes one thing. Content verification placed as a filter rather
than as a ranker is the difference between a citation and a claim: every
returned document is judged, and the ordering the reader sees is not the
verdict's. Verification before answering is not itself new [37, 38]; what is
measured here is where the verdict belongs in the pipeline and what it costs
there. E4 does not establish a retrieval advance -- BM25 reaches Hit@5 1.00
on this corpus -- so recall is not the contribution, and no margin, threshold
or paraphrase variant tested repairs the ordering role of the verdicts. That
is the question the failure analysis of the verifier takes up.

8.6. E5: End-to-end agent behaviour, abstention, and cost

    The agentic grid. Four arms -- the Search, Librarian and Hybrid lanes
plus a bare model with no retrieval tools at all -- answered four questions
(Q2, Q4, Q6, Q7) twice each through the public chat API, with one engine
(omp over GLM-5.3-Flash) and a 20-turn budget. Citation outcomes agree
on all 16 of 16 arm­question cells across the two rounds: every grounded
cell named the gold document (12/12); the bare arm -- the same model,
told to answer from its own knowledge -- cited a source in 0 of 4 cells in
either round. The de-contaminated fact-marker check (delete every answer
line naming a gold document from the answer's first 600 characters, then re-
test the remainder) is 12/12 in round 1 and 11/12 in round 2; the exception,
Hybrid-Q2, keeps its markers only inside lines that also cite the gold -- an

                                               43
artifact of the deletion rule, and its citation hit stands. Six of the twelve
grounded cells answer from libraries of one or two documents, where hitting
the gold is a low bar; the substantive evidence is the six cells over the 31-
document process library. Lane operating points are in Table 9. Where
the bare arm is wrong, it is wrong in the way that motivates retrieval: on
the lamination question it gave a plausible textbook diagnosis (interfacial
adhesion failure, moisture ingress) missing the corpus's recorded root cause,
thermocouple drift leaving the encapsulant under-cured.

    Abstention is a property of the reading-and-answering layer, not
of a threshold. Three out-of-corpus questions produced three explicit not-
found reports (47.9, 73.3 and 113.5 s wall clock; turns were not recorded for
this probe family), each enumerating the scope it searched (five libraries, 42
documents), declining to answer from memory, and citing no document as
evidence. The retrieval tools themselves never abstain: six graded probes at
the tool layer -- three partially covered, three absent -- retained 10 to 12
of their 12 candidates at every tier, with top scores up to 0.983 on absent
probes and 0.965 on partial ones; the acceptance threshold of 0.5 never fired
as a refusal. Honesty is therefore enforced where the answer is written --
in the lane's read-then-answer contract [37, 51, 50] -- not by a similarity
cut-off.

    Where the time goes. In the twelve grounded cells, machine-side re-
trieval and verification stayed sub-second (BM25 0.9 ms mean, dense 18 ms,
two-stage 77 ms, judged hybrid 542 ms mean) against an end-to-end wall
clock of 36­91 s. The gap is model turns: 11.7, 12.8 and 15.3 s per turn
for the Search, Librarian and Hybrid arms -- roughly 12­15 s per turn, the
turn-dominated profile reported for agentic retrieval [4], and the reason the
platform moves orchestration server-side and caps turns. Results repeat; wall
clock does not: Search-Q2 took 70 s over 6 turns in round 1 and 165 s over
11 turns in round 2, Librarian-Q4 84 s over 6 turns and 92 s over 11.

    Cost. The same task set on three engines separates by one to two orders
of magnitude at the same quality tier (Table 16): all 18 scenarios pass with
every answer gold-hit and no fabricated citation. The cheapest engine pays a
50­90 s harness cold start per call, so its wall clock is about twice its engine
time; the Claude harness's wall clock is approximately its engine time.

8.7. E6: When the verifier fails

    Same-domain score inflation. The 191 candidates judged across the
sixteen hybrid tool runs -- twelve per run except Q14, whose merge produced

                                               44
eleven -- carry no usable separation between gold and non-gold. Every
candidate scored at least 0.589, far above the 0.5 acceptance threshold, so
on this corpus the threshold rejects nothing. The 17 in-pool gold instances
(Q16's gold never entered its pool) have a median of 0.88 and the 174 non-
gold candidates 0.86, a difference a two-sided Mann­Whitney test cannot
distinguish from chance (p = 0.26). Only five of the 17 golds rank in the top
three of the judged ordering and nine rank eighth or lower. Re-ranking with
the verdict therefore loses gold: the judged ordering reaches Hit@5 0.4375
(7/16; 0.44 in Table 14), where the verdict used only as a filter, or as one
signal in rank fusion, holds 0.94 (Section 8.5).

    The verdict is a model [44], and a second model fails the same
way. We replayed the same 191 logged (query, candidate) pairs -- a replay,
not a re-retrieval -- through a generic natural-language-inference checkpoint:
330M parameters, one question-as-context template, premises truncated to
roughly the first 500 characters after tokenization, with 59 of the 191 pairs
hitting the cap. It scores below the verdict (Hit@5 0.3125, MRR 0.1375)
and its separation is inverted: gold median 0.0113 against non-gold 0.05285
(Mann­Whitney p = 0.120). Truncation is not the explanation, because six
of the ten gold documents that fit the context window whole still score below
0.01; nor is it an out-of-domain effect, as unrelated topics still score corpus
documents at up to 0.9973, so an NLI threshold would abstain on nothing
either. A span-level replay inverts harder and significantly: over the 191
pairs and 3,489 span inferences at roughly 320 characters per span, the max-
pooled score is 0.4728 for gold against 0.91855 for non-gold (p = 0.0074),
with Hit@5 0.1875. Rank fusion of the two signals repairs neither (Hit@5
0.3125, MRR 0.111458). Replacing the judge checkpoint is not the repair.

    Paraphrase. The verdict's agreement with gold collapses when the same
questions are rephrased; that grid is reported in Section 8.5 and not repeated
here.

    What the failure tracks. Neither a generic NLI checkpoint, nor a
span-level variant of it, nor rank fusion over both reaches the verdict's own
quality; the second corpus shows the same gate losing nothing there (Sec-
tion 8.5), so the collapse tracks confuser density within one domain -- many
near-duplicate documents competing for the same question -- and not the
checkpoint, the domain, the language, or the corpus size [45, 48, 49, 50]. The
verdict earns its place as a filter and loses it as a ranker.

                                               45
8.8. E7: Non-functional behaviour

    Stability. A 27-run suite -- three task types × three lanes × three
rounds on the live multi-base instance (review/stability-multiround-20260930)
-- is summarised in Table 17. Session-trace replay confirms 27/27 lane-
contract compliance, with per-cell median wall clock spanning 34­76 s.
Caveats: correctness calls are the authors' trace readings, cache state was
uncontrolled, two platform cells re-ran once after a timeout, and the ±35 s
variance sits in the answer-generation window.

    Concurrency and integrity. A separate load campaign (review/stress-20260923/STRESS-R
2026-09-23) wrote one library concurrently: 20 simultaneous document cre-
ations finished in 0.8 s, 20/20 HTTP 200, with no corruption of .tree-fs.json
or .knowledge-base.yml (list count 20); 20 simultaneous index builds fin-
ished in 4.1 s (20/20, 620 chunks); 10 simultaneous experience creations
finished 10/10. Lexical re-verification found 3/3 probe documents and a
vector top-3 check found one; the artifact attributes that to homogeneous
templates diluting the semantic signal, not to data loss. This bounds con-
current writes only: no crash-mid-write or partial-index adversarial test was
run, so durability under interruption remains untested.

    Integrity incidents. Two index-integrity incidents surfaced during
evaluation setup -- one document missing from its vector collection, one
library whose collection had been silently lost. Both were caught by the
pre-measurement checks and repaired through the platform's own path
(index-document, reindex); the detection path, not the incident, is the
point.

    Deployment reality. The live snapshot of 2026-10-12 shows a running
system with 19 libraries, including the 322-document arXiv corpus content-
routed into five domain libraries. Every live-system figure here is a times-
tamped snapshot of a system in use, not a frozen benchmark; the five indus-
trial libraries behind the measurements above were then absent from the live
catalog.

9. Discussion and lessons learned

    The evidence in Section 8 supports a distinction that the retrieval lit-
erature tends to blur: finding a document and being allowed to cite it are
different operations, and the second one is a system property. The lessons
below are ordered from the retrieval layer outwards, because that is the order
in which we learned them.

                                               46
L1. Vectors are fast; content is accurate, and verification has two
costs. The tool-layer comparison is the cleanest statement of the retrieval
subproblem: on a curated corpus a millisecond-level BM25 beats an 18 ms
dense retriever, and neither knows whether a document answers the question.
The gate converts ranked candidates into accountable evidence with per-
citation provenance. Its costs are temporal (about 0.5 s per dozen candidates
end to end; 1­2 s per six-document batch for the resident worker) and, on this
corpus, accuracy-related: 0.94  0.44 Hit@5 under same-domain inflation
when the verdicts are used as an ordering. Auditability is what the gate
buys today; ranking quality is what calibration must earn back.
L2. Latency engineering is turn engineering. With retrieval sub-
second, the only lever that matters for interactive latency is the number of
agent turns. Tool whitelists that remove detour tools, one-call server-side
pipelines, and small judge batches that avoid transport timeouts together
took the hybrid lane from about 29 turns in the earliest serial-skill configura-
tion to 3­5 turns in the present measurements. The same accounting explains
the engine comparison: the third engine's wall clock exceeded its engine-side
time by 50­90 s per call, which is harness startup, not retrieval.
L3. The verifier is a model too, so calibrate it. The inflation analy-
sis is the paper's cautionary result: a verifier inherits domain-internal blind
spots, and a threshold calibrated on cross-domain negatives (0.5) does not
separate same-domain near-misses. Fail-closed semantics and forced read-
ing contain the damage; they do not replace calibration. The second-signal
probes sharpen the point: a generic entailment model fails too, but differ-
ently, anti-separating golds while still rejecting no out-of-corpus topic, and
a symbolic rule decides the numeric case that the shipped judge mishandles.
Verifier choice changes which errors you get, not whether you get any.
L4. Honesty needs an enforcement layer, not a prompt. Lane sep-
aration works because the server physically withholds tools: a librarian-lane
agent cannot call the vector tool even if instructed to. The out-of-corpus
probes show the same asymmetry for abstention. A threshold cannot refuse;
it accepted every out-of-corpus candidate it was shown. The reading-and-
answer layer refused all six probe prompts with disclosed searches and zero
fabricated citations. A bare agent without the platform refused explicitly in
two of four runs, and in the others produced confident, unsourced, partly
wrong answers whose wrongness no audit trail exposes. Unassisted honesty
is a coin flip; enforced honesty is inspectable.
L5. Production incidents taught honest observability. Three early

                                               47
incidents shaped the implementation: a corrupted index segment that made
one library silently un-retrievable across lanes, an out-of-memory indexing
run that returned success with an empty payload, and judge failures that
had to reject candidates rather than pass them. The common thread is that
every layer reports what it actually verified and every consumer treats the
unverifiable as failed. Table 19 consolidates the incident log into the failure-
mode catalogue we wish we had on day one, extended with the three defects
the organization and functional campaigns surfaced.
L6. The acquisition gate sets the ceiling for everything above it.
In the 100-document campaign, 21 documents failed to parse, mostly dense
scanned material with formulas and tables. The system's value here is not
that the rate is low, but that the failure is a reported outcome: a parse failure
that is stored as an empty or garbled document would enter the catalog,
receive a description, be indexed, and be retrieved and cited by the same
machinery that cites good documents, with no signal anywhere that anything
was lost. The same argument applies to a missing index or a stale description.
This is why the acquisition layer owns a post-condition rather than a warning,
and why the knowledge layer is a component of the system rather than a
precondition to retrieval.
L7. Organization is a retrieval budget. Content routing decides what
the candidate generator can see. A catalog that is curated and consistent
lets an agent choose a library and a document set from metadata alone,
which is the mechanism behind the catalog-first lane and behind cross-library
discovery in a deployment with nineteen libraries. An ungoverned catalog
produces the opposite: candidate generation degenerates into a global vector
search whose recall is limited by the cap, and no downstream verification
can recover a document that was never proposed. The organization layer is
therefore not administrative overhead around retrieval; it is where retrieval's
recall is decided.

    The lessons share one implication for how systems of this kind should
be evaluated. A claim about enforcement is a claim about a control, so it
needs a matched ablation: the same pipeline with the mechanism disabled,
the same questions, the same corpus. Where we have that control (retrieval
with and without verification at the tool layer) the claim is quantified; where
we did not have it at first (an end-to-end agent arm with the gate disabled)
we fixed the protocol before running it rather than after (Section 8.6). The
verifier's negative result is the reason we insist on the distinction: without
the matched ablation, a filter and a re-ranker are indistinguishable from the

                                               48
outside, and only one of them is defensible.

10. Threats to validity

Scale. The retrieval corpus at the centre of the evaluation is 42 documents
across five libraries; the acquisition and organization campaigns scale that
to 322 documents, and the second corpus adds 79. Absolute Hit@5 values
(BM25 at 1.00 on the industrial corpus) will not transfer to collections orders
of magnitude larger, where the hybrid lane's candidate cap and the librarian's
shelf pruning become load-bearing rather than optional. The benchmark
suite shipped with the platform is the intended scaling path, and a hundreds-
of-documents run with an author-independent question set remains the full
answer.
Question set. Sixteen tool-layer and four agent-layer questions on the in-
dustrial corpus, ten on the second corpus and forty cloze questions per lan-
guage in the acquisition campaign were authored by the system's developers.
Gold documents and fact markers are released for independent scrutiny, but
the samples are small and favour the corpora's vocabulary. The paraphrase
grid quantifies part of that exposure, since lexical ceilings drop under re-
wording while the verifier findings hold. One circularity is named rather
than silently enjoyed: Q16's question embeds its gold document's filename
token, which guarantees lexical success on that question; the fix is sched-
uled, and because Q16 is the one question on which the fusion stage loses a
gold, the correction can only tighten the reported decomposition. The second
corpus carries a related exposure that we disclose instead of exploiting: the
acquired arXiv documents are named {domain}__{identifier}__{slug},
so content-routing accuracy against the topical prefix is not an independent
measurement of routing quality, and we report routing coverage rather than
routing accuracy (Section 8.3).
Experimental controls. The tool layer isolates the gate's contribution
cleanly: its nine configurations include retrieval without verification and the
same retrieval with the gate in either role, so the ranking-layer claim rests on
a matched control. The agent layer originally did not carry the correspond-
ing control, because its four arms are three grounded lanes (retrieval and
verification) plus a bare agent (neither), which cannot separate the contribu-
tion of retrieval from that of verification. The missing arm, a grounded lane
with the gate disabled and the fusion order returned, was added at the end
of this revision with its protocol fixed before the run (Section 8.6), together

                                               49
with a second ablation that varies the search partition rather than the gate
(Section 8.3). Both inherit the same questions, libraries, engine and turn
budget; neither was tuned after its results were seen.
Evidence provenance is uneven. The evidence groups differ in how they
were produced, and the paper reports the difference rather than smoothing
it. The acquisition, organization and functional campaigns are audited runs
whose per-check outcomes are released. The experience subsystem, by con-
trast, is supported by case evidence: one distillation campaign with thirteen
candidate drafts, a single sedimented experience, and an approval path that
was never exercised. The full 100-document ingestion exists as a derived
state file rather than as a timed, per-document log, and its accounting is
not fully closed: 100 documents were submitted, 91 names appear in the
derived state, and 79 documents are present in the library. Part of the gap
is the 21 reported parse failures, but the remainder is unattributed, so the
parse-failure rate should be read as a bounded estimate rather than as an
exact measurement. We state this because a system paper's most useful
contribution may be the honesty of its ledger.
Judge and engine dependence. Scores and turn counts come from one
judging checkpoint and one agent engine per campaign; lanes, not models,
are the object of comparison, but absolute numbers will move with engine
versions. The deployed gate remains a single checkpoint, although the two
strongest verifier findings are no longer single-signal: a generic entailment
model fails too, but differently (it anti-separates golds and still rejects no out-
of-corpus topic), and a symbolic rule decides the numeric case the shipped
judge mishandles. Trained evidence-verification models and learned signal
ensembles remain open; the naive span-max probe and the rank-fusion en-
semble answer the two easiest upgrades negatively.
Design surface. Three lanes triple the policy surface an adopter must test,
and the reported evidence supports two distinct operating points, with the
catalog-first lane as the exhaustive option and the orchestrated lane as the
latency-tuned interactive default. A two-lane deployment is defensible today,
and query-shape auto-selection is future work.
Consumer-side evidence. The honesty contract is evaluated from the sys-
tem's side, that is, that not-found reports are well formed and non-fabricated,
not from the reader's side. Whether an operator acts on a not-found report,
escalates to a human, or ingests the recommended source is not measured,
and this is the paper's main missing external-validity leg. The natural study
is a small operator trial over a fixed probe set with two conditions, platform

                                               50
reports versus the same model's unaided answer, scored on the downstream
action taken; the probe set and transcripts are released so it can be run
independently.
Nondeterminism. Agent runs are stochastic. We report two full repeti-
tions of the sixteen-cell grid (citation agreement 16/16, with wall time and
turn counts fluctuating substantially), retry only zero-turn transport flakes,
and release all per-run logs including timeout re-runs. Fact-marker checks
are automated substring matches; we additionally report decontaminated
checks computed after removing answer lines that name a gold document,
and the out-of-corpus answers were audited manually. Deterministic layers
were verified to be bit-identical across two rounds except for latency fields.

11. Conclusion

    QDCVR turns "the retriever found it" into "the system verified it". Its
design position is that the governed object of a knowledge-base management
system is content: content decides where a document belongs, content de-
cides whether a candidate may be cited, and the absence of content that
answers the question is itself an outcome the system must report. That posi-
tion is implemented as contracts rather than conventions (RQ1): parse gates
that reject visibly, placement rules that read the document, a three-source
consistency audit over the disk, the tree registry and the library metadata,
lane policies enforced by a server-side tool whitelist, and verdicts that fail
closed.

    The five research questions are answered by measurement rather than
assertion. Acquisition and content routing hold at the scale we tested, while
21% of submitted papers failed to parse and were rejected rather than stored
(RQ2). Staged, multi-channel retrieval with content adjudication reaches
documents that one-shot vector top-k misses, and the benefit comes from
staged recall plus content verification, not from the fusion of the two retrieval
stages (RQ3). Verification costs well under a second and buys per-citation
provenance, but as an ordering it inherits a same-domain blind spot that
we quantify and partially repair; the failure tracks confuser density rather
than the checkpoint, domain, language or scale (RQ4). Honest abstention
lives in the reading and answering layer, never in the threshold, end-to-end
latency belongs to model turns, and engine choice moves cost by two orders
of magnitude at the same quality tier (RQ5).

                                               51
    The roadmap follows from the same ledger. Verifier calibration against
per-library null distributions, trained evidence-verification models, and the
verdict-as-filter role that the tool layer already supports are the immediate
next steps; larger-corpus evaluation with an author-independent question set,
consumer-side evidence that not-found reports serve their readers, and lane
auto-selection from query shape are the open empirical ones. The direction
is towards retrieval systems whose trustworthiness is an inspectable property
of the pipeline rather than a hope about the model.

Figure 1: QDCVR architecture. Documents enter through the acquisition
pipeline (left) and are parsed, quality-gated, split, described and indexed into four
stores. At run time (right), clients and external agent harnesses reach the platform
through the chat endpoint, which attaches a lane policy; the policy is enforced
against the MCP tool layer, whose 95 tools call domain services that operate the
storage engines. All retrieval lanes terminate at the content-verification gate, and a
lane that survives no candidate returns an explicit not-found report. Organization
and life-cycle management (bottom) maintain agreement between the tree registry,
the document files and the library metadata.
