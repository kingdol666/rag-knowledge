\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\noindent\textbf{Corpus and scale.} The results cover bounded deployed
collections and a small arXiv-based corpus. Retrieval-only modes searched live
libraries, whereas lexical and dense baselines used a corpus index; these are
not identical retrieval substrates. Findings should not be generalized to
larger or differently organized collections without replication.

\noindent\textbf{Question construction and denominators.} Questions and gold
annotations were developer-authored. The three-arm grid of
Section~\ref{sec:e5} is ten questions of one question family on one model,
run once per arm: the citation and latency separations hold cell by cell,
but the grid cannot resolve differences smaller than that, and its
grounding reading is at ceiling for all three arms. Two grid answers contain
an evidence-insufficiency qualifier; the grader's
\texttt{insufficient\_evidence} flag is a substring test that also fires when
the phrase appears inside a fully answering text, so we read those two flags
as measurement artifacts, not as hedges. Agent-track retrieval
hits are inferred from trace and answer, while citation checks provide the
more direct functional measure.

\noindent\textbf{Execution and grading.} The refusal battery has one final
observation per arm after rerunning two MCP-mount interruptions. Fabrication
checks were manually audited, and the binary gate required reclassification
of one body-level partial answer. Retrieval-only elapsed time is not directly
comparable to end-to-end agent latency. The E5 grader is deterministic but
lexical: its gold-ID, keyword and citation-marker checks do not establish
semantic claim support, and aliases are reported as a separate regrade. The
BQ06 gold key was corrected to the source paper's thermodynamic reading after
inspection, and declared aliases cover known word-form variants (BQ09's
abbreviation, BQ10's phrasing); the pre-correction question set and its grade
(strict 10/9/9) are retained alongside the corrected regrade (strict 9/9/9). The
MCP lifecycle script passed its 16 direct assertions and the stdio protocol
test passed, but the lifecycle script is not pytest-collection-safe because it
calls \texttt{sys.exit} at module import.

\noindent\textbf{Scope.} Tests ran on a single local workstation and one
verifier checkpoint. They do not establish generalization across model hosts,
large-scale corpora, independently authored questions, operator adoption,
adversarial access or crash-mid-write recovery. The pagination repair was
verified on selected catalog and query cases; broader transport boundary
testing remains necessary.
