\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipelines for heterogeneous, multi-domain
corpora suffer two failure modes that dense similarity alone cannot remove:
documents that are \emph{semantically adjacent but operationally irrelevant}
obtain high cosine scores, and large knowledge bases monopolise the top-$k$ of
a cross-base search. We present \sys{}, a deployed knowledge-base platform in
which retrieval is \emph{content-adjudicated} and \emph{domain-scoped}:
candidates are recalled by a BM25\,$\rightarrow$\,vector cascade, then
re-ranked by reading their actual text against an interpretable $0$--$8$ rubric
under an explicit content-overrides-vector rule; a routing layer selects a
small set of bases before searching; and an \emph{experience lifecycle}
distils operational lessons into credibility-tiered, decaying entries exposed
to LLM agents through 94 Model Context Protocol tools. We evaluate four
retrieval methods through one tool layer on BEIR SciFact (30 official queries),
a 20-query bilingual in-house corpus (13 bases), and a new multi-domain
benchmark that partitions the official supporting documents of 50 HotpotQA
questions into nine topic bases ($38\%$ of queries have gold in two bases).
The result is a bounded claim: adjudication repairs real rank-one errors and
takes the best answer@1 on the multi-domain set, but is significantly
\emph{worse} than its own recall stage on full ranking there ($-0.061$ nDCG@10,
$p{=}0.018$) and does not improve ranking on the clean single-domain corpus ---
at an order of magnitude higher latency, a rank-one repair instrument, not a
ranking improvement. We also report an unstable experience-synthesis result (entries in
2 of 6 state-reset rounds; judged $7$--$9/10$ when produced, vs a
heuristic-extraction mean of $3.5/10$), and a provenance audit that led us to
withhold one internal benchmark whose producing script could not be recovered.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has become the default way to ground large
language models in private knowledge \cite{lewis2020rag,gao2024ragsurvey}.
Almost all deployed pipelines share one architectural assumption: the document
that should be returned is the document whose embedding is closest to the query
embedding. That assumption is convenient and cheap, and it is the source of two
failure modes we observed repeatedly while operating a production knowledge
platform over a heterogeneous, multi-domain corpus.

\noindent\textbf{Similarity is not usefulness.} Two documents can be close in
embedding space while only one of them answers the question, and the failure is
not hypothetical. Query \texttt{sf-002} of the BEIR SciFact benchmark asserts
that \emph{``4-PBA treatment decreases endoplasmic reticulum stress in response
to general endoplasmic reticulum stress markers''}; its gold document is
PubMed id 32587939. Dense retrieval ranks a same-domain neighbour first and
relegates the gold document to rank two (Hit@1 $0$, MRR $0.5$) --- both
documents live in the same ``ER stress'' region of the space, but only one
carries the claim's evidence. This case is mined from the frozen benchmark
artefact, not constructed: it is one of three such queries in the run
(\texttt{sf-002}, \texttt{sf-017}, \texttt{sf-027}), and when the adjudication
stage \emph{reads} the two candidates it inverts the order and returns the gold
document first (Hit@1 $1.0$, MRR $1.0$) --- the concrete operation of
``content overrides vector'' (\S\ref{sec:adjudication}). A vector score is a
joint statement about the query and the document; it is not a statement that
the document \emph{contains} the answer.

\noindent\textbf{Big bases crowd out small ones.} In a multi-base deployment the
largest base dominates the candidate pool, and small but directly relevant bases
are pushed out. To measure the regime rather than assert it, we built a
multi-domain benchmark from a public corpus: 50 HotpotQA questions whose official
supporting documents we partitioned into nine topic knowledge bases
(\S\ref{sec:multidomain}); $38\%$ of those queries have gold documents in two
different bases, so cross-base coverage is a property of the gold standard
itself, not of our test design. Both failure modes are measurable, and both bear
on the two mechanisms this paper evaluates (\S\ref{sec:eval}).

Existing mitigations attack these problems from the wrong end. Cross-encoder
re-rankers improve ordering but are opaque and add a model to the serving path.
Self-reflective methods such as FLARE \cite{jiang2023flare} and Self-RAG
\cite{asai2023selfrag} ask the generator to judge its own retrieval, making the
judgement inseparable from generation, hard to audit, and expensive. Corrective
RAG \cite{yan2024crag} adds a retrieval evaluator, but it is a learned scorer
emitting a scalar, so the operator still cannot see \emph{which} aspect of a
document failed. None makes the retrieval decision itself \emph{legible} to the
person who must explain why a document was returned.

This paper describes \sys{}, a deployed knowledge-base platform built on a
different principle: \emph{recall cheaply, then adjudicate on content}. Candidates
are recalled by a two-stage cascade, and are then \emph{read} and scored against
an explicit three-dimension rubric, with the rule that a content score overrides
a high vector score. Two further decisions complete the design. Queries are
\emph{domain-scoped} --- routed to a bounded set of knowledge bases before any
document is compared --- and operational knowledge is modelled as a first-class
\emph{experience lifecycle} rather than folded into the document store.

\paragraph{What is new here.}
Each ingredient has a precedent, and we are precise about what does not carry a
citation. Domain routing has been studied for query \emph{complexity}
\cite{jeong2024adaptiverag} and for entity \emph{graphs}
\cite{edge2024graphrag,huang2025hirag}; we route at \emph{knowledge-base}
granularity, the axis that matters when a corpus is partitioned by domain.
Content-based verification has precedent in learned re-rankers
\cite{nogueira2019rerank} and evaluator models \cite{yan2024crag}; we make it an
explicit, named-dimension, auditable stage whose verdict can override the recall
order. Experience management is standard in knowledge-management practice; we
add a credibility model in which trust is \emph{earned} through use and decays
with time. The contribution is the combination, the auditability of each
decision, and an honest account of where the combination stops helping.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{C1 (domain scoping).} A knowledge-base routing layer that
  selects a bounded working set before retrieval. On the in-house corpus the
  scoped configuration's only lead is precision (P@5 $0.210$ vs $0.200$); it
  trails dense on Hit@1 ($0.800$ vs $0.900$), Recall@5 ($0.908$ vs $0.917$) and
  MRR ($0.863$ vs $0.931$), at $20\times$ the latency. We
  do not report a false-positive-rate improvement: the benchmark that measured
  one had no traceable producer and is withheld (\S\ref{sec:provenance});
  isolating the routing layer's own contribution is open, not a result.
  \item \textbf{C2 (content adjudication).} A separable pipeline stage with an
  interpretable $0$--$8$ rubric and a \emph{content-overrides-vector} rule,
  with the regime boundary measured. On clean single-domain SciFact it does not
  improve ranking (dense wins Hit@3/Recall@5/nDCG@10; adjudication only Hit@1,
  at an order of magnitude in latency); on multi-domain HotpotQA-in-9-bases it
  is \emph{significantly worse} than its own recall stage on full ranking
  ($-0.061$ nDCG@10, $p{=}0.018$) yet best-in-class on top-rank answerability
  (answer@1 $0.580$ vs $0.480$--$0.520$): a rank-one repair mechanism for
  vocabulary-sharing queries, not a ranking improvement.
  \item \textbf{C3 (experience lifecycle).} An end-to-end lifecycle for
  operational knowledge --- extraction, credibility tiering, temporal decay,
  agent-facing retrieval --- in which no freshly generated entry can present
  itself as authoritative; we report synthesis quality and its run-to-run
  instability honestly, including two baselines under an identical judge.
  \item \textbf{C4 (agent-native surface).} The platform exposed as 94 Model
  Context Protocol tools \cite{anthropic2024mcp}, verified end-to-end: 73 of 73
  externally driven API checks pass against the live deployment.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of the paper positions the work, describes the architecture and
method, presents the experience lifecycle and agent surface, evaluates, and
discusses limitations.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Dense and hybrid retrieval.}
Dense passage retrieval \cite{karpukhin2020dpr} and contrastive retrievers
\cite{izacard2022contriever} optimise the quality of the recalled set. BM25
remains a strong sparse baseline \cite{robertson2009bm25}, and hybrid fusion is
standard practice; BEIR \cite{thakur2021beir} showed that no single retriever
dominates across domains. \sys{} adopts the hybrid cascade as its recall stage
and does not claim it as a contribution: in our measurements it is an
acceleration device (\S\ref{sec:recall}).

\paragraph{Reranking and verification.}
Cross-encoder re-rankers \cite{nogueira2019rerank} and retrieve-rerank-generate
pipelines \cite{glass2022re2g} improve ordering with a learned scorer.
Self-RAG \cite{asai2023selfrag} and FLARE \cite{jiang2023flare} interleave
generation with retrieval critique, and CRAG \cite{yan2024crag} adds a retrieval
evaluator that triggers corrective actions. These approaches judge relevance
with a model whose verdict is a scalar or a token, and the judgement is entangled
with the generator. \sys{} differs in that adjudication is a \emph{separate,
auditable stage} whose output is a small set of named rubric dimensions, and whose
verdict can override the recall ordering by construction.

\paragraph{Knowledge organisation and graph RAG.}
GraphRAG \cite{edge2024graphrag} and HiRAG \cite{huang2025hirag} build entity or
hierarchical knowledge structures to answer global, corpus-level questions.
Adaptive-RAG \cite{jeong2024adaptiverag} routes queries by predicted complexity.
Our routing layer is complementary but operates over \emph{knowledge-base}
granularity rather than query complexity or entity structure: we route to a set
of bases, then retrieve inside them. We are careful not to overstate this: the
general problem of deciding \emph{which collections to search} long predates
embedding-based platforms, and we do not claim novelty over that literature.
What is specific to the present setting is the coupling of base-granularity
routing with per-base content adjudication, and the auditable way the routing
decision is recorded. We also flag the evaluation gap honestly: we report no
routing baselines (always-search-all, catalogue-scoring, or an oracle router)
and no routing accuracy ceiling, so the routing layer's isolated contribution is
an open question rather than a measured result (\S\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\paragraph{Enterprise and agentic knowledge bases.}
AgenticRAG \cite{suresh2026agenticrag} moves grounding burden off a fixed
retrieval stack and onto an agentic harness over enterprise knowledge bases;
AutoKB \cite{sahay2025autokb} automates construction of structured domain
knowledge bases; DeepRead \cite{li2026deepread} exploits document structure to
improve agentic search. All three address the same industrial setting. They differ
in \emph{where the judgement lives}: they improve the agent's behaviour over a
retrieval result, whereas we make the retrieval stage itself adjudicate content and
expose that judgement for audit. An agentic harness on top of an adjudicating
retriever is the natural combination, and we have not evaluated it.

\paragraph{Agent tool-use for knowledge management.}
ReAct \cite{yao2022react} and Toolformer \cite{schick2023toolformer} established
tool-augmented LLM behaviour; the Model Context Protocol \cite{anthropic2024mcp}
standardises the interface. Work such as MCP-Pyserini \cite{ge2026mcppyserini}
exposes IR resources as tools. \sys{} takes the same direction at platform scale:
the knowledge base is not a service the agent calls once, but a tool surface the
agent operates, including ingestion, curation and experience management.

\paragraph{Experience and operational knowledge.}
Knowledge-management systems store documents; they rarely store
\emph{lessons}. Experience-factory and post-mortem literature treats operational
knowledge as a first-class artefact, but we are not aware of a deployed RAG
platform that models experience with explicit credibility tiers and temporal
decay and then exposes it to an agent. \S\ref{sec:experience} describes our
attempt and \S\ref{sec:exp-analysis} reports where it currently falls short.

\section{System Architecture}\label{sec:system}

\sys{} is a deployed, cross-platform knowledge platform. This section describes
how it is built and, more importantly, \emph{why} it is built that way: three
architectural commitments constrain everything in \S\ref{sec:method} and
\S\ref{sec:experience}. Figure~\ref{fig:arch} shows the topology and
Table~\ref{tab:scale} the scale.

\input{figures/fig-architecture.tex}

\subsection{Three services, one capability set}\label{sec:sys-services}
The platform is split so that each service owns a single kind of work.
A \textbf{web layer} (Nuxt, \texttt{:6789}) owns the knowledge-base and document
lifecycle --- creating bases, writing and moving documents, tags, previews ---
and acts as the browser's only network peer, so no client ever talks to the
compute tier directly and no CORS or backend-URL leakage is possible.
A \textbf{compute backend} (FastAPI, \texttt{:8771}) owns retrieval, vector
indexing, graph construction, experience management and persona training.
A third process, \textbf{MinerU}, does OCR and layout analysis for PDFs and runs
on an ephemeral port it picks itself, so a deployment can never silently attach to
a stale engine instance.

The split is not merely organisational. Because the capability set is published
\emph{twice} --- as REST endpoints for the human UI and as 94 Model Context
Protocol tools for agents --- a procedure validated in one surface is directly
executable in the other. We consider this the property that makes the platform
\emph{agent-native} rather than agent-accessible: there is no privileged path that
only a human can take.

\subsection{Five storage layers, one write path}\label{sec:sys-layers}
A knowledge base is materialised simultaneously as (L1) files on disk, (L2) a tree
index, (L3) a per-base YAML catalogue, (L4) a vector collection, and (L5) a
property graph. Each layer exists because a different stage needs a different
access shape: the catalogue answers metadata questions without touching vectors;
the graph answers relational questions the vector index cannot; the tree index
answers structural questions cheaply.

This redundancy is also the platform's main consistency hazard, and the design
answer is a single rule: \emph{every mutation travels one write path} that applies
to all five layers inside one logical transaction, after which the ingestion
module verifies that all five agree. Table~\ref{tab:ingestion} reports the result
--- membership accuracy and storage completeness both $1.000$ across two corpora.
The rule is what makes the rest of the system safe to build on: an agent that
creates a document cannot leave the index and the catalogue disagreeing, so it can
retry a failed step without repairing state.

\subsection{Write--read asymmetry}\label{sec:sys-asymmetry}
Writes go through the service tier so that consistency is enforced in one place.
Reads used by retrieval \emph{bypass the service tier entirely} and read the
on-disk indices directly. The asymmetry is deliberate and has a direct
consequence for the method: it makes the cheap stages of retrieval essentially
free, which is what allows an expensive adjudication stage to exist at all. The
measured cost of that stage is reported in \S\ref{sec:honest}: content
verification raises per-query latency from $0.066$\,s to $1.34$\,s on the
in-house corpus.

\subsection{Hierarchical knowledge bases}\label{sec:sys-hierarchy}
Knowledge bases nest arbitrarily. A parent base acts as a container: its children
carry the documents and are addressable both individually and in aggregate, and
document counts roll up recursively. The hierarchy is the unit the routing layer
operates on (\S\ref{sec:routing}), and it is validated in evaluation: recursive
counting was correct on all three nesting shapes tested (12 children with 73
documents, 1 child with 17, and a flat base with 9).

\subsection{Design principles}\label{sec:sys-principles}
Three commitments recur below. \emph{Legibility over accuracy}: when choosing
between a better opaque decision and a slightly worse explainable one, choose
explainable --- hence named rubric dimensions rather than a score.
\emph{Earned trust}: no artefact is authoritative on creation; credibility
accrues from use and decays with time. \emph{Honest absence}: when evidence is
thin, the system says so rather than presenting a weak result as strong.

\section{\sys{}: Content-Adjudicated Retrieval}\label{sec:method}

\sys{} stands for \emph{Query-Driven Content-Verified Retrieval}. The pipeline
has seven stages (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarised in
Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline}); we describe the stages that carry the method's
claims and treat the rest briefly.

\input{figures/fig-pipeline.tex}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\small
\caption{\sys{} retrieval}\label{alg:pipeline}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require query $q$, candidate bases $\mathcal{K}$, budget $k$
\State $q' \leftarrow \textsc{Rewrite}(q)$ \Comment{Stage 0: intent + rewrite}
\State $\mathcal{K}^{*} \leftarrow \textsc{Route}(q', \mathcal{K}),\; |\mathcal{K}^{*}| \le 3$
       \Comment{Stage 1: domain scoping}
\State $C \leftarrow \textsc{TwoStageRecall}(q', \mathcal{K}^{*})$ \Comment{Stage 2}
\State $C \leftarrow \textsc{Dedup}(C);\; C \leftarrow \{c \in C : s(c) \ge \tau\}$
       \Comment{Stage 3}
\ForAll{$c \in C$}
  \State $r(c) \leftarrow \textsc{Adjudicate}(\textsc{Read}(c))$
         \Comment{Stage 4: 0--8 rubric, temperature 0}
\EndFor
\State $T \leftarrow \textsc{Tier}(C, r)$; \textbf{content overrides vector}
       \Comment{Stage 5}
\State \Return $\textsc{Synthesise}(T)$ with blind-spot report
       \Comment{Stage 6}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Stage 0: Intent and Query Rewriting}\label{sec:intent}
The incoming query is classified into one of five intents (factual, procedural,
troubleshooting, comparative, navigational) and rewritten from colloquial
surface form into a declarative statement plus keyword set. Intent changes the
retrieval bias: troubleshooting queries consult the experience store before the
document store, whereas factual queries go straight to the vector index. We
attach no number to this stage: an earlier draft quoted a rewriting gain from
the withdrawn benchmark family (\S\ref{sec:provenance}), and no re-measurement
exists on the committed harness.

\subsection{Stage 1: Domain Scoping}\label{sec:routing}
The routing layer selects a bounded working set of knowledge bases before any
document is retrieved. Each base carries a name, a description and a document
catalogue; the router scores the rewritten query against those descriptors and
keeps at most three bases:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{K}^{*} = \operatorname*{top\text{-}3}_{K_i \in \mathcal{K}}\;
   \mathrm{Sim}\bigl(q',\, \mathrm{desc}(K_i)\bigr).
\end{equation}
The effect is not merely a smaller search space. Because a query is evaluated
against \emph{domain descriptions} rather than document embeddings alone, a
query whose vocabulary is generic (``heat management'', ``fast charging'') is
pinned to the domain the user actually meant, and the semantic neighbourhoods of
unrelated domains never enter the candidate pool. That is the intended mechanism;
the quantitative claim that it reduces false positives came from the benchmark we
withdrew in \S\ref{sec:provenance}, so we state the mechanism and not a number.
A partial check is available in the traceable data: the in-house run records which
stage produced each query's candidates, and all 20 queries were served from the
scoped stage rather than falling back to an unscoped search.

We close part of this gap with an oracle experiment on the multi-domain
benchmark (\S\ref{sec:multidomain}): restricting retrieval to the single gold
knowledge base (a perfect single-base router) or even taking the \emph{best}
per-gold-base result yields \emph{lower} quality than the deployed
always-search-all mode (two-stage nDCG@10 $0.772$ restricted vs $0.841$ global;
best-restricted $0.795$). At 454 documents, cross-base distraction does not
crowd out gold documents, and single-base restriction is structurally fatal for
the $38\%$ of queries whose gold spans two bases (Recall@2 capped at $0.5$
under any single-base router). So at this scale scoping has no gain to deliver
even under an oracle; its value, if any, must be sought where cross-base
distraction dominates. An early routing-accuracy figure in our records traces
to the withdrawn family and is no longer claimed.

\subsection{Stage 2: Two-Stage Recall}\label{sec:recall}
Recall is a BM25 stage followed by vector refinement and graph-neighbour
expansion. BM25 supplies a sparse candidate set; the vector stage re-ranks inside
that set, so the expensive dense comparison runs over tens rather than thousands
of chunks. On the in-house corpus this cuts the candidate space from $13{,}709$
chunks to a per-base pool while preserving recall.

We deliberately do \emph{not} claim two-stage retrieval as a contribution. It is
an engineering accelerator, and our own standard-benchmark results show it can
\emph{lose} to one-shot dense retrieval on clean corpora
(Table~\ref{tab:scifact}: Hit@3 $0.833$ vs $0.900$). Reporting it as a
contribution would be a mistake; we report it as infrastructure.

\subsection{Stage 3: Deduplication and Thresholding}
Candidates are deduplicated by content fingerprint and filtered by a similarity
threshold $\tau$ (default $0.35$). The threshold is a precision/recall dial, and
it is the component most likely to hurt recall: on SciFact it can drop a gold
document whose similarity sits just below $\tau$. We analyse this failure
explicitly in \S\ref{sec:honest}.

\subsection{Stage 4: Content Adjudication}\label{sec:adjudication}
This is the stage that distinguishes \sys{} from a re-ranking pipeline.
Each surviving candidate is \emph{read} --- the top of its text is loaded through
the same document tool an agent would use --- and scored at temperature $0$ on
three named dimensions totalling $0$--$8$: \emph{topic relevance} ($0$--$3$;
$3$ = directly about the query subject), \emph{scenario match} ($0$--$3$;
$3$ = addresses the query's actual problem), and \emph{answer evidence}
($0$--$2$; $2$ = carries citable data, steps or conclusions). Scores come from
the document text, not from its embedding.

The decision rule makes the adjudication authoritative:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{decision}(d) =
\begin{cases}
\text{accept }(P_0) & c(d) \ge 6\\
\text{supplement }(P_1) & c(d) = 5\\
\text{discard} & c(d) \le 4
\end{cases}
\label{eq:override}
\end{equation}
independently of the vector similarity $s(d)$,
where $c(d)$ is the content score and $s(d)$ the vector similarity. A document
with $s(d)=0.95$ and $c(d)=3$ is discarded. This is the operational content of
``vectors are fast, content is accurate''.

Three properties follow. The verdict is \emph{interpretable} (which dimension
failed), \emph{auditable} (the read text and scores are recorded, so a result
can be justified after the fact), and \emph{separable} (adjudication can be
disabled --- exactly what the ablation does). The harness implements the
production procedure through re-ranking (recall, thresholding, adjudication via
the same document tool an agent uses; three verification reads per query, depth
ablated). Tag expansion and answer synthesis are not exercised, so our claims
concern the retrieval core. The cost is latency: the
adjudicator reads on average $3{,}939$ characters per query in-house and
$4{,}266$ on SciFact, which \S\ref{sec:honest} treats as a first-class result.

\subsection{Stage 5--6: Tiering and Blind-Spot Declaration}\label{sec:blindspot}
Surviving documents are tiered $P_0/P_1/P_2$; short chunks (under $50$
characters) are demoted regardless of score, because such chunks receive
inflated similarity from embedding artefacts. When the confirmed set draws on
fewer than two knowledge bases, the system states that coverage is limited
instead of presenting a thin result as complete. This ``rather say nothing than
say it wrong'' behaviour is a design commitment we consider part of the method,
not a UI detail: a retrieval system that cannot express absence forces its user
to treat every result as equally confident, which is precisely the failure the
adjudication stage exists to remove.

Two error directions are measurable. First, the rule is \emph{advisory by
construction}: it appends a coverage disclaimer and never removes a result, so
withholding-a-legitimate-result cannot occur in the measured pipeline (every
benchmark query returned results for every method). Second, the
\emph{missed-declaration} direction is measurable on the multi-domain benchmark
(\S\ref{sec:multidomain}), where $38\%$ of queries need cross-base coverage:
taking ``the top-$5$ represents fewer gold bases than the gold set spans'' as
``should carry the disclaimer'', the missed rate is $26.3\%$ for two-stage,
dense and adjudicated, $63.2\%$ for BM25 --- the sparse path most needs the
declaration, as the rule's motivation predicts. The false-alarm direction
(declaring when coverage is complete) is decided by the answer-synthesis stage,
which the harness bypasses; we state that gap rather than infer a number.

\section{Experience Lifecycle}\label{sec:experience}

Documents record what was known; operational knowledge records what \emph{worked}.
The second is what agents need when a task fails, and it is what most knowledge
platforms do not model. \sys{} treats experience as a first-class artefact with
its own lifecycle, storage, credibility model and retrieval path.

\subsection{Lifecycle}\label{sec:lifecycle}
Experiences move through eleven stages: \emph{signal capture}, \emph{candidate
extraction}, \emph{draft synthesis}, \emph{review/approval},
\emph{publication}, \emph{vector indexing}, \emph{retrieval}, \emph{application
feedback}, \emph{review scoring}, \emph{credibility decay}, and \emph{health
monitoring}. Extraction runs in a deterministic heuristic mode or, via a
registry of 15 agent engines, an LLM --- falling back silently to heuristics
when none is usable.

\subsection{Credibility Tiers}\label{sec:tiers}
A published experience is not equally trustworthy for every purpose, so
experience is tiered by combined evidence ($\mathrm{vec}$ = vector similarity,
$c$ = content score):
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Tier}(e)=
\begin{cases}
P_0 & \mathrm{vec}\!\ge\!.65 \;\wedge\; c\!\ge\!6 \;\wedge\; \mathrm{rating}\!\ge\!4 \;\wedge\; \mathrm{rev.}\!\ge\!1\\
P_1 & \mathrm{vec}\!\ge\!.45 \;\wedge\; c\!\ge\!4\\
P_2 & \mathrm{vec}\!\ge\!.35 \;\wedge\; c\!\ge\!3\\
\mathrm{discard} & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with modifiers that \emph{downgrade} rather than upgrade: an entry disputed by
three or more reviews with mean rating below $2.0$ is capped at $P_2$, and an
entry with no reviews and no recorded applications is capped at $P_1$ no matter
how high its similarity. Automatically extracted entries start unvetted, which
caps them at $P_1$ until independent evidence arrives. The design intent is that
trust must be earned through use; a freshly generated experience can never
present itself as authoritative.

\subsection{Temporal Decay}
Operational knowledge expires. Entries that are unverified and unapplied for
more than 30 days are demoted, and a scheduled decay pass re-evaluates the store.
Decay is deliberately conservative --- it demotes, it never deletes --- so that a
stale-but-historically-useful entry remains retrievable at a lower tier.

We evaluate the rule as a sensitivity analysis and state its observational
limits. The deployment is younger than the window: every measured entry carries
a same-day timestamp, so a longitudinal validation of the 30-day threshold is
not yet possible and is not presented. Sweeping the demotion window over
$7/14/30/90$ days, the demoted set is empty at every window --- no entry is
older than seven days, so no rule of this family fires, correctly --- and the
rule's precision against a ``genuinely stale'' ground truth is undefined within
the observation window. Because decay demotes but never deletes, a
wrongly-tuned threshold degrades retrieval order gracefully rather than
destroying entries: the failure mode we can afford while the threshold remains
unvalidated.

\subsection{Multi-Path Retrieval}
Experience is recalled along five parallel paths --- vector similarity, keyword
match, scenario match, tag match, and quality-feedback match --- which are fused
and deduplicated. The rationale is that operational queries often name a
\emph{symptom} rather than a topic: a user searching for ``why did the run
fail'' shares no vocabulary with an entry titled ``pulse self-heating window
violation'', but shares its scenario and tags.

We evaluate the fusion on a purpose-built set: eight operational experiences
seeded through the platform's own write path (scenario, tags, problem,
solution, key lessons) and twelve frozen queries --- half \emph{symptom-style},
sharing no vocabulary with the target entry's title (``parsing job hangs
forever and the file never appears'' $\to$ ``PDF parse stuck\ldots''), half
\emph{vocabulary-style}, sharing solution words. Because the deployed endpoint
exposes no per-path switches, the fusion is re-implemented at harness level
from the service's published scoring rule (keyword/scenario/tag matches scored
by $\min(0.42 + 0.06\,\mathrm{hits} + 0.15\,\mathrm{cov},\,0.72)$ over their
fields; per-query vector scores from the experience vector index; quality as a
tier/rating bonus); a top-3 agreement check against the live endpoint matches
$1$ of $6$ spot checks, so the numbers characterise the fusion design, not the
shipped endpoint's end-to-end behaviour.

\emph{Results.} The full five-path fusion is perfect on the twelve queries
(Hit@1 $=$ MRR $= 1.000$), and it is the only perfect configuration.
Leave-one-out: removing quality-feedback costs most (Hit@1 $0.833$), removing
keyword or scenario costs $0.083$ each, removing vector or tag none. Single
paths are all imperfect and fail on \emph{disjoint} queries --- vector-only
$0.833$ fails symptom-style queries with no lexical overlap, keyword-only
$0.917$ fails paraphrases, tag-only $0.417$, scenario-only $0.750$,
quality-only $0.167$ --- which is the complementarity the five-path design
assumes, and quality signals are what break ties among plausible entries.

\section{Agent-Native Interface}\label{sec:agent}

The platform is designed to be operated by an agent rather than browsed by a
human. Concretely, it exposes \textbf{94 Model Context Protocol tools} covering
knowledge-base CRUD, document ingestion, parsing, tagging, search, vector
indexing, graph construction, experience management and persona training, plus 14
agent skills that describe \emph{procedures} over those tools (ingest a document,
audit a base, distil an experience, search across bases).

Two design choices make the surface usable rather than merely large. First,
write operations are atomic and singular: creating a document does not silently
also index it or tag it, so an agent can retry a failed step without creating
inconsistent state. Second, the tool layer is the \emph{same} layer the human UI
uses, so a procedure validated in the UI is directly executable by an agent and
vice versa. We report the end-to-end validation of this surface in
\S\ref{sec:e2e}.

\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Research Questions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1} Does domain-scoped routing reduce cross-domain false positives?
  \item \textbf{RQ2} Does content adjudication change ranking, and where does it pay?
  \item \textbf{RQ3} Do the individual components contribute, and how much?
  \item \textbf{RQ4} Does the pipeline degrade on public, non-self-authored benchmarks?
  \item \textbf{RQ5} What quality does automatic experience synthesis actually reach?
  \item \textbf{RQ6} Is the agent-facing surface correct end-to-end?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Setup}
\paragraph{Corpora.} Three corpora are used, each pinned to one dated snapshot of
the deployment (Table~\ref{tab:scale} reports the two snapshots separately; they
are \emph{not} interchangeable and no count is mixed across them).
\emph{In-house, snapshot 2026-07-29}: 13 knowledge bases across eight domains
(polymer materials, energy and batteries, biomedicine, catalysis, AI/ML, embodied
AI, economics, food science), 154 documents and 13{,}709 chunks, embedded with
BGE-M3 (1024-d) \cite{chen2024bgem3}, indexed in ChromaDB with a jieba BM25
keyword index. A later ingestion run (snapshot 2026-09-13) covered 184 documents
but did not re-measure chunk or graph counts, so the two are never combined.
\emph{Public retrieval}: the BEIR SciFact collection \cite{thakur2021beir} ---
148 articles ingested through the production pipeline, evaluated on 30 official
queries against the official relevance judgements --- plus a 16-question SQuAD
v1.1 set. \emph{Multi-domain public}: 50 HotpotQA dev questions
\cite{yang2018hotpotqa} whose official supporting and distractor documents are
partitioned into nine topic knowledge bases by a deterministic keyword rule
(454 documents; $38\%$ of queries have gold in two bases); this benchmark
replaces a withdrawn internal 50-query set (\S\ref{sec:provenance}) and is used
for the multi-domain comparison.

\paragraph{Methods.} All methods read through the same tool layer and the same
index; only the ranking stage differs. \emph{BM25} is the sparse stage-1 ranking,
\emph{Two-stage} adds vector refinement and thresholding without adjudication,
\emph{Dense} is one-shot vector retrieval, and \emph{\sys{}} adds content
adjudication and (where noted) domain scoping. \emph{Hybrid-RRF} is reciprocal
rank fusion of the sparse and dense lists.

\paragraph{Protocol.} Deterministic channels are free of randomness; repeats
are bit-identical apart from latency. LLM-driven channels run at temperature
$0$ and are reported as means with a $\pm1.5$ run-to-run tolerance on judge
means.

\paragraph{Implementation and hyper-parameters.} All configurations share one
index and one tool layer. Embeddings are BGE-M3 (1024-d, L2-normalised)
\cite{chen2024bgem3}; chunks are $500$ characters with $50$ overlap; the vector
store is ChromaDB (persistent, default HNSW); the keyword index is jieba BM25
over a $12{,}000$-character window per document. Retrieval defaults are vector
top-$k=10$, two-stage stage-1 $k=40$ and stage-2 $k=10$, similarity threshold
$\tau=0.35$, and three content-verification reads per candidate. Fusion weights in
the two-stage cascade are $0.5$ keyword and $0.5$ graph-neighbour, with
neighbour depth $1$ and a minimum of three candidates before the stage-2 filter
applies. The platform default for stage-1/2 is $20/5$; the benchmark runs use
$40/10$ and the difference is recorded in the frozen snapshot manifest.
Library and model revisions: BGE-M3 loaded locally from HuggingFace snapshot
\texttt{5617a9f61b028005a4858fdac845db406aefb181} via sentence-transformers
$5.6.1$ (transformers $4.51.3$, torch $2.10.0$); ChromaDB $1.5.9$ persistent
HNSW with the library-default parameters $M{=}16$, ef\_construction${=}100$,
ef\_search${=}100$ and only \texttt{hnsw:space}${=}$\texttt{cosine} set
explicitly; jieba $0.42.1$ for tokenisation, with the BM25 stage using
$k_1{=}1.5$, $b{=}0.75$ (Robertson--Spark-Jones weighting as implemented in the
keyword index service).

\paragraph{Statistics.} The protocol is committed and executed
(\texttt{31\_stats.py}); every comparison below names its numbers. For the
deterministic retrieval channels, repeats are bit-identical apart from latency,
so we report double-runs within a session (bit-identical, r1${}={}$r2) and
cross-reset runs for session-level variance rather than five nominal repeats of
an exactly reproducible pipeline. For pairwise comparisons we use the
\emph{Wilcoxon signed-rank test} as the primary statistic --- distribution-free,
appropriate at $n{=}30$--$50$ where normality cannot be assumed --- report the
Shapiro--Wilk test on the paired differences to justify that choice, give a
\emph{per-comparison} $95\%$ confidence interval from paired bootstrap
resampling ($B{=}2000$, seed $0$, percentile method), and apply
\emph{Holm--Bonferroni} correction across the full family of comparisons. On
the multi-domain benchmark the corrected results are significant: the
adjudicated configuration beats BM25 by $+0.241$ nDCG@10
($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}{=}2\times10^{-5}$) and is \emph{beaten} by its own two-stage
recall stage ($-0.061$, $p{=}0.018$) and by dense ($-0.088$, $p{=}0.018$). On
SciFact ($n{=}30$) no component-level difference survives correction (all
$|\Delta| \le 0.023$), and we report those as descriptive contributions with
intervals rather than claims. The LLM channel (experience synthesis) is
inherently variable; we report six state-reset runs and analyse the variance as
a finding (\S\ref{sec:exp-analysis}).

\subsection{RQ1--RQ2: Main Results (traceable evidence only)}\label{sec:main}
We report only results whose producing script is committed and whose artefact is
frozen (\S\ref{sec:provenance}). Two benchmarks meet that bar: the public BEIR
SciFact evaluation (Table~\ref{tab:scifact}) and the in-house bilingual corpus
(Table~\ref{tab:inhouse}).

On the \textbf{public benchmark} (30 official queries, official qrels, four
methods through one tool layer) the result is mixed and we report it as such:
dense retrieval has the best Hit@3 ($0.900$ vs $0.833$), Recall@5 ($0.900$ vs
$0.833$) and nDCG@10 ($0.834$ vs $0.809$); BM25 has the best MRR ($0.839$); the
content-verified configuration wins only Hit@1 ($0.767$ vs $0.733$). The
two-stage hybrid, which is that configuration's own recall stage, is the weakest
method on the same queries.

On the \textbf{in-house corpus} (20 queries over 13 knowledge bases) the
content-verified configuration's only lead is P@5 ($0.210$ vs $0.200$); it
trails dense on Hit@1 ($0.800$ vs $0.900$), Recall@5 ($0.908$ vs $0.917$),
Hit@5 (tied at $0.950$) and MRR ($0.863$ vs $0.931$), at $1.34$\,s against
$0.066$\,s. The effect is language-dependent: on
Chinese queries it matches dense at Hit@1 $1.000$, whereas on English it is behind
($0.700$ vs $0.800$).

\paragraph{What these two results jointly support.}
Content verification \emph{re-orders the top of the list} rather than improving
ranking overall: it raises Hit@1 on the public benchmark and P@5 in-house,
while losing Hit@1/Recall@5/MRR in-house and nDCG on the public set.
The mechanism that would explain the inconsistency --- that adjudication helps
in proportion to cross-domain distractors --- is untested on these two
effectively single-domain benchmarks; \S\ref{sec:multidomain} tests it on a
multi-domain public benchmark and reports a mixed answer.

\subsection{Withheld results and the provenance audit}\label{sec:provenance}
An earlier draft led with a 50-query internal benchmark (false-positive rate
falling from $0.635$ to $0.348$, best P@1 $0.457$, significant advantage over a
hybrid baseline). \textbf{We have removed it.} A provenance audit found that its
artefacts --- \texttt{cikm\_summary.json}, \texttt{figure1\_comparison.json},
\texttt{table1\_main.tex}, \texttt{table3\_ablation.tex}, \texttt{results/v4/}
--- have \emph{no producing script anywhere in the repository} (an exhaustive
search over 417 code files found no writer); the only runner present is a
standalone BM25 script over $\sim$30 synthetic abstracts with no vector index,
no adjudication and no LLM. Four audit facts compound this: the family's
ablation ran on \textbf{eight} queries, not the fifty its caption implied; its
significance was a paired $t$-test on Recall@5 whose four $t$-statistics were
\emph{negative}, a deficit presented as an advantage; the false-positive rate
was defined over returned documents only, so abstention scored zero by
construction; and one statistic was a hard-coded literal mislabelling a mean as
a median.

The corrective is procedural, and is the paper's most transferable lesson:
\textbf{every table must name its producing script, and the script must write
the artefact by a committed path.} \texttt{provenance\_audit.py} now fails if
any cited artefact loses its producer, and the frozen snapshot records a
SHA-256 per file. The withheld benchmark stays withdrawn permanently; in its
place we built a new multi-domain public benchmark with committed producers and
an official gold standard (\S\ref{sec:multidomain}).

\subsection{RQ4: Public Benchmark and the Honest Reading}\label{sec:honest}
SciFact is the evaluation least favourable to us and therefore the most
informative (Table~\ref{tab:scifact}). We state the conclusion plainly:
\textbf{content adjudication does not improve
ranking on a clean, single-domain benchmark}. SciFact is a homogeneous scientific
claim-verification corpus in which the gold document usually names the query
entity, so vector similarity is already a good proxy for usefulness and there are
few cross-domain distractors for adjudication to remove. The threshold in
Stage~3 can additionally drop a gold document whose similarity falls just below
$\tau$, which is where part of the Hit@3 gap comes from.

The distractor hypothesis --- that adjudication helps in proportion to the
number of cross-domain distractors present --- was the paper's central
explanatory claim, and the false-positive figures that originally supported it
came from the withdrawn artefact family (\S\ref{sec:provenance}). We have now
tested it on a multi-domain public benchmark built for the purpose
(\S\ref{sec:multidomain}), and the answer is mixed rather than favourable: on
vocabulary-sharing queries adjudication still repairs rank-1 errors (the
\texttt{sf-002} pattern, and the highest answer@1 of all methods on the
multi-domain set); on multi-hop questions with almost no lexical overlap, the
same word-matching verification \emph{mis}-ranks, and the adjudicated
configuration is significantly worse than its own recall stage ($-0.061$ nDCG@10,
$p{=}0.018$). Verification promotes documents whose \emph{text} matches the
question's terms; when neither candidate's text matches, the heuristic has no
signal and its re-ordering is noise. This is not recoverable by a query-level
trigger: splitting the fifty queries at the median query-to-gold-title overlap,
adjudication is worse in \emph{both} strata (low $n{=}27$: Hit@1 $-0.185$; high
$n{=}23$: $-0.217$). The cost side is unchanged and measured: adjudication read
on average $4{,}266$ characters per query on SciFact with a low verification
pass rate, i.e.\ it spent latency re-reading documents dense retrieval had
already ranked well. Per-method coverage on the multi-domain set is reported as
gold recall (Recall@2/Recall@5, Table~\ref{tab:hotpot}) together with the
blind-spot analysis of \S\ref{sec:blindspot}, replacing a false-positive rate
that abstention could game.

\subsection{RQ5: Experience Synthesis}\label{sec:exp-analysis}
The lifecycle (\S\ref{sec:experience}) executes reliably: meditation runs
succeed, tiers and the decay pass behave as specified, multi-path retrieval
returns results. The \emph{quality} of automatically synthesised experience,
however, is poor --- a negative result we report with its baselines and its
run-to-run instability.

Table~\ref{tab:experience} shows the measured outcome: 10 experiences were
produced for one base and none for the other (KB coverage $0.50$), the mean judge
score is $\mathbf{3.5/10}$, and only $37.5\%$ of entries were judged grounded in
their source documents. The judge's justifications identify the failure
precisely: of eight sampled entries, the two that scored well ($5.0$, $8.0$)
were structurally sound but carried ``no content body, parameters, or lessons'';
the remaining six scored $1.0$--$2.0$ (``empty shell'', ``no verifiable facts
--- only a title, a bare domain token as scenario, and a category'').

The architectural cause is identifiable: the synthesis stage produces
\emph{well-structured metadata with empty payloads}. The lifecycle treats these
as unvetted and caps them at $P_1$, so the failure degrades safely rather than
injecting noise into the authoritative tier --- the behaviour the credibility
model was designed for. But a lifecycle that quarantines low-quality output is
not a system that produces good experience, and we do not claim the latter.

\input{generated/tables/tab-experience.tex}

\paragraph{Multi-run stability under state reset.}
An earlier freeze reported a different outcome on re-execution (ten judged
entries, then zero); an audit hypothesised state contamination. We made the
reset part of the benchmark --- every round first deletes all published
experiences and rejects all drafts, verifying zero counts --- and re-ran six
rounds in two three-round samples. Instability \emph{persists under reset},
refuting contamination: the English base produced $0, 3, 0, 3, 0, 0$ entries
across the six rounds, the Chinese base zero each time. Two facts sit inside
the variance. When the agent \emph{does} produce, entries are good --- one
round's three entries scored $9.0$, $8.0$, $7.0$ under the same strict judge
that gave the heuristic table above $3.5$ --- and the Chinese base's zero is
the quality gate correctly refusing encyclopedic content. The variance is the
generative agent's per-run produce/skip judgement, not platform state; the
credibility model contains the consequence either way (unvetted entries are
capped at $P_1$). We report the instability as a finding rather than selecting
a favourable round.

\paragraph{Baseline comparison for experience synthesis.}
We compare three materials under an \emph{identical} judge prompt
(grounded 4 / structure 3 / reusable 3; same judge model, eight frozen
operational queries): (i) \emph{no synthesis} --- the raw top-$3$ documents
the two-stage tool retrieves for the query; (ii) \emph{one-shot LLM summary}
--- the seven source documents summarised into ``lessons'' by a single prompt,
with no scenario/tags/lifecycle metadata; (iii) \emph{ours} --- the top-$3$
pipeline-created experiences retrieved from the experience store. Pipeline
experiences score \textbf{6.00}/10 on average, ahead of one-shot summaries at
\textbf{3.75} and raw documents at \textbf{3.62}: distillation into
structured, scenario-tagged entries is more reusable than either baseline,
and the ordering is stable under a re-worded judge prompt
($5.25$ / $4.00$ / $2.75$). One incident reported for completeness: in a
first sample the summariser received empty inputs from a harness path bug and
\emph{refused to invent lessons} --- the honest-refusal behaviour the platform
designs for, in an unexpected place. \emph{Judge reliability:} the same 24
judge calls scored twice (original and independently worded prompt, one rubric)
agree moderately --- exact $25\%$, within one point $62.5\%$, mean
$|\Delta|{=}1.13$, $r{=}0.716$, $\kappa{=}0.40$ on the binarised ``usable''
decision --- so effects above $\sim$1 point survive (our gaps are $2.3$--$2.4$)
while sub-point differences do not; this is an LLM--LLM agreement, not a human
study. Producer: \texttt{40\_experience\_suite.py}, \texttt{42\_e4\_fix.py},
\texttt{66\_judge\_agreement.py}; transcripts archived.

\subsection{RQ6: End-to-End Verification of the Agent Surface}\label{sec:e2e}
We exercised the externally reachable surface with an independently written
client that never imports application code: 73 checks across nine groups
(authentication; knowledge-base and document lifecycle; content-based retrieval;
graph construction and query; the full experience lifecycle including drafts,
decay and extraction; persona training and evaluation; the engine registry;
cleanup). \textbf{All 73/73 pass} against the live deployment. Two findings are
worth recording. First, an earlier run showed four failures that were harness
artefacts --- the supervising process restarted the backend mid-run --- a
reminder that end-to-end claims are only as good as the harness asserting them.
Second, the exercise surfaced two genuine contract defects that we fixed: read
endpoints accepted only one of two documented parameter spellings, and two
engine-registry entries declared environment-variable requirements that the
engines' own documentation contradicts, which had made two working engines
unselectable.

\subsection{Reproducibility of the reported numbers}
Every number in this paper is generated from frozen artefacts rather than
transcribed. The exact bytes used are archived with a manifest of SHA-256
digests, and the table/figure generator verifies each digest on every run, so a
reported number cannot silently change. Three further safeguards were added
after an audit found results being overwritten in place. First, every benchmark
run now writes to an immutable, timestamped directory
(\texttt{results/run-<UTC>}); nothing is ever overwritten. Second, every result
file embeds its own identity: the git commit of the producing tree, a hash of
the retrieval configuration, an explicit random seed (zero --- the retrieval
pipeline is deterministic), and the run id. Third, every table and figure in
this paper names its producing script in the caption, and
\texttt{provenance\_audit.py} fails the build if any cited artefact loses its
producer. The artefact packages the committed scripts, the query sets and
relevance judgements, per-query per-method scores, the judge prompts and
transcripts, and the digest manifest.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:conclusion}

\paragraph{What we would claim, and what we would not.}
The defensible claim is narrower than when we began, and the narrowing is the
paper's most honest contribution. Three statements rest on reproducible
evidence: ingestion is lossless (membership and completeness $1.000$ across two
corpora and five storage layers); content adjudication \emph{re-orders} the top
of a list rather than improving ranking overall; and the agent surface is
correct end-to-end (73/73 checks). The claim we \emph{refine} rather than make
is the motivating one --- that scoping plus adjudication substantially reduces
false positives in heterogeneous corpora. The original evidence was withdrawn
(\S\ref{sec:provenance}); the replacement multi-domain benchmark bounds the
adjudication half (full-ranking benefit absent where queries and evidence do
not share vocabulary, top-rank repair real where they do,
\S\ref{sec:multidomain}); the scoping half remains architecturally motivated
but unmeasured; the cost side is measured throughout.

\paragraph{A provenance failure, reported as a finding.}
Our own internal benchmark --- the one we had led with --- could not be traced
to any producing script in a 417-file search, ran its component study on eight
queries while its caption implied fifty, and reported a paired test whose four
$t$-statistics were all negative under a column that read as an advantage
(\S\ref{sec:provenance}). We now run \texttt{provenance\_audit.py} as a gate,
and recommend it to anyone reporting system measurements: \emph{if you cannot
name the script that wrote a table, you do not have the table.}

\paragraph{Latency.}
Content adjudication costs about $20\times$ in latency on the in-house corpus
($1.34$\,s vs $0.066$\,s). It is viable because reads bypass the service tier
and run locally, and because adjudication applies only to a small candidate
set; it would not be viable in a latency-critical single-domain setting, where
on the evidence of \S\ref{sec:honest} we do not recommend it.

\paragraph{Evaluation scale.}
The public evaluations are moderate: 30 SciFact queries, 50 multi-domain
HotpotQA queries, 16 SQuAD questions (at ceiling, reported for completeness).
The HotpotQA partition shows the approach scales to a multi-base public corpus;
a full-BEIR evaluation is the obvious next step, and we do not claim it here. A 32-question XQuAD subset was also collected through
committed scripts, and we report it rather than omitting it: both methods
saturate ranking (Hit@5 $1.000$, MRR $1.000$) and the only separating metric is
Precision@5, where the adjudicated configuration scores $0.275$ against dense
$0.831$ --- a top-$5$ \emph{composition} effect, not a retrieval failure: the
adjudicated pipeline returns fewer redundant parts of the same document, so at
ceiling it looks worse on a metric that counts repeated relevant parts as
precision. We flag the adverse headline number explicitly rather than hide it.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
\emph{Construct}: the $0$--$8$ rubric and the experience judge are LLM
judgements; we mitigate with temperature $0$, frozen prompts, and a measured
dual-judge agreement study (\S\ref{sec:exp-analysis}: $\kappa{=}0.40$, mean
$|\Delta|{=}1.13$), which is a proxy for --- not a substitute for --- human
inter-annotator agreement. \emph{Internal}: the component study uses the same
thirty SciFact queries as Table~\ref{tab:scifact}. \emph{External}: the
in-house corpus is domain-specific and self-authored; the HotpotQA partition
(\S\ref{sec:multidomain}) removes the single-domain restriction and uses an
official gold standard, but the partition itself is author-chosen.
\emph{Author-evaluation bias}: every benchmark here was designed, run and
scored by the system's authors on a deployment they operate. We mitigate with
public gold standards and unfavourable results; independent evaluation remains
the only complete remedy.

\paragraph{Future work.}
The experience-synthesis instability is generative, not architectural: when the
agent chose to distil, its entries scored $7$--$9/10$ under the strict judge, so
the repair path is to stabilise the produce/skip decision itself (e.g.\ a fixed
decision rubric over corpus type) while keeping the payload-quality gate that
contains bad output. The Stage-3 threshold should be learned per corpus and the
stage-2 pool deepened: the ablation shows the pool size, not the threshold
value, loses gold documents on SciFact. Adjudication should condition its
intervention on query--evidence lexical overlap, which our two public
benchmarks bracket. The oracle experiment shows scoping has no gain to deliver
at 454-document scale, so its value must be tested at corpus sizes where
cross-base distraction dominates; and a human inter-annotator agreement study
for the judge rubric remains the missing validation.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
We described \sys{}, a deployed agent-native knowledge platform, and evaluated
its two central retrieval mechanisms on public benchmarks (BEIR SciFact with
official qrels, and a new multi-domain benchmark partitioning HotpotQA's
official supporting documents into nine topic knowledge bases), a bilingual
in-house corpus, and a state-resetting six-round experience protocol. Content
adjudication is a rank-one repair instrument for queries that share vocabulary
with their evidence --- it fixes real top-rank errors on SciFact and achieves
the best answer@1 on the multi-domain benchmark --- while significantly hurting
full ranking on multi-hop questions with little lexical overlap; ingestion is
lossless; the lifecycle contains unstable generation safely but does not make
it reliable; and scoping shows no gain even under an oracle at this scale.
Negative results of this kind tell the next system where the mechanism stops
working.
